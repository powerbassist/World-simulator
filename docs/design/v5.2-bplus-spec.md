# V5.2 b+ — votes, feelings, dismissals, the agenda, ownership by law

Gate b scored 2/5 (`.superpowers/sdd/v5.2-gateb-report.md`). Q1 now holds: "oui
plein de personnes". Q2 does not: "refusé m'a rien couté en apparence". The
author pulled four V5.3 items into V5.2. This spec covers the five items that
need a design before Systems and Interface build them (the V5.2 brief, "### b+").
Each item gives the player's view, the rule, the numbers, and a band wherever a
number moves.

Labels: **MEASURED** (a cited line, a probe or a table), **INFERRED** (read from
the code or derived, not run), **OPINION** (the designer's call). Source lines
are pinned to origin/main `c4408081`, the tree the code map read.

## R2 — votes made visible

**The author:** "laws passed or rejected instantly"; votes were not seen as
proposed, accepted or rejected.

**Today (MEASURED):**

- A player's bill divides and applies in the same call: `Legislature.propose()`
  (`src/sim/legislature.gd:1281`).
- The result overwrites `ns.last_law_result` (`nation_state.gd:857`).
- One note follows (`src/ui/main.gd:417`).
- The counts exist: `resolve_vote()` returns yes, no and abstain seats, the
  vote of each party and the rebels (`legislature.gd:618`).

**The rule:**

- **Tabled now, divided at the month's close.** Proposing a bill tables it:
  - it carries a vote day, the last day of the current month;
  - it divides as the FIRST step of the next close (the one that ends the
    current month), before any other
    step. A bill tabled mid-month therefore applies before that close's
    Economy, Opinion and Elections, as it does today. A bill tabled from
    inside a close (the harness's step hook, see below) lands one close later;
  - only one player bill may be tabled at a time. A Diary-booked law that comes
    due while another bill is tabled is refused, with the reason named ("a bill
    is already before the Assembly"), and its Diary entry reads refused;
  - `propose()`'s contract changes. It returns the tabling: `tabled`, the vote
    day and the forecast. The division's result arrives at the division, in
    the month's report and in `ns.last_law_result` as today. The callers that
    read the result at once must read it at the division instead:
    - `src/ui/diary_driver.gd:66-67` (`ok = result["passed"]`): a booked law
      now reads "tabled" on its day. Its Diary entry is resolved, done or
      refused, when the division reports, so it never reads as failed in
      between;
    - `tools/balance_probe.gd:1079`, `:1296`, `:1560`, `:1578` and `:1975`:
      these print the result and the seat counts, and now print them at the
      division step;
  - Article 49.3 stays immediate, because it is adoption without a vote;
  - the referendum route and the second chamber's hold keep their own
    calendars.
- **One card per bill, never holding.** The tabled note ("TABLED: <law>. The
  Assembly votes on <date>. Forecast: …") is updated in place into the result
  ("CARRIED / DEFEATED: <law>, 312 for, 259 against, 6 abstaining; rebels:
  <party> 7"), with `inbox.set_body` on the same card id. The result uses
  `resolve_vote()`'s counts. Notes never hold the clock (the 4 + 6 budget,
  [[decision-surfaces]]).
- **The Diary shows it:** the vote day is a fixed entry on the agenda (R8), and
  the result stays on that day.
- **The hemicycle (R6, Interface's)** draws the result: one dot per seat,
  coloured by how it voted.

**Bands:**

|---|---|
| Player bills that produce exactly one inbox card (tabled, then updated in place) | **100 %** |
| That card holding the clock | **0 %** |
| The counts on the card equal `resolve_vote()`'s yes, no and abstain seats | **100 %** |
| Re-baseline: the balance table | **moves, predicted** (INFERRED). The regimes propose INSIDE the tick, in the step hook before Elections (`tools/balance_probe.gd:950-953`), so under R2 their bills land one close later, and the proposing month's Elections step runs without the law. Every law-proposing regime may move. The harness keeps the game's rule and does not shortcut the division: a probe that skips a machine measures a world the game never runs (AGENTS.md). The moves are reported row by row, and the author accepts them (I4). The regimes that propose no law stay ZERO |

## R11 + Q2 — relations as feelings, and the cost of a refusal shown

**The author:** relations are feelings, not "grudge +0.17"; refusing "cost me
nothing, apparently".

**Today (MEASURED):**

- The price exists: a decline costs rapport −0.10 and a grudge of 0.18, and
  twice the grudge for a principled person.
- The person card shows grudge as a two-decimal number (`person_card.gd:161`)
  and, since #130, rapport as a number too ("Rapport with you",
  `person_card.gd:162`).
- The lever lines print "a grudge +0.18" (`lever_words.gd:83`, `:101`, `:123`).
- The registers:
  - `person_rapport`, range [−1, 1], with no decay;
  - `person_grudges`, range [0, 1], decaying by 0.008 a month;
  - `person_loyalty`, range [−1, 1] (`nation_state.gd:1453`, `:1396`, `:1377`;
    `characters.gd:167`).

**The rule:** words, never numbers, on every player surface (the person card,
the audience, the request card's price, the lever lines). A number remains only
in a tooltip. Each word band is a threshold on a register, and Content writes
the words (`data/`, through `Strings`). The bands are tied to what the person
will do, so a word means a behaviour.

| Register | Bands (the cut points) | Why these cuts |
|---|---|---|
| Grudge | [0, 0.05): no grudge shown · [0.05, 0.20): *annoyed* · [0.20, 0.45): *resentful* · [0.45, 0.60): *hostile, may leak* · [0.60, 1]: *bitter, may defect* | 0.05 is the card's current floor; 0.45 is the brief gate (`GRUDGE_BRIEF_THRESHOLD`); 0.60 is the defection gate (`GRUDGE_DEFECT_THRESHOLD`), so the word names the act that becomes possible |
| Rapport | [−1, −0.30): *cold* · [−0.30, −0.05): *wary* · [−0.05, +0.05]: *neutral* · (+0.05, +0.30]: *warm* · (+0.30, 1]: *close* | ±0.05 is half of one decline's rapport (−0.10), so a single refusal from neutral is always visible. A delegation's snub (−0.05) sits exactly on the cut, and the closed side keeps it *neutral*, so only a refusal moves this word |
| Loyalty | [−1, −0.40): *ready to resign* for an office holder, *disloyal* for anyone else · [−0.40, −0.10): *uncertain* · [−0.10, +0.10]: no word shown · (+0.10, +0.40]: *loyal* · (+0.40, 1]: *devoted* | −0.40 is the resign floor (`LOYALTY_RESIGN_FLOOR`). The resign act covers only an office holder (`characters.gd`, `_office_held`), so the word promises it only where it can happen |

**Which side of each cut a value falls on is declared:** [ ) includes the
lower bound, ( ] the upper. A value within 1e-9 of a cut counts as on
the cut, on either kind of bracket (one shared helper; [[bands-v5.2]], the
B6 amendment, states both pairs of comparisons), so a sum of
authored steps that lands on a cut in decimals lands on it in the game: three
declines from 0 read −0.30, not −0.30000000000000004. Every person starts at loyalty 0.0
(`characters.gd:69`), so loyalty has a neutral band around 0 and shows no word on
day 1. A cut at the default value would have given all 1,595 people the same
word.

**A delegation may or may not move a feeling word**, and no row claims either.
Its grudge goes through the trait intake: 0.045 for an ordinary person (under
the floor from 0), and 0.09 for a principled one, which reads *annoyed*. Its
snub (−0.05) applies only to a vain person or an office want
(`audiences.gd:149-151` at `47e1727a`). From any rapport in [0, +0.10] the snub
lands in *neutral*: from [0, +0.05] no word moves, and from (+0.05, +0.10] the
word moves from *warm* to *neutral*. From other values a delegation can cross a
cut, and the words then show it. The dated line is the delegation's guaranteed
trace.

**The words are Content's; the cut points are declared here.** The italic words
above are placeholders.

**The cost of a refusal, shown afterwards (Q2):**

- After a decline or a lapse, the person card carries a dated line: "<date>:
  you refused them; they took it badly". Its word changes come from the
  register deltas. The line stays for 12 months, matching the Diary's "Your
  word".
- The request card's price is shown in words before the answer ("they will be
  annoyed; rapport turns wary"). The card shows after the answer exactly what
  it promised before.
- With the cuts above, one decline from neutral moves rapport to *wary*
  (−0.10). It moves grudge to *annoyed* (0.18), or *resentful* for a principled
  person (0.36). So a single refusal is visible on two lines.

**Bands (the interface's invariants; Systems has nothing to build):**

| | Declared |
|---|---|
| One decline or lapse from a person at rapport 0 and grudge 0 changes at least one shown word | **100 %** |
| A delegation writes a dated line on the person ("your minister promised them <want> by <date>") | **100 %** |
| A broken delegated commitment changes at least one word (grudge +0.215 reaches *resentful*; rapport −0.2 reaches *wary*) | **100 %** |
| Player surfaces that print a grudge, rapport or loyalty number outside a tooltip | **0** (a lint, in the style of `test_i18n_lint`) |
| The words shown after an answer equal the words its card promised before | **100 %** |

## R4 — dismissing a minister

**The author:** "i cant revoke ministers".

**Today (MEASURED):**

- A portfolio is held by a party, not a person: `ns.cabinet[portfolio] =
  party_id` (`src/sim/cabinet.gd:249`).
- The only verb is `assign()`, which reassigns a portfolio to another coalition
  party for a bloc cost of −0.04 (`RESHUFFLE_BLOC_PENALTY`). It is booked
  through the Diary.
- A party that holds fewer portfolios than it is entitled to loses loyalty:
  0.5 × the shortfall (`cabinet.gd:163`). **Corrected (2026-10-25):** this
  loyalty reaches resignations only (`cabinet.gd:323`, the one reader beside
  R4's own preview in `removal.gd:93-94`). The division never reads it:
  `Legislature.resolve_vote()` takes each party's line from `support()`, with
  `Government.coalition_bonus()` = 0.45 × cohesion × discipline, and its rebels
  from `Discipline.rebel_seats()`; both read `ns.party_discipline`, never the
  cabinet (`legislature.gd:620-633`, `government.gd:401-404`,
  `discipline.gd:148-161`, at `a606f1e2`). The first draft's claim that the
  shortfall "feeds the rebels on every division" was false, and so was the
  preview built on it. "The Assembly" below fixes both.
- A vacancy runs at effectiveness 0.5 (`VACANT_EFFECTIVENESS`).

**The rule — a new verb, Dismiss:**

- **What it does:** the president removes the portfolio's holder at once (a
  dismissal is sudden, so it is not booked in the Diary). The portfolio is left
  vacant at effectiveness 0.5 until a Reassign.
- **The holder's party:**
  - its held count falls, so its shortfall rises and its loyalty falls by 0.5 ×
    the change (the existing formula; no new number);
  - its leader takes a grudge pulse of **0.25**, through a removal door of its
    own. The door applies the trait intake (principled ×2, loyal ×0.5) and the
    register's [0, 1] clamp, but not the refusal ceiling.
    `Characters.price_refusal` caps a refusal at `GRUDGE_REFUSAL_CEILING`, 0.47
    (`characters.gd:643-647`, `character_act_constants.gd:215`), so that
    refusals alone never cross into the acts. A dismissal is an act against
    the person, not a refusal, so it may.
    When the party's CURRENT leader (after any `party_leader_override`) is the
    president, no pulse is written. The cost is then the party's loyalty change alone: a
    president can reshuffle their own people with no personal grudge, but
    their party still feels the shortfall.
- **The country:** a bloc cost of −0.04, the reshuffle's own
  (`RESHUFFLE_BLOC_PENALTY`).
- **Reassign carries the same price when it takes a portfolio from a party**
  (red-team, re-derived below). Today a Reassign moves a portfolio from party
  A to party B at −0.04 and nothing else. With the pulse on Dismiss alone,
  Reassign would be a dismissal with no grudge, and it would dominate. So
  every removal of a party's portfolio writes the 0.25 pulse on that party's
  leader, whether by Dismiss or by Reassign away. Filling a vacancy writes
  none. Cabinet's automatic resignation vacancy (`RESIGNATION_THRESHOLD`,
  `cabinet.gd:301`) is not a removal and writes no pulse.
- **The limit:** one removal (Dismiss or Reassign away) per portfolio per 12
  months (OPINION: a government that changes the same office twice in a year
  reads as chaos, and the limit keeps the verb from being a free loop). A purge
  of all five portfolios in one month stays possible, at its full price: five
  pulses, −0.20 in bloc costs and five shortfalls.
- **The Assembly: the party's discipline (declared 2026-10-25).** The
  author was offered dismissal "avec ses conséquences : rancune, son parti,
  l'Assemblée". Today the third part does not exist (above). So every removal
  (Dismiss, or Reassign away) also steps the holder's party's live discipline
  down by **0.10**, through the existing writer `Discipline.penalise()`
  (`discipline.gd:110-120`), clamped to [`DISCIPLINE_MIN` 0.10, the party's
  authored figure]. A new constant `DISMISSAL_DISCIPLINE_PENALTY := -0.10` goes
  beside `REBELLION_PENALTY` (−0.05) and `FORCE_DISCIPLINE_PENALTY` (−0.07) in
  `discipline_constants.gd`.
  - **It reaches the division through both of discipline's readers, and no new
    path:** the party's line falls, because the coalition bonus is 0.45 ×
    cohesion × discipline, so 0.045 × cohesion less support; and its rebels
    rise, because the rebel share is 0.35 × (1 − discipline) × strain / 0.45.
  - **Worked example (INFERRED, from the formulas):** a partner of 115 seats at
    discipline 0.85 and cohesion 1, on a government bill its members would
    vote against (line yes, own view no, strain 0.30). Before: round(115 × 0.35
    × 0.15 × 0.30 / 0.45) = 4 rebels. After one dismissal, discipline 0.75 and
    the line 0.045 lower, so strain 0.255: round(115 × 0.35 × 0.25 × 0.255 /
    0.45) = 6 rebels. And a party whose support sat within 0.045 of its
    yes threshold changes its whole line.
  - **Decay: none of its own.** The monthly tick already pulls discipline back
    toward its target at `DISCIPLINE_INERTIA` 0.08 (`discipline.gd:94-107`).
    With the target unchanged, 0.92^n of the step remains: about two thirds
    after 5 months, 37 % after 12, a half-life of about 8.3 months. That falls
    inside the one-removal-per-portfolio-per-12-months limit, so a second
    removal from the same office meets a party that has mostly, not wholly,
    recovered.
  - **The president's own party pays it too.** No grudge pulse is written when
    its leader is the president (above), but its deputies still see a colleague
    sacked; in France the president's party holds most portfolios, so this is
    the common case.
  - **Why 0.10 (OPINION):** above Article 49.3's −0.07, which overrides the
    whole house; a dismissal singles out one party, so that party feels it
    more. It equals two full-severity rebellions (`REBELLION_PENALTY` −0.05 at
    severity 1). **The worst case is set by the count, not the clamp:** a
    five-portfolio purge of one party in one month is −0.50, which takes a party
    at the common authored 0.85 to 0.35. The 0.10 floor binds only from an
    authored 0.60, one party in data (authored discipline runs 0.60 to 0.95,
    with 85 parties at 0.85; QA, on `b9eebd30`). No cap (OPINION): a purge of one
    party's every office should be ruinous in the Assembly, and the
    one-removal-per-portfolio-per-12-months limit already stops a repeat.
- **What the player sees:** the confirmation names the party, the leader's
  feeling after the pulse (R11's words), and the party's reliability in the
  Assembly: when a bill is tabled (R2), its forecast yes and no before and after
  the dismissal, read on a state copy with the discipline step applied (the
  `Budget.project()` pattern, so the preview writes nothing); with no bill
  tabled, a sentence that the party's deputies will be less reliable for about
  eight months. Not `Cabinet.loyalty`, which no division reads. It is the price
  before the act, the same contract as the request card.
- **Why 0.25 (OPINION):**
  - it sits above a refusal (0.18): losing an office is felt more than a denied
    request;
  - it sits below the brief gate (0.45): one dismissal alone never makes an
    ordinary leader leak;
  - doubled for a principled leader it is 0.50, past the brief gate (the
    removal door has no refusal ceiling). A principled leader whose minister
    is sacked briefs against the government. A second removal from the same
    party can carry any leader toward the defection gate (0.60).
    That is the consequence the author asked for, on the person most likely to
    take it personally.

**Bands:**

| | Declared |
|---|---|
| Any removal of a party's portfolio (Dismiss, or Reassign away) writes the leader's pulse (0.25 × the leader's grudge factors, clamped at 1.0, no refusal ceiling) and the party's loyalty change (0.5 × Δshortfall) | **100 %** (a test) |
| A second removal of the same portfolio within 12 months | **refused, with the reason named** |
| Re-baseline note: Reassign away now carries the pulse, so `managed_cabinet_shorted` and `austerity_cabinet_shorted` (the regimes that call `Cabinet.assign` at month 6) MAY move | **predicted**, reported for the author |

| Re-baseline: every other regime | **ZERO** (INFERRED: no other regime removes a portfolio; Systems confirms by `cmp`) |
| Any removal steps the holder party's live discipline by −0.10 through `Discipline.penalise()`, clamped to [0.10, the authored figure], the president's own party included (declared 2026-10-25) | **100 %** (a test) |

| France at month 6: dismiss one portfolio of the largest coalition party other than the president's (the president's own if none), against an undismissed twin. Measured on the next government bill whose division has the worked example's shape in the twin: that party's line yes and its members' own view no (`legislature.gd:630-631` computes rebels only then). The count is that party's **yes seats**, which catches both more rebels and a whole line that flips | **at least 1 fewer yes seat**, and the bill's yes margin no higher; reported with the numbers (Systems' probe, run with R2's tabling) |

| Re-baseline note: `managed_cabinet_shorted` and `austerity_cabinet_shorted` reassign at month 6, so the discipline step moves them further than the pulse alone | **predicted to move**, reported for the author |

## R14 — nationalise or privatise only through a law, a vote and a cost

**The author:** nationalising or privatising at will is unrealistic.

**Today (MEASURED):**

- Three company actions are executive verbs (`data/company-actions/ownership.json`):
  - a minority stake of 0.35;
  - full nationalisation;
  - privatisation.
- The price goes onto the debt as a balance-sheet swap (`companies.gd:999-1018`).
- No law touches ownership: a grep of `data/laws/` finds nothing.

**The rule:**

- **Full nationalisation and privatisation become bills.** In France,
  nationalisations and transfers of ownership are matters for the law (the
  Constitution, Art. 34), so the president tables one and the Assembly votes on
  it.
  - Each action carries `"requires_law": true` in data. Code reads the field,
    never an action id (rule 1).
  - The bill names its firm, through a new effect key `company_ownership:
    {company_id, stake}`. It is offered as a ready bill for each eligible firm
    (no drafting period, unlike a composed law in `src/sim/statute.gd`). The
    vote is the cost, not a wait.
  - It is an ordinary law at a simple majority. The second chamber may hold it,
    as it holds any ordinary bill.
  - It divides at the month's close and is visible as in R2.
- **The cost:**
  - the price stays as today, on the debt, paid only if the bill carries;
  - the political price stays the action's own `bloc_offset`, applied on
    adoption;
  - the vote is the new cost: the forecast, the rebels and a possible defeat.
    The court may strike a bill that reaches past its tolerance, as for any law.
- **The minority stake stays an executive act.** A state holding agency buys
  minority stakes without a law, so the verb remains for a stake of at most
  0.35, without a vote. **It cannot stack** (MEASURED): its precondition is
  `state_owned == 0`, and `state_owned` reads 1 for any state share above 0
  (`companies.gd:732`). So a second stake, or a full nationalisation on top of
  a stake, is refused. Only the law takes a firm from 0.35 to 1.0, after a
  privatisation back to 0.
- **Article 49.3 may carry an ownership bill (intended).** It is the
  president's costly bypass for any ordinary bill: a censure motion risked, an
  approval cost and its cooldown. The vote's cost is replaced by 49.3's own,
  not removed.
- **The bill's position and cooldown (declared 2026-10-24, before #146's
  re-baseline goes to the author).** #146 shipped them as provisional values;
  they stand, for these reasons:

  | Bill | Position | Cooldown |
  |---|---|---|
  | full nationalisation | economic **−0.6**, social **0.0** | **24 months** |
  | privatisation | economic **+0.6**, social **0.0** | **24 months** |

  - **−0.6 (OPINION, placed against the roster):** taking a whole firm into
    state ownership is one of the most interventionist acts a government
    tables. It sits with the roster's −0.6 laws (`minimum_wage_rise`,
    `high_earner_surtax`, `rent_stabilisation_act`), one step inside the two
    flagships at −0.7 (`wealth_levy`, `working_time_reduction`). The 120
    authored laws span −0.7 to +0.7 on the economic axis (`data/laws/*.json`,
    MEASURED).
  - **+0.6 for privatisation:** the mirror, with `corporate_tax_cut`,
    `industrial_deregulation` and `labour_market_flexibilisation`, one step
    inside `financial_deregulation` at +0.7. Symmetric on purpose: neither
    direction is cheaper to pass by content, so the Assembly's own make-up
    decides which one carries.
  - **Social 0.0:** ownership is an economic question. A per-sector social lean
    (for example a nationalist party backing the nationalisation of a
    strategic firm) would be truer, but no decision in this block turns on it.
    It would also put the position on the firm rather than on the verb. Not
    built.
  - **24 months:** the roster's usual cooldown, carried by 66 of the 120 laws
    (MEASURED). It is keyed by bill id, so by verb and firm, and the
    legislature writes it only when a bill fails: defeated, blocked, struck
    down, or lost at a referendum (`legislature.gd:172`, `:349`, `:1384`,
    `:1411` at `c7ffdc1f`; a repeal writes it too, `:1004`). So a defeat on one firm locks that firm's same
    verb for two years, and leaves every other firm open.
  - **No cooldown on adoption (INFERRED, kept):** once nationalisation is
    adopted, the privatisation bill for that firm opens at once. A flip back
    needs a majority for +0.6 straight after one for −0.6, so the vote itself
    stops a flip-flop, and each flip pays its `bloc_offset` and its price.
  - **What #146 measured with these values:** in `managed_nationalises`, the
    month-6 bill for `FRA_meridienne` is defeated, 156 to 421, and the
    regime's rows land on `managed`'s (#146's PR body). That is the rule
    working: France's centrist Assembly refuses a −0.6 bill. Privatisation's
    fate in France has not been measured.

**Bands:**

| | Declared |
|---|---|
| A full nationalisation or a privatisation that changes `state_share` without an adopted law (carried by a vote, or forced by Article 49.3) | **0** (a test) |
| Re-baseline: every regime but `managed_nationalises` | **ZERO** (INFERRED: no other regime calls the ownership verbs) |
| Re-baseline: `managed_nationalises` | **moves**, predicted: its month-6 nationalisation becomes a bill that may be held by the second chamber or defeated (the regime row is `tools/balance_probe.gd:307`; `_nationalise` at `:1500-1505` picks the largest stake, full nationalisation). The rows and the bill's fate are reported, and the author accepts them |
