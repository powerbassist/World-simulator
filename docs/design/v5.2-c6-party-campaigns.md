# V5.2 C6 — campaigns paid by the party

**The author (gate b, item R9, verbatim):** "campaign efforts are funded by the
state, it should be by the party" (`.superpowers/sdd/v5.2-gateb-report.md`,
author batch 1).

This spec re-prices the five campaign efforts at party scale, pays them only
from the governing party's money, gives every party of the played nation a
public-funding income, and retires the state spending line. The bands it moves
are declared in [[bands-v5.2]] as **B9**, before Systems runs anything. It
supersedes [[v5.2-spec]] C6's "a campaign can draw on it before the state line":
there is no state line any more.

Labels: **MEASURED** (a cited line, a probe table or a source), **INFERRED**
(read from the code or derived, not run), **OPINION** (the designer's call).
Source lines are pinned to origin/main `47e1727a`.

## Today

- **MEASURED.** Each effort carries `cost_pct_gdp`, a standing rate for as long
  as it runs (`data/campaign-actions/campaign.json`): ground game 0.03, donor
  circuit 0.045, town halls 0.055, digital push 0.075, broadcast 0.10 points of
  GDP a year.
- **MEASURED.** The sum is a computed spending line, `campaign_bill_pct_gdp`
  (`src/sim/campaign.gd:480-485`), added to spending in `src/sim/budget.gd:172`.
  It widens the deficit, and the deficit reaches the `public_finances` issue
  through `opinion_constants.DEFICIT_SCALE_PP` (6.0, `opinion_constants.gd:365`).
  That fiscal hit is the campaign's only price today ([[campaign-model]] §6).
- **INFERRED.** For France (GDP about EUR 2.9 trillion) one effort costs EUR
  0.9-2.9 bn a year. The governing party holds EUR 48 m
  (`data/parties/FRA.json`, `renouveau_civique.funds_eur_m`). Paid from party
  money at today's prices, the chest would last days.
- **MEASURED.** The register already exists: the start `Party.funds_eur_m`
  (`src/core/party.gd:35-39`), the delta `ns.party_money_delta_eur_m`
  (`nation_fields.gd:334`, `absent_ok`), the read
  `President.party_money_eur_m()` (`president.gd:98-103`) and the writer
  `President.adjust_party_money_eur_m()` (`president.gd:208-212`). Nothing in
  `src/` calls the writer yet.
- **MEASURED.** At most two efforts run at once (every start carries the
  precondition `campaign_count <= 1`), each starts at most once per window
  (`campaign_started`), and the window is `CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_MONTHS` = 10
  (`campaign_constants.gd:90`). A stopped effort keeps what it bought
  (`campaign.gd:615-619`).

## The rule

### 1. The payer is the governing party

The party that pays is `ns.incumbent_party_id`'s, the president's own. No other
party's money is touched, and no other nation campaigns ([[v5.2-spec]] C12; the
AI nations book nothing).

### 2. The price, per thousand inhabitants per month

Each effort is re-priced as **EUR per thousand inhabitants per month**, read
against `ns.population` (`nation_state.gd:119`; France 68.6 m,
`data/nations/FRA.json`). The new term is `cost_eur_per_k_pop_month`; it
replaces `cost_pct_gdp` in `CAMPAIGN_OP_TERMS` (`campaign_constants.gd:179-182`),
and the content ceiling `MAX_CAMPAIGN_COST_PCT_GDP` becomes a ceiling in the new
unit.

| Effort | Today, % of GDP a year | New, EUR per 1 000 people a month | France, EUR m a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground game | 0.03 | **8.1** | 0.56 |
| donor circuit | 0.045 | **12.1** | 0.83 |
| town halls | 0.055 | **14.8** | 1.02 |
| digital push | 0.075 | **20.2** | 1.39 |
| broadcast | 0.10 | **26.9** | 1.85 |

- **The anchor (MEASURED source, INFERRED arithmetic).** France's legal ceiling
  on a presidential campaign is **EUR 22.509 m** for each of the two run-off
  candidates, and EUR 16.851 m for a first-round candidate (2022 figures; the
  Loire-Atlantique prefecture's *Mémento éléments financiers Présidentielle
  2022*; LCP, "Dons, banques, argent public... D'où vient l'argent des campagnes
  présidentielles?"). The new prices set the **mean** of the ten allowed pairs,
  run for a full 10-month window, at that ceiling: mean pair 0.122 % of GDP a
  year, so one point of the old unit becomes EUR 18.45 m a month for France,
  then EUR 268.9 per thousand inhabitants.
- **One factor for every effort (INFERRED).** Every price is the old one times
  the same factor, rounded to 0.1, so the ratios between efforts hold to within
  0.4 %. `tools/campaign_probe.gd`'s check that no effort is dominated compares
  reach against price, so a uniform rescale leaves its verdict as it is.
- **Why per inhabitant and not a flat EUR figure (OPINION).** A flat EUR price
  fits the four playable nations, but every nation is meant to become playable,
  and their governing parties hold EUR 0.1-70 m. France's own law already scales
  a legislative candidate's ceiling with the population it covers: EUR 38 000
  plus EUR 0.15 per inhabitant of the constituency, times an update coefficient
  (Code électoral, art. L52-11; the Alpes-Maritimes prefecture's *Plafonds des
  dépenses de campagne LEG2024*). Per
  inhabitant, a full window of the dearest pair costs EUR 32.3 m in France and
  EUR 17.2 m in Poland. A flat price would charge Poland the same as France.

### 3. Paying, and stopping when the money runs out

- **A start needs one month's price in the chest.** `Campaign.execute()`
  refuses a start when `President.party_money_eur_m(incumbent)` is below the
  effort's price for one month, with the reason "the party cannot pay for it".
  The panel shows that reason on the disabled button. The check is one month of
  the new effort alone, so with another effort running the chest may pay both
  for fewer months than the player expects: the panel shows, before the start,
  how many months the chest pays at the combined rate (section 9).
- **Each month is paid before it is accrued.** In `Campaign.tick_month()`,
  before `_accrue()`, each running effort is paid for the month, **the cheaper
  first** (ties by effort id). An effort the chest cannot pay **stops**: it
  keeps what it bought, like a player's stop. Cheaper first means a short chest
  buys the most months of effort, and the order is one a player can predict: the
  dearer effort is the one that stops. (The first draft paid in `_accrue()`'s
  id order, `campaign.gd:214-215`; the red-team found it let an alphabetically
  earlier, dearer effort starve a cheaper one.)
- **A stop for money is a note, never a hold.** It does not count against the
  4 + 6 budget of stops and holding cards ([[decision-surfaces]]).
- **The projection pays on its copy only (INFERRED).** `Budget.project()` runs
  `Campaign.tick_month()` on a copy, and `party_money_delta_eur_m` is a DEEP
  field (`nation_fields.gd:334`), so a forecast never spends real money.
- **The money can go below zero only through running costs** (section 6), never
  through an effort: the start and the monthly payment both refuse first. A
  party in debt can run nothing.

### 4. The legal ceiling (author's call 3)

- **Data:** an optional per-nation ceiling on what one window may spend,
  `campaign_spending_cap_eur_m`. France: 22.509 (the run-off figure, because
  the window ends at the run-off month, 2027-05). Absent means no ceiling, which
  is Germany's case: German law caps no party's campaign spending (Content
  sources it).
- **What the window has spent** is a register, `campaign_spent_eur_m`
  (`absent_ok`, absent reads 0, cleared when the window closes, like the rest of
  the campaign state in `_clear()`).
- **Recommended, a soft ceiling:** the president may spend past it. Each EUR m
  over the ceiling leaves the evidence a party-money donation of the same size
  leaves (kind `party_money`, `president_constants.gd:27`), so the auditors'
  route can find it. This is the real case of the outgoing president's 2012
  campaign, whose account was rejected and so lost the state's refund (Public
  Sénat, "Présidentielle 2022: quelles sont les règles de financement de la
  campagne?"). It gives
  France's party money a use, ties C6 to c's shadows, and makes overspending a
  priced risk. It needs c1's evidence writer (`.superpowers/sdd/v5.2c-plan.md`,
  `President.grow_evidence`), so C6 builds after c1.
- **Alternative, a hard ceiling:** a start or a monthly payment that would pass
  the ceiling is refused or stops. Simpler, independent of c1. In France the
  chest would then never bind (section 7).

### 5. The income: public funding, from two pots

- **Data (author's call 2):** a per-nation block `party_funding` with two pots
  and their sources:
  - `vote_pot_eur_m_year`, shared among the nation's parties by vote share;
  - `seat_pot_eur_m_year`, shared by seat share.
- **France (MEASURED sources):** for 2024 the first fraction is **EUR 32.25 m**
  (by first-round votes at the last legislative election) and the second **EUR
  34.19 m** (by parliamentarians), EUR 66.44 m in all (Le Grand Continent, 9 July
  2024, "Financement public: le RN devrait augmenter nettement ses ressources";
  LCP, "Législatives 2024: le 1er tour, victoire politique et pactole assuré pour
  le Rassemblement national"). The per-seat equivalent is about EUR 37 000 per
  parliamentarian a year (LCP), which is 34.19 m over 925 seats (577 deputies
  and 348 senators).
- **Why pots, not a rate per vote and per seat (OPINION).** French law fixes the
  two pots each year by decree and divides them; the per-vote figure is derived.
  A rate per vote would also need an electorate and a turnout per election, and
  the game holds neither. A rate law such as Germany's converts to a pot at the
  last election's votes, and Content records the conversion in the row's note.
  The per-vote and per-seat equivalents stay in the note as the check.
- **The check (INFERRED arithmetic, partly sourced).** With the pots, the RN's
  2024 result gives 32.25 × 0.2925 × 0.93 + 126 × 0.037 = EUR 13.4 m a year,
  against the EUR 13.33 m Le Grand Continent reports. Sourced: 29.25 % of
  first-round votes (the Interior ministry's figure, in Public Sénat's results
  page, "Législatives 2024: tous les résultats en temps réel") and the parity
  deduction of about 7 % (LCP, above). **Not sourced:** the 126
  parliamentarians, an estimate of the RN's deputies plus its few senators.
- **The shares:**
  - **After a legislative election:** the seat share is `ns.seats_by_party`
    over `ns.assembly_seats` (written at `elections.gd:144`), and the vote share
    is that result's `vote_share` (`ns.last_election`, written at
    `elections.gd:145`; executive and termed-out results never write it).
  - **Before the first one** (`last_election` is `{}`, `elections.gd:106`):
    both shares are the authored `seat_share` (`data/parties/*.json`). Not
    `seats_by_party`: that is whole seats from `ElectoralSystems.pr_national()`
    (`elections.gd:104`), so France's 0.28 (161.6 of 577) becomes a whole number
    of seats (INFERRED), and the net income of section 6 would be a few thousand euros a year instead
    of exactly zero. French law also fixes the first fraction for a whole
    legislature from its election's result, so the purse moving only at an
    election is the real shape.
- **Dropped, and said so:** the 1 %-in-50-constituencies threshold, the parity
  deduction, and the Senate's own composition (the seat pot is shared by
  Assembly seats alone). Each is a refinement no decision in the game turns on.
- **Cadence:** a twelfth of each party's yearly funding, every month, for every
  party of the played nation (the V5.2a registers' scope, [[v5.2-spec]] answer
  1), through `President.adjust_party_money_eur_m()`.

### 6. Running costs: the party spends what it was sized for

- **The rule (OPINION):** each party's running costs equal its public funding at
  the game's start. Its net income is therefore funding now less funding at the
  start: **zero until an election changes its shares**, positive after a win,
  negative after a loss.
- **Why:** without running costs the chest only grows. France's governing party
  would hold EUR 48 m + about 18.6 m a year (66.44 × 0.28): about EUR 166 m by
  the 2032 vote before any campaign (6.33 years from January 2026), against a full window of the dearest pair at EUR 32.3 m. The money would never
  bind, and c1's party-money offer would buy nothing. Real parties spend most of
  their funding on staff and premises.
- **What it gives the player:** a dissolution or a legislative election now moves
  the party's purse. Winning seats funds the next campaign; losing them drains
  the chest.
- **Storage: none.** The running costs are the funding computed at the
  **authored** shares (`data/parties/*.json`, `seat_share`, standing in for both
  shares), a value derived from content at every read, as `_terms_by_effort()`
  derives the prices (`campaign.gd:492-503`). No register, so nothing for a save
  to carry and no bump. Since the shares before the first election are the same
  authored values (section 5), the net income is exactly zero until a
  legislative election, at the standard start and in every scenario, including
  those that override the seats (`deu_grand_coalition`, `gbr_hung_parliament`,
  `pol_coalition`): their purse moves at their first election.

### 7. The arithmetic: where the money and the ceiling bind

INFERRED, from the authored chests (`data/parties/*.json`), the populations
(`data/nations/*.json`) and the prices above; net income zero (section 6).

| Nation, governing party | Chest, EUR m | Full window, cheapest / mean / dearest pair | Ceiling | What binds |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FRA, `renouveau_civique` | 48 | 13.9 / 22.5 / 32.3 | 22.509 | **the ceiling, in every window**: 5 of the 10 pairs fit a full window. With a soft ceiling, the chest binds overspending: 48 is 1.5 full windows of the dearest pair |
| DEU, `buergerunion` | 60 | 16.9 / 27.4 / 39.3 | none | **the chest, in the second window**: 60 − 39.3 = 20.7 left |
| GBR, `progress_alliance` | 70 | 14.0 / 22.8 / 32.6 | Content sources it | nothing, unless the ceiling is under 32.6: two full windows of the dearest pair cost 65.2 |
| POL, `narod_i_tradycja` | 39 | 7.4 / 12.0 / 17.2 | Content sources it | nothing, unless the ceiling is under 17.2: two windows cost 34.4 |

- **The choice inside a window:** under a ceiling, which pair and for how many
  months. That is the timing decision the campaign was built around
  ([[campaign-model]] §6), now priced in money instead of in the deficit.
- **The choice across windows:** where the chest binds, spending fully now
  leaves less for the next vote, and a party-money offer (c1) refills it.

### 8. Retiring the state line

- `campaign_bill_pct_gdp` goes, with its readers: the spending sum
  (`budget.gd:172`), the snapshot (`budget.gd:839-840`), the budget panel's
  figure (`budget_panel.gd:547`), the campaign panel's row
  (`campaign_panel.gd:218`), `campaign.gd`'s three writers (lines 160, 189, 242)
  and `bill_pct_gdp()`.
- **No save bump (MEASURED).** `Fields.read_into()` reads only the keys its table
  names (`nation_fields.gd:69-74`), so an older save's `campaign_bill_pct_gdp` is
  ignored. The one new register, `campaign_spent_eur_m`, is `absent_ok`; the
  running costs are derived, not stored (section 6); party money's delta is
  already `absent_ok`.
- **The `DEFICIT_SCALE_PP` headroom it used is freed:** 0.1750 of the 0.2039
  points ([[campaign-model]] §7b). Nothing claims it in this block.

### 9. What the player sees

- **The campaign panel:** the party's money, each effort's price a month, the
  months the chest can pay at the current rate (and, before a start, at the
  rate with the new effort added), and the ceiling's room left
  (with a soft ceiling, the amount spent past it, named as what the auditors
  can find).
- **The budget panel:** the campaign line goes.
- **The party's purse, somewhere the player looks:** the funding a year, the
  running costs, and the net. The People or party view is Interface's choice.

## Red-team: dominated paths

Searched by hand first, then by GLM 5.3; every GLM claim was re-derived before
it was kept (the list of what GLM said and what survived is in the PR body).

1. **"Spend everything every window" is dominant where nothing binds**
   (INFERRED). Party money has one sink, the campaign, until c1 adds offers,
   and c1 adds income, not a sink. Where neither the chest nor the ceiling
   binds (GBR and POL in the table, pending their ceilings), the money decision
   is empty and only the pair and the timing remain. **Kept as a known limit:**
   the decision the campaign poses there is the one it posed before C6, with
   the fiscal price gone.
2. **A soft ceiling with no evidence would be a free upgrade** (INFERRED). That
   is why over-ceiling spending writes party-money evidence at the same rate as
   a donation: overspending EUR 10 m is as exposed as taking EUR 10 m.
3. **Stopping before running out wastes nothing** (INFERRED). A stopped effort
   keeps its accrued mobilisation, and payment is monthly, so a player cannot be
   charged for a month the effort did not run.
4. **The cheap pair cannot dominate by price alone** (INFERRED). The ratios are
   unchanged, so `campaign_probe.gd`'s dominance table stands. Under a ceiling
   the cheap pair runs longer, but the dear pairs reach other blocs, and France's
   centrist owns the small blocs ([[campaign-model]] §7d), so which pair wins
   depends on whose voters the president needs. Per euro, town halls reaches
   rural voters and pensioners more cheaply than broadcast, but broadcast reaches
   more pensioners per month and also moves workers and students, and only two
   efforts run at once, so where the slots bind before the money, broadcast is
   not dominated.
5. **Payment order** (GLM, kept): paying in id order let a dearer effort that
   sorts first starve a cheaper one. Fixed in section 3: the cheaper is paid
   first.
6. **A dissolution now also moves the purse** (GLM, kept as intended). Winning
   seats raises the net income; losing them lowers it. The stake is small beside
   a dissolution's own: three points of both vote and seat share move the funding
   by about EUR 2 m a year (66.44 × 0.03), against a window's ceiling of EUR
   22.5 m. It is
   a reason more, never the reason.

## The author's calls

1. **The price unit.** (A, recommended) EUR per thousand inhabitants per month:
   it scales to every nation. (B) A flat EUR price per month: simpler to read,
   wrong for small nations.
2. **Where the income rule lives.** (A, recommended) A per-nation
   `party_funding` block in `data/nations/*.json`: funding is national law, and
   France shares `semi_presidential_republic` with Romania, Turkey, Ukraine,
   Cyprus and Belarus, whose laws differ. (B) A field on
   `data/political-systems/*.json`, as briefed: one value per regime type, so
   France's pots would apply to all six.
3. **The ceiling.** (A, recommended) A soft ceiling: overspending leaves
   party-money evidence for the auditors. Needs c1 first. (B) A hard ceiling:
   refused past it; independent of c1; France's chest never binds. (C) No
   ceiling: the chest alone, which binds in France only in the second window.

## Proposals for other owners

- **Content:** re-price `data/campaign-actions/campaign.json` (the table in
  section 2); author `party_funding` for the played nations first (France as
  above, with the sources), then the rest; author `campaign_spending_cap_eur_m`
  where a law sets one (France 22.509; Germany none; the United Kingdom and
  Poland sourced). The United Kingdom's public money goes almost entirely to
  opposition parties (Short money); that is a fact for the row's note, and the
  governing party's pots may be near zero.
- **Systems:** the payment and stop rule (section 3), the income and running
  costs (sections 5-6), the ceiling (section 4, after c1 if the soft ceiling is
  taken), the retirement (section 8), and the predicted re-baseline against
  [[bands-v5.2]] B9. `tools/balance_probe.gd` prints `cost_pct_gdp` when it opens
  a campaign (line 2113) and its header still says `CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_MONTHS` is 8
  (line 461; it is 10): both need the new term. Its `_campaign()` starts efforts
  through `Campaign.execute()`, so the harness pays and stops exactly as the
  game does.
- **Systems, c1's tuning:** a party-money offer should be about the size of one
  or two months of effort (France EUR 0.6-3.7 m), or the link between a bribe
  and a bigger campaign is invisible.
- **Interface:** section 9.
- **Model docs (builders):** [[campaign-model]] §6 and §9 (the bill and its
  constants), and `tools/campaign_probe.gd`'s clause (ii), which B9 replaces.

## Not in this spec

- **The state's refund** of 47.5 % of the ceiling to a candidate above 5 %
  (2022 sources above). It is real, but it is the state paying for the campaign,
  which R9 asks to remove, and it would hand every incumbent half the price
  back. A `campaign_refund_share` field would be cheap if the author wants it.
- **Private income** (members' dues and donations, capped at EUR 7 500 a person
  a year in France): folded into the authored start and the running costs.
- **`CAMPAIGN_WINDOW_MONTHS` is not re-tuned.** It was chosen against clauses (i)
  to (iii) ([[campaign-model]] §8); clause (ii) retires, which can only widen
  the admissible windows.

## What could not be verified

  harness's campaign fits under the ceiling (B9's prediction).
- The United Kingdom's and Poland's ceilings, and Germany's and Poland's pots:
  Content sources them.
- The 2025 pots: one source reports EUR 64.15 m in all for 2025 (Landot
