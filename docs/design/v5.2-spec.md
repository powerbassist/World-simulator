# V5.2 design spec — People who talk

The design for [[v5]]'s block V5.2 (sub-blocks a–d): people ask to see the
president, talk, bargain, lie and remember, and the president knows some of them
better than others. This page maps every player decision in the block to its
trade-off, shows that no influence lever dominates the others, lists every
conflict with what the project has already decided, and ends with a pre-mortem.
The bands that judge the build are declared in [[bands-v5.2]], before anything
is built.

This is a design, not a build plan. Where it says "lands on", it names the
existing machine a piece should reuse; the owner (Systems for `src/sim` and
`src/core`, Interface for `src/ui`, Content for `data/`) decides how.

## The registers (V5.2a)

Each register is an edge **from the president to one person**, or a field of
the president alone. None is a person-to-person edge. Each is a delta over
content, so an empty register reads as "nothing has happened yet", which is
what the done-when asks of the save table ("never met" reads correctly, no
bump).

**Whom the registers cover — the author's call (2026-10-21, answer 1):** the
played nation's people, plus the foreign heads of state and government the
president meets. Not all 2 147.

| Register | What it holds | Absent reads as | `absent_ok` (proposed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Knowledge | how well the president knows a person, `[0, 1]` | 0, a stranger | true |
| Rapport (V5.2a calls it "affinity") | how the person feels about the president personally, `[-1, 1]` | 0, neutral | true |
| Relationship | stranger, acquaintance, friend, close, rival, enemy, secret affair | stranger | true |
| Social skill | the person's reading and deceiving skill, `[0, 1]` | **content**, never state | n/a (data) |
| Spouse | a `spouse_id` on the leader's person record | **content**, never state | n/a (data) |
| Personal wealth | the leader's wealth, as a delta over an authored start | 0 change | true |
| Party money (the author's call, 2026-10-21, answer 4) | the governing party's funds, as a delta over an authored start | 0 change | true |
| Commitments | dated promises and IOUs in flight | none | true |
| Exposure risk | the hidden evidence a corrupt act or an affair leaves | none | true |

- **OPINION:** "rapport", not "affinity". [[cabinet-model]] already defines
  `affinity(p, f)`, the fit between a party and a portfolio. A second meaning
  for the same word in `src/sim` is a reading trap. The author's word stays in
  the UI text if the author prefers it.
- **INFERRED:** loyalty (`person_loyalty`) and grudges (`person_grudges`) exist
  since V4.3 (`src/core/nation_fields.gd:285-287`). Rapport is not loyalty.
  Loyalty is political (does the person follow the player), and rapport is
  personal (does the person like the president). A rival can be loyal to the
  party line, and a friend can vote against the bill. The levers read both.

## The decisions and their trade-offs

Every decision below costs something in a currency the player can see, and
buys something the player can see. A lever with no cost dominates by repetition,
so none is free.

### An audience request (V5.2b)

| Decision | Costs | Buys | Best when | Worst when | Lands on |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Book** | diary days (the occupancy rule), travel if the person is away | the scene: levers, topics, knowledge gained | the person carries a want the player can serve or needs served | the diary is full; the request is a trap (a corrupt offer) | `calendar-model` §12 occupancy; `met_months` stamp |
| **Delegate** (to a minister or the chief of staff) | no diary days; the result is a fixed, middling outcome; no knowledge gained | the request is answered; most of the grudge avoided | the diary is full; the person matters but not personally | the person wanted the president (`vain`, an office want): rapport falls | the cabinet's portfolio holder; [[cabinet-model]] effectiveness |
| **Decline** | the want is denied: one grudge pulse, and a rapport loss | the days, and no commitment made | the person's want opposes the programme; a corrupt offer | the person is `principled` (grudge ×2) or `networked` (their brief costs ×1.5) | the grudge ladder ([[people-model]] §14.6) |

- **OPINION:** delegation is the valve that keeps booking from becoming R5 again
  (PR #42's audit, R5: "cannot register anything … broken"). A request that the
  diary cannot place must say why and offer delegation on the same card.

### The influence levers, once a topic is reached

Each audience offers **three** attempts (the author's call, 2026-10-21, answer 2). A failed
lever has a cost too, so the player cannot try them all in order for free.

| Lever | Spends | Odds read | Home ground | Hostile ground |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Argue** | an attempt; nothing else | how close the ask sits to the person's wants; knowledge (to pick the right argument) | high knowledge; the ask serves one of the person's wants | `stubborn`; the ask opposes a want |
| **Bargain** | a real currency: a favour (relations or rapport), budget (an aid line), or a vote trade (an IOU) | the currency's size; `pragmatic` | `pragmatic`; a person with an office or meter want the player can pay | `principled`; the player has nothing to pay with |
| **Promise a date** | a commitment: a dated promise; broken, it is a lie revealed | `ambitious`; the date's credibility | an office or programme want with a date the player controls | the date slips (a law the assembly blocks); a high-skill reader |
| **Pressure** | rapport, and a grudge pulse on success as on failure | the person's standing (low is easier); the president's approval | low standing; a person who needs the government | `networked` or `stubborn`: the briefing risk rises |
| **Flatter** | little; the effect fades in months | `vain`; social skill (a good reader sees through it) | `vain`, low skill | `principled`, high skill: it backfires |
| **Appeal to friendship** | the relationship itself, a step down when used | the relationship: gated on friend or closer | a friend or close person, a big ask | anyone below friend: not offered |

- **Lands on (INFERRED from the sources):**
  - The odds are seeded rolls over (person id, month stamp, salt), the
    precedent being `Summits.draw()` on `Cycle.unit()` ([[summits-model]] §2).
  - Bargain reuses the council's three currencies, FAVOUR, AID and VOTE TRADE
    ([[orgs-model]], "Bargains — three currencies"), extended from members to
    people, not rebuilt beside them.
  - Every effect on approval goes through the one applier,
    `Legislature.apply_effects`, as `brief_against` does ([[people-model]] §14.5).
  - A refusal's price is the existing grudge and loyalty registers; no third
    number is built ([[people-model]] §14.1: "two channels").
- **Shown as ranges (the fog call):** the odds print as a range whose width
  falls with knowledge, with its reason ("you barely know her"). A hidden trait
  shows as unknown, never as a wrong value.

### The other topics

News of the family, an opinion, gossip about a third person, a dinner, and
flirting. They spend an attempt or a diary evening, and buy **knowledge** and
**rapport**, not a vote.

- **OPINION:** this is where knowledge is earned. Without it, the only way to
  learn a person is to fail at levers in front of them, and the knowledge band
  in [[bands-v5.2]] would measure luck.
- **Gossip about a third person** writes the president's knowledge of that
  third person, never an edge between the two others (see conflict C1). It can
  also be false: gossip is the cheapest place a lie enters the game.
- **Flirting** is the one road into the secret affair. It is offered only where
  the content allows it, and the author's likeness call applies: nobody is
  excluded ([[v5-reflection]] §0, Likeness).

### Phone calls

| Decision | Costs | Buys | Versus an audience |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Call** | no diary days; at most one call per person per month (proposed) | one lever attempt, or one topic | lower odds for the same lever; no knowledge gained beyond the call's topic |
| **Take an incoming call** | the clock holds while it lasts | the caller's ask, at once | lighter: the caller expects less |

- **OPINION:** calls exist so that time stays scarce without being a wall. They
  are the cheap option, not the strong one, so they must stay weaker than an
  audience for the same lever.

### Corruption, in the author's four currencies (V5.2c)

The author's call: corruption "pays in all four: the leader's personal wealth,
party and campaign money, diverted state budget, favours" ([[v5-reflection]]
§0). Each is offered or asked for in a scene, and each leaves evidence.

| Currency | Who offers or asks | Buys | Evidence it leaves | Exposed through |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Personal wealth** | an executive or a press owner offers it to the president | the president's personal wealth; a want served in exchange | a money trail | **the press** (an outlet whose owner is hostile), **the courts** |
| **Party and campaign money** | a donor offers it to the party | party money, which a campaign can spend | the party's accounts | **the auditors**: the `party_finance_case` class of scandal (`data/events/scandals.json:195`) |
| **Diverted budget** | the president diverts a budget line | money for a want or an ally, off the books | a gap in a spending line | **the auditors** and **the courts**; the largest evidence of the four |
| **Favours** | anyone; the cheapest to give | a want served, an office, a contract | the least: a person who knows | **a turned person**: if the relationship turns, a rival or an enemy talks |

- **Four exposure routes, named once:** the press, the courts, the auditors,
  and a turned person. [[bands-v5.2]] B6 bands all four.
- **OPINION:** the currencies are ordered by how much evidence they leave, from
  favours (the least) to diverted budget (the most), and by how much they buy,
  in the same order. That is what makes each a choice rather than a ranking.
- **OPINION, the middle of the order — party money below personal wealth:**
  party money passes through an intermediary. The donation lands in the
  party's accounts, where the president can deny knowing, and only the
  auditors' route reaches it. Personal wealth lands in the president's own
  accounts, and both the press and the courts can follow it there. Two routes
  against one, and no deniability: the president's own money is the more
  exposed. Nothing in the game measures this yet; it is a design claim for B6
  to test.
- **Lands on:** exposure arrives as a scandal event through the existing desk
  and hazard machinery ([[events-model]]; `procurement_scandal` and
  `honours_scandal` already exist, [[events-model]] §7, the content table). The seeded
  hazard rises with the evidence held.

### Lies and their detection (V5.2c)

| Decision | Costs | Buys | Caught by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lie** (in an argument, a promise, gossip) | exposure risk; caught, rapport and knowledge collapse, and the person may talk | a lever's odds rise for that attempt | the listener's reading skill against the president's deceiving skill; a later fact that contradicts it (a promised date passes) |
| **Catch a lie** (the person lies to the president) | nothing; it is a read | the president's reading skill against the person's | the president's knowledge of the person |

- A broken dated promise **is** a lie revealed at its date, with no roll. That is
  what makes "promise a date" cost something.

## The dominance matrix

Method, not a 6 × 168 table: for every lever, a state where it is the best
choice and a state where it is the worst, each anchored to the eight traits that
already ship ([[people-model]] §14.2) so the odds read existing constants.

| Lever | Best in (home) | Worst in (hostile) | Beaten at home by | What stops it winning everywhere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Argue | known person, ask aligned with a want | stubborn; stranger (the player picks the wrong argument) | friendship, where a friendship exists | low odds without knowledge |
| Bargain | `pragmatic`, a payable want | `principled`; nothing to pay | argue, when the ask is aligned (free) | it spends a real currency every time |
| Promise a date | `ambitious` with an office want | the date slips; high-skill reader | bargain, when the date is not in the player's hands | a broken date is a revealed lie |
| Pressure | low standing, a dependent person | `networked`, `stubborn` | argue, against anyone with standing | the grudge it writes even on success |
| Flatter | `vain`, low reading skill | `principled`, high skill (it backfires) | everything, on a big ask | small effect, fades |
| Friendship | friend or close, a big ask | below friend (not offered) | nothing at home: that is its point | it spends the relationship |

- **OPINION:** the matrix is a set of claims for the probe to falsify, not a
  proof. [[bands-v5.2]] declares band B5: over the probe grid, no lever is the
  best choice in more than 40 % of cells, every lever is the best in at least
  5 %, and no lever strictly dominates another (0 dominated pairs, the standard
  the author chose for the events in V5.2d, [[v5-reflection]] D15).
- **A phone call is the same matrix at lower odds.** It is in the grid as a
  channel, so a band failure can also be "calls dominate audiences".

## The re-baseline: the table WILL move

- **The author's call (2026-10-21, answer 3): an unanswered request is a
  decline once its window passes.** A balance regime answers nothing, so in
  every regime each request to the played nation's president lapses into a
  decline. Each decline writes a grudge pulse and a rapport loss, and grudges
  feed the want ladder's briefs, which price `bloc_offset` through the applier
  ([[people-model]] §14.5-14.6). **So the 51-regime table may move** on the
  played nation's rows, in every regime, not only in hands-off.
- **INFERRED, which rows can and cannot move.** A lapse alone writes a grudge
  and a rapport loss, and no vote reads either; a vote moves only once a
  lapse-driven act (a brief, a defection, a resignation, a challenge) prices
  `bloc_offset`. So:
  - a row cannot move if its election resolves before the first act a lapse
    can cause: before the first request's window closes, and before the grudge
    it writes crosses the brief's threshold;
  - a row can move after that, and the further into the run, the more acts
    have fired;
  - an AI nation's row can move only through what the played nation's changes
    do to it (below).
- **The campaign channel (C6) moves the campaign regimes too.** If a campaign
  draws party money before the state line, `campaign_bill_pct_gdp` changes
  ([[campaign-model]] §6), and with it the deficit and the vote. The
  `managed_campaigns_*` regimes move through that channel as well as through
  lapses; the prediction must name both. (Superseded by
  [[v5.2-c6-party-campaigns]]: the state line is retired, and B9 in
  [[bands-v5.2]] declares what moves.)
- **Therefore, at build time:** Systems writes the PREDICTED re-baseline before
  it runs (which rows, which direction, how far), measures it, and the author
  accepts it before the merge, as at V5.1-0d. Band B3 bounds how far the
  hands-off France 2027 margin may move ([[bands-v5.2]]).
- **INFERRED, what stays still:** the AI nations book no audiences (C12) and
  receive no requests (answer 1 covers the played nation's people only), so an
  AI nation's own rows move only through what the played nation's moves do to
  them.

## The author's answers (2026-10-21)

The four questions this spec put to the author, relayed by the parent session;
the author took the recommended option on all four.

1. **Registers for whom:** the played nation's people, plus the foreign heads of
   state and government the president meets. (The alternative was all 2 147.)
2. **Lever attempts per audience:** three. (The alternative was unlimited, with
   each failure priced.)
3. **An unanswered request is a decline** once its window passes. The hands-off
   regimes move, so Systems predicts the re-baseline at build time and the
   author accepts it (above). (The alternative was a free lapse, which would
   have made silence the dominant answer.)
4. **Party money is its own register**, a delta over an authored start (C6).
   (The alternative was to fold it into personal wealth.)

## Pre-mortem: "V5.2's play gate scored 1/5. Why?"

Five causes, each with what in this spec prevents it.

1. **Nobody talked again.** The request generator rode the want ladder's edge
   trigger, which by design fires 0 acts in an idle term ([[people-model]]
   §14.6, P1: "0 acts in an idle 168-month term"). A hands-off first term was
   silent, and Q1 failed exactly as in V5.0. **Prevented by:** a request
   trigger of its own (an alive, unsatisfied want asks on a cadence; a
   programme choice invites its sponsors; an office want asks at a reshuffle),
   and band B1 measured on an **idle** term, with the first request by month 3.
2. **The requests arrived, and the desk swallowed them.** Four desk slots, a
   protected card column and about 12 events a term
   ([[v5-reflection]] D16) left no room, so requests were dropped or buried.
   **Prevented by:** C9, the inbox lane as a V5.1c dependency, and B1 counted
   at **arrival in the inbox**, not at generation.
3. **"I cannot book anyone, it's broken."** Occupancy refused the bookings
   without saying why, the R5 shape (PR #42's audit). **Prevented by:**
   delegation and decline on every request card, a refusal that names the
   clash, and band B2 on the share of requests the diary cannot place.
4. **"Je ne sais pas."** The ranges were so wide that no lever was a choice, or
   the player never learned anyone, so Q4 had no answer. **Prevented by:** the
   other topics as the place knowledge is earned, the range's printed reason,
   and band B4's width bounds and growth rate.
5. **Refusing cost nothing anyone could see.** A decline wrote a grudge no
   panel showed, so Q2 ("what refusing cost") was unanswerable.
   **Prevented by:** band B3: every decline writes a priced change on the
   person's card, and a decline-everything term produces at least one act from
   a person who was declined.

**OPINION (a sixth, below the five):** the templated lines read as the narrator
the 102 events already speak in. V5.2d's speakers and voices are the answer, and
the play gate is the only test of it.
