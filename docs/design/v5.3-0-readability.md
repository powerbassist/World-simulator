# V5.3-0 — readability and feeling

The last four play gates scored 1/5, 2/5, 2/5 and 2/5. The closing gate of V5.2 said "2/5, déroutant", then "le jeu est toujours illisible" ([[v5]], "V5.2 — the close-out"). At V5.3's plan gate (2026-10-30) the author named what loses him, through the question tool: "Trop de texte", "Je ne sais pas quoi faire", "Ça manque d'émotion", and NOT "Je ne sais pas où regarder". He took the order "Lisible d'abord, puis le reste", the approach "Chef de cabinet + kit", and three answers on this block's three sections:

- **A, the chief of staff:** "Oui, mais il pause le jeu". The monthly briefing holds the clock: about 12 stops a year, an exception he chose to the rule of at most 4 uninvited stops and 6 holding cards a year ([[decision-surfaces]], "The author's answers", 1). The other holds keep their budget.
- **B, the text diet:** "Oui".
- **C, the emotion kit:** "Oui", without generated portrait expressions.

This note is the design. Interface, Systems and Content build from it after the author has read it. The parent's brief for the block is `.superpowers/sdd/v5.3-brief.md` (gitignored); the gate reports quoted below are `.superpowers/sdd/v5.2-gateb-report.md` and `.superpowers/sdd/v5.2-gate-close-report.md`.

## 2. A — the chief of staff

### 2.1 Who they are

- **One person record per playable nation** (DEU, FRA, GBR, POL: the four nations with `"playable": true` in `data/nations/`), in `data/people/<NAT>.json`, with a new kind, `chief_of_staff`: a name, a portrait, a voice archetype, traits, no wants, no party. They are invented people, not real officeholders. (OPINION)
- **The appointment.** The chief serves the played OFFICE, not the person in it: the played leader's office, as the nation's political-system template defines it (a president, or a head of government), never a per-nation rule. Appointed at the game's start, read from data, so block 0 needs no save field and no bump: the chief is "the person of kind `chief_of_staff` in the played nation's file". (OPINION)
- **A reshuffle does not touch them.** The chief is the president's staff, not a minister: `ns.cabinet` never holds them, cohabitation does not remove them, and a change of the played leader keeps them. Replacing the chief is a verb of [[v5.3-skills]]'s build (their **administration** skill is what makes the briefing better or worse later), and the field that remembers a replacement comes with that verb, `absent_ok` (absent reads as the authored chief, AGENTS.md's rule for a bump). Block 0 ships no dismissal verb. (OPINION; call 5)
- **They must move no number.** The code walks `loader.people` at 12 call sites in ten files (MEASURED): seven in `src/sim` (`audiences.gd`, `audience_triggers.gd`, `characters.gd`, `companies.gd`, `exposure.gd`, `party_money.gd`, `speakers.gd`) and three in `src/core` (`game_state.gd`, `loaders/people_loader.gd`, `loaders/companies_loader.gd`). A person with no wants and no party cannot ask (`audiences.gd:291-301` reads `_unserved`), cannot defect or challenge, and holds no grudge, so today's rules already keep them out (INFERRED). That is not enough to trust: `Characters.init_nation` seeds standing, loyalty and grudge keys for every person (`characters.gd:57-70`), and a later walker could pick them by kind. So the build adds **one general rule by kind**: a kind listed in a `KIND_STAFF` constant (beside `KIND_COLLECTIVE` in `src/sim/character_constants.gd:43`) is skipped by every walker that can write a number, and the people loader accepts the new kind. Never an id check. The proof is the D10-style gate: `tools/balance_parallel.py`'s table `cmp`-identical to the base (band RF8).
- **Why one more person need not shift a draw (MEASURED):** the request draw is a hash of each person's own id, salt and month (`Cycle.unit_pair(person.id, ...)`, `audiences.gd:300`), not a sequence one stream hands out in turn, so a new id leaves every other draw where it was. Systems checks the other walkers for a draw or a sum over the whole list before the build.
- **If the `cmp` fails anyway** (GLM's finding 1), the build stops and Systems names the walker that moved. The fallback is to keep the chief out of `loader.people` altogether, in a file of the nation's staff that only the UI and V5.3's skills read; a moved number is never accepted without the author's word before the merge.

### 2.2 The monthly briefing, and the other holds

**What it is:** a card at the start of each month, on the SCENE layer of the screen stack, that holds the clock until the player dismisses it. Its parts, top to bottom:

1. the chief's face (with the mood badge, section 4.2) and one voiced line (2.6), **shown as text** under the face: `voice.gd` plays only a line on screen, and the line counts in the briefing's word ceiling (3.5);
2. **three priorities now** (2.3), each one line with an icon, a deadline chip and a **Go** button (2.4);
3. **what your choices did** last month (2.5): up to three lines, each the change a register booked, or a figure's movement marked "uncertain" with a small sparkline;
4. **the closing button.** It reads **Continue** when nothing else holds the clock. When another surface still holds it (an interrupt, a held card, the programme offer), it reads **Next: [that surface]** and opens it, so dismissing the briefing never leaves the player before a clock that does not move. (GLM's finding 5)

Keys: Enter or Space presses the closing button, 1 / 2 / 3 is that priority's Go, Escape is the closing button too (the SCENE layer's cancel).

**When it shows.** At the end of `_finish_month()` (`src/ui/main.gd:1209`), after the election result branch and `_enforce_term_start_programme()`, for the same reason those two are ordered: one pause per tick, read from the state the tick settled.

**How it coexists with the other holds the same month.** Every arrival in today's game lands at the month boundary: event cards arrive in the Events step, the lapse guard reads the last month, the election result and the programme offer are read in `_finish_month()` (MEASURED: `src/sim/world_month.gd:93-100`, the `STEPS` order; [[decision-surfaces]], "The surfaces that exist today"). So the briefing and the other holds meet at the same moment, and the rule is an order, not a merge:

| Order | Surface | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Election result | Unchanged. It owns the pause until dismissed; the briefing waits for its `dismissed`, as the front page already does (`front_page.gd`, the election-night hold). If the player lost, there is no briefing |
| 2 | The monthly briefing | Opens. Its priority 1 is the most urgent held surface of this boundary (an interrupt, a major card, a minor card in its last month, the term-start programme offer), whose Go opens it |
| 3 | The term-start programme offer | Unchanged: it holds until chosen or declined. On a term's first month the briefing names it as a priority |
| 4 | Interrupt crisis, holding event cards | Unchanged. **The briefing never releases them:** when it closes, the clock stays held for as long as `_decision_holds_clock()` (`main.gd:1426`) says so, its closing button reads "Next: ..." and opens the card, and `hold_reason.gd` names the cause, as today |
| — | Front page | Unchanged: a note that never holds. It shows once the briefing is dismissed (it already waits for a plate's `dismissed`), and its LIFE_SECONDS run on the running clock |
| — | Requests, notes | Never hold, unchanged |

- **The counting rule** (band RF4): the briefing is counted on its own, **12 holds a year**, outside the 4 + 6 budget, as the author chose. The other holds keep their budget and are counted as today. The probe also reports **stops** (the distinct moments the clock stops) and **dismissals per stop**, because a boundary with a briefing, a programme offer and a major card is one stop but three surfaces to get through: counting it as one stop is the designer's arithmetic, not the player's (GLM's finding 5). RF4 puts a ceiling on that number. (OPINION)
- **The price, said once (MEASURED):** at speed 3 a game month runs in about 4.3 seconds (`src/core/clock.gd:12`, 7 days a second), so a monthly hold is a stop every 4.3 seconds of running at speed 3; at the author's pace of about 74 seconds a game month ([[bands-v5.2]], "The frame") it is one stop every 74 seconds. The author chose the stop; call 2 asks whether a setting may soften it.

### 2.3 The three priorities: the ranking rule

The ranking is a pure, read-only query over existing state, in `src/sim` (it reads GameState and writes nothing, so a headless test runs it; AGENTS.md rule 2). Proposed: `src/sim/priorities.gd`, with its constants in `src/sim/priorities_constants.gd`. It returns exactly three entries `{kind, subject, deadline_month, score, go}`.

**Fog first.** A priority reads only what the player can already see: never an armed event (`ns.event_armed`, `events.gd:149`), never `exposure_evidence`, never a grudge under the band that shows a word (`feelings.gd`), and bloc approval only as the polls show it (the Society panel's reading through `PollNoise`, never the true value). A briefing that names a hidden threat is a leak of the fog. (OPINION; band RF2's test pins it)

**The candidates** (each read from a register that exists today, MEASURED by name):

| Kind | Read | Deadline | Weight W |
|---|---|---|---:|
| `crisis` | a desk entry with `interrupt` true or `severity` 3 (`events.gd:544-551`) | its `months_left` | 10 |
| `election` | `next_election_year/month`, or the EP election, when 6 months away or less | the election month | 9 |
| `decision` | a desk entry of severity 1-2 | its `months_left` | 8 (sev 2), 6 (sev 1) |
| `bill` | `ns.tabled_bill` (`vote_month`, `vote_day`, `legislature.gd:1366`) or a statute draft's `months_left` | the vote | 8 |
| `majority` | the governing seats under the majority mark the Politics seat bar draws, or a censure the template allows | none (trend) | 8 |
| `commitment` | `ns.commitments` with a `due_month` (promises, delegations; `levers.gd:312`) | `due_month` | 7 |
| `request` | an own request in `ns.audience_requests` (`deadline_month`, `audiences.gd:370`) | `deadline_month` | 6 |
| `programme` | the term's programme entry with a deadline (`src/sim/programme.gd`) | its deadline month | 6 |
| `bloc` | a bloc's polled approval moved by more than twice the poll's margin (the Society panel states "about 3 points", so more than 6) between the mean of the last 3 months and the mean of the 3 before; a mean, because a 5-point move between two noisy readings is often the noise (GLM's finding 10) | none (trend) | 5 |
| `budget` | the deficit worse by 0.5 pt of GDP or more against 12 months before, from `state.history` | none (trend) | 5 |
| `unrest` | `unrest_pct` rising over 3 months | none (trend) | 5 |
| `person` | a person whose feeling is "hostile, may leak" (`feelings.gd`, grudge band from 0.45) | none | 4 |
| **standing** | the next election or the term's end; the weakest polled bloc; the budget balance | always present | 1 |

**The score:** `score = W × U × M × N`.

- **U, urgency,** from months to the deadline `d`: 1.0 when `d ≤ 1`, 0.7 when `d ≤ 3`, 0.4 when `d ≤ 6`, 0.2 beyond. A trend has no deadline: U = 0.7 in the month it first qualifies, 0.4 after.
- **M, magnitude,** in [0.5, 1.5]: a desk entry's severity (0.75, 1.0, 1.5); a request's person standing (0.5 + standing); a bloc's move in points / 5; the deficit's move in points / 1; 1.0 for the rest.
- **N, novelty:** 0.6 when the same kind and subject was in last month's briefing and neither U nor M rose; 1.0 otherwise. **N never applies to a deadline 3 months away or less** (a dated kind with U ≥ 0.7; a trend's onset month is not a deadline): novelty is there to stop repetition, and it must never bury a request about to lapse or a crisis the player cannot solve in one month (GLM's finding 3). Last month's three are handed in by the caller (the UI keeps them as presentation memory, not GameState), so the first briefing after a load has N = 1 everywhere. (OPINION)

**The rules that make it three good lines:**

- **Held surfaces take their slots first.** Every surface that holds the clock at this boundary (an interrupt, a major or critical card, a minor card in its last month, the term-start programme offer) is a priority before the score is read, in that order; the score fills what is left. Held surfaces fold by kind like the rest: two held decision cards are one `decision` line, the more urgent named and "+1 more decision" beside it, so the three lines stay three kinds; if held kinds alone fill three lines, the third folds the rest. Without this, a minor card in its last month (6 × 1.0 × 0.75 = 4.5) loses to an election three months away (9 × 0.7 = 6.3), and the briefing names three things while the clock waits on a fourth (GLM's finding 2).
- **Exactly three, always.** The standing kinds exist in every state of every playable nation (an election date or a term end, a weakest bloc, a budget), and they carry W = 1, so they only fill slots nothing urgent wants. A nation with no election (a template whose `lose_condition` is not a vote) gets `unrest` as its standing read instead. Band RF1 measures it over the four playable nations.
- **A quiet month says so.** When only standing kinds fill the three, the card's header is "A quiet month", the voiced line is a calm one, and the standing reads rotate (N applies to them as to any line, so a filler shown last month gives way to another; the no-damping rule still holds an election 3 months away or less in place), so a quiet stretch does not print the same three lines every month (GLM's finding 4). The author chose a monthly hold; call 2 asks whether a quiet month may skip it.
- **One line per kind.** Two requests lapsing show the sooner one, with "+1 more" on its line; the three lines are three different kinds. (OPINION: the red-team's "the same three things every month" is first a "three of the same thing" problem)
- **Ties,** in order: higher W, then the sooner deadline, then the kind's row order in the table above, then the subject id ascending. The last is a stable sort key, not a special case: no rule ever names an id.
- **The constants are tuning:** the weights, the urgency steps and the trend cuts live in `priorities_constants.gd`, and RF2 is the band they are tuned to.

A Go whose target has no such control fails, which is what "Go to a dead end" means as a test.

### 2.5 What your choices did

The honest rule: **a line says what a choice did only when a register booked that number as that choice's own effect.** Everything else is shown, but marked "uncertain". The code already took this line once: `effects_ledger.gd` pools a month's decisions into one entry because a month's numbers cannot tell them apart (its own comment, QA on #69). Block 0 keeps that line and adds the booked numbers beside it.

| Class | What it is | Where it is read (MEASURED) | How it is shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BOOKED** | the number the sim wrote as the choice's own price at the time | a law's channels for the passage stamped last month (`law_ledger[id].months`: the deltas the passage wrote, `legislature.gd:819-834`); a commitment judged last month (`commitments_judged`: kept or broken); a decline or a lapse last month (`report["audience_requests"]["lapsed"]`, priced by `Audiences.decline_price`, `audiences.gd:119`); an action's authored `bloc_offset` (campaign, diplomatic, company actions) | **the booked change in the register's own terms**, never a measurement it does not hold: a law's channel as its direction and a size word by band ("workers: clearly against, booked to the pension law"), the figure only in the tooltip, as `feelings.gd` does for relations (gate b R11: "human relations are feelings, not grudge plus or minus 0.17"; a raw `bloc_offset` delta in [-1, 1] is that same number); a tax or spending channel as its figure, which the player already reads in points; a person's feeling as its words ("Turcy: annoyed → resentful"); a commitment as its verdict ("kept"). An arrow in a status tone from the player's side (GOOD up for approval, BAD up for the deficit) and the result's tone word (4.1); no sparkline, since nothing was measured over time (GLM's finding 6) |
| **UNCERTAIN** | a national figure's movement since the month's decisions | the pooled entry (`recent_effects[0].movements`, `Trends.movements_between`) | before → after with the sparkline, the arrow in a NEUTRAL tone, never green or red, the word "uncertain" and a tooltip "many causes move this figure" |

- **At most three lines,** BOOKED first by size, then UNCERTAIN; "Nothing you decided last month" when there is none, followed by the month's biggest movement as UNCERTAIN.
- **The sparkline** (UNCERTAIN lines only) is the figure over the last 6 to 12 months of `state.history`, through `chart_line.gd`, with last month marked.
- **A person's act after a decline** (a brief, a leak) is not BOOKED today: the act names the person (`characters.gd:443`), but the grudge it fired on is a sum of every source and carries no cause. Until Systems stamps a cause, such a line is UNCERTAIN. **Proposal for Systems (optional):** a `cause` key on the month report's act records and on the decline and lever writes, report-only (not saved, moves no number), so a later block can show it as LINKED.

### 2.6 The chief's voiced line

- **Situations:** calm, tense, crisis, picked from the three priorities: **crisis** when one of them is of kind `crisis`; **tense** when one is a held surface, or has W ≥ 6 and a deadline 3 months away or less; **calm** otherwise. An election is not a crisis: it plays tense in its last 3 months, and the music has its own campaign band for it (4.3). Reading the weight alone would play the crisis line for six months before every election (W 9).
- **Count:** 3 variants per situation, 9 lines per chief, 36 lines for the four chiefs. The variant rotates by month index, so the same line never plays two months running; at 12 briefings a year each line is heard about 1.3 times a year (12 / 9). (OPINION)
- **Characters:** about 70 characters a line, about 2 500 characters for the 36 (INFERRED from the existing lines' length, 51 characters for `activist_f_30/agree` in the manifest).
- **Voices:** the Starter plan's 10 custom slots are spent ("10 créées + 4 toutes prêtes", [[v5]], "Measured"), and a premade voice takes no slot ([[v5]], "What changed how the project works"). So each chief speaks in **a premade voice of their own**, not one of the 14 archetypes: the archetypes already speak for the event speakers and the cast, and the one person heard twelve times a year must not sound like a passing activist (GLM's finding 14; the author's "Une par visage"). The other route, an existing archetype, is call 1's (b).
- **Language:** English. `voice.gd` plays a clip only when its hash matches the shown line, and the shown line is the English msgid; the voice-follows-the-nation rule (R16, "En V5.3") changes that rule after block 0, and re-records then. Call 3.
- **Credits:** measured before the batch by the pipeline's own subscription read (`voice_pipeline.py:295`, `character_limit − character_count`), never assumed. The last read was 24 578 left (2026-10-30); the Starter allowance renews monthly (30 000 a month as third-party pricing summaries report it; not read on the vendor's page). The batch runs only on the parent's GO.

## 3. B — the text diet

### 3.1 The unit, and why it changes

V5.2-0b cut **system sentence words** (nodes of 8+ words), and it worked: on today's main only Orgs (89), Economy (40), Campaign (24) and Agenda (23) keep 20 or more; every other tab is under 20 (MEASURED below). The author still says "Trop de texte". So what remains is not sentences: it is **words on short labels, buttons and table cells, and authored text shown whole**, which the sys 8+ unit cannot see. Block 0's unit is therefore **every visible word** on a tab: system words of any length, content words and button words (the sys 8+ ceilings stay as they are). For a table, the count of text nodes is reported beside it, because a table's weight is its density.

### 4.3 Music by tension, and stingers

**The behaviour** (OPINION):
- **Four music beds:** calm, tense, crisis, campaign (the 3 months before an election). The band is the briefing's situation (2.6): calm, tense or crisis, and the election countdown picks campaign.
- **Against fatigue:**
  - **up at once, down slowly:** a move to a tenser band (calm to tense, anything to crisis, the campaign's start) switches at the next briefing; a move back to a calmer band waits for two consecutive months in it. Hysteresis on both sides would lag the emotion it exists for: a crisis resolved in a month would never be heard (GLM's finding 13);
  - a change crossfades over 4 seconds;
  - each bed is at least 2 minutes and loops seamlessly;
  - after two plays through, the music rests for 60 seconds under the ambient bed alone;
