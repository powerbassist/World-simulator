## 1. What exists (MEASURED)

**Industry.**
- A firm verb runs in one call and lands at once. `Companies.execute()` (`src/sim/companies.gd:801`) prices a nationalisation as `(stake − held) × value` and sets `state_share` (`:1002-1005`), then adds the price to `debt_eur_bn` the same instant (`:1021`). `break_up` marks the firm failed and hands its share to the sector's other majors in the same call (`:942-985`).
- `full_nationalisation` and `privatisation` already go through a law (`requires_law`, `data/company-actions/ownership.json`). So the "instant" the author saw is the apply after the vote, with no transition behind it. `minority_stake` (35 %) has no gate at all, and neither has `break_up` (`data/company-actions/breakup.json`).
- `break_up` refuses with a reason when no other major of the sector can absorb the split (`companies.gd:971`). That is very likely G1's "it worked once and dont work for next company" (INFERRED: the second firm was the last major of its sector, or under the 10 % share gate). The panel prints the reason only after the press (`src/ui/companies_panel.gd:529-541`), and nothing on the row says before the press that the verb can fail.
- A dividend starts the month after a nationalisation (`src/sim/budget.gd:84`). `DIVIDEND_YIELD` is 0.0312 of output a year and a firm is worth `PRICE_TO_OUTPUT` (1.00) times its output (`companies_constants.gd:397`, `:323`).
- **What the tab looks like (a design critique of `companies_panel.gd`).** The first thing the eye meets in each sector is a heading of four figures joined by dots, "%s · %.0f bn · %.0f %% named · regulated 0.12" (`:316`). Under it come a programme line, "BODY · 80 firms · 0.58 of sector · 1 234 567 jobs" (`:361`), five caption lines of body firms, then each major as a name, a status, a figures caption, a worth caption and a row of verb buttons (`:439-514`). No line says what the player can do here or why. The verbs carry their effects in a tooltip only. The panel measured 287 words against a target of 145 ([[v5.3-0-readability]] 3.2). The usability finding is the author's own: the only things to act on are buttons whose consequences are hidden, and one of them destroys a firm with no confirmation.

**Budget and tax.**
- The Budget tab already shows a "now" and a "proposed" column for every line, in % of GDP and in EUR bn, from `Budget.preview(state, pending)` on a copy of the state (`src/ui/budget_panel.gd:500-553`, `_pct_and_eur`). What it does not show is the **difference**: the player has to subtract two columns of percentages in their head, and nothing is green or red.
- The Tax Code tab moves rates with sliders and commits through the same `Budget.commit()` (`src/ui/tax_panel.gd:360-363`). It shows no yield per row; its only estimate is a new tax's "Would yield %s%% of GDP at birth" (`:484`). A rate change shows up only in the Budget tab, if that tab is open.
- The 24-month forecast runs off the frame and is exact, not an estimate (`src/ui/budget_forecast.gd:1-24`).

**Regions.** The Territory tab ranks regions and opens one with its figures, the player's sites (each with a Go to the tab whose verbs act on them) and its three largest cities (`src/ui/region_panel.gd:1-17`). There is no verb inside. A region's economy moves through the sites placed there and the network's access (`src/sim/regions.gd:98-200`), and both are decided elsewhere (Public Works, Energy).

**Audiences.** A request asks to see the president; the player books a day, delegates or declines; on the day a scene offers three attempts, each a lever (argue, bargain, promise a date, pressure, flatter, friendship) or a topic, with the odds shown as a range ([[audiences-model]] 5-6). What a success buys is that "the request settles without the decline price, loyalty +0.05 and a grudge's relief of 0.05" ([[audiences-model]] 5). Requests are booked from a column beside the Diary's grid (`src/ui/diary_requests.gd:1-12`) or from the inbox's REQUESTS lane (`src/ui/request_lane.gd:1-15`).

**Opinions of the president.** `person_rapport` reads 0 for anyone not in the register (`src/sim/president.gd:78-79`), and nothing seeds it at a new game. So every person starts "neutral", the band [-0.05, 0.05] (`src/ui/feelings.gd`). Rapport is read by the lever odds (`K_RAPPORT` 0.20, `lever_constants.gd:23`), by the speakers (`speakers.gd:55`), by the hostile-press count (`exposure.gd:140`, rapport below 0) and by the rival bond (`shadows.gd:153`: below `RIVAL_RAPPORT` −0.30 a person turns rival, or enemy with a grudge).
- France has 63 people; **27 have a party** (renouveau_civique 5, gauche_populaire 6, union_conservatrice 5, front_souverainiste 5, parti_social_uni 4, alliance_verte 2) and 36 do not (press owners 7, judges 6, executives 6, activists 4, union leaders 3, ...) (`data/people/FRA.json`). No press owner has a party.

**Divisions and polls.** `Legislature.resolve_vote()` (`src/sim/legislature.gd:615`) reads each party's support for a law from ideology and the cabinet, plus a defaulted `support_bonus`; it does not read public opinion on the measure, nor the measure's size. A grep finds 22 call sites with comments included (the tier says about 25). Polls exist on a named bill only, two kinds, bought once and fixed (`src/sim/statute.gd:416`, `statute_constants.gd:84`).

**Density.** `tools/decision_surfaces_probe.gd` counts holds and stops in an idle and an active regime (`--briefing`), not decisions offered. In 24 idle months, 6 event decisions reached France ([[decision-surfaces]]). No probe counts how often the player has something to decide.

## 2. The decision grammar (b's core, which a uses)

One rule for every decision in the game, so the player learns it once (OPINION).

### 2.1 The effects strip: what the player reads before acting

Every verb, before it is committed, shows a strip of **at most three lines**:

1. **Money:** the effect on the public balance in EUR bn a year, signed, GOOD green when the balance improves and BAD red when it worsens (the status tones, never the accent: AGENTS.md's two kinds of colour). A one-off price is shown as "−4.2 bn once" and its yearly carry beside it ("then +0.13 bn/yr"). This is G4's rule, generalised to every verb.
2. **Who it moves:** at most three arrows, the blocs or people most moved, as words and arrows ("workers ↑, business ↓↓"), from the same numbers the sim will write (the authored `bloc_offset`, a law's position). A feeling word for a person ("Valcourt: wary → cold").
3. **When and what can go wrong:** the duration ("6 to 9 months"), the next decision it will bring, and the risk in one clause ("the assembly may refuse it", "the firm can contest").

A verb whose strip cannot be filled from the sim's own numbers is not offered (OPINION: this is the a-rule "a sector that moves nothing is refused" applied to verbs). Each strip reads the sim's own function (`Budget.preview()`, `Companies.value_eur_bn()`, the action's authored effects), never a copy, and `tests/` pins one fixture per verb kind: the strip's money line equals what the commit then books, to the cent, for an exact estimate.

**Ranges.** Today every estimate is exact, because the sim is deterministic on a copy. b's statistics service sets how precise each table is: its quality turns an exact figure into a range that always brackets the truth, the fog grammar the audiences already use ([[audiences-model]] 6). Before that setting exists, the strip shows the point figure. When it lands, the same strip shows "+2.8 to +3.6 bn" and e's tax rows read it.

### 2.2 Three shapes of decision

- **Binary** (yes or no: privatise, address the nation). The strip shows the one effect.
- **Graded** (a level among a few: a minority stake or the whole firm; a programme at 25, 50 or 100 %). The strip shows each grade's effect side by side, so the grades are compared on screen, not by trial.
- **Quantified** (a number: N billion to a region, a rate). The strip updates as the number moves, with the forecast's quiet-frame rule (`budget_forecast.gd`) for anything slow.

### 2.3 Processes: a decision that takes time

A decision that changes ownership, structure or the law **takes time and can be stopped** (OPINION, from G2: "nationalisation is still instant"). A process is a row in one register (`ns.processes`, proposed: an id, a kind, a subject, a stage, `months_left`, the price range and the price fixed so far). It is shown in an **In progress** strip at the top of the tab it belongs to, each row with its stage, the months left and its next decision. Each stage that asks something of the player is a decision arrival (section 7). Stopping a process is a verb with its own strip: what has been spent stays spent, and a stop after a public stage costs a U-turn (a small authored bloc price).

**The save.** The register is `absent_ok`, no bump: an absent register reads "nothing in progress", which is the truth for every older save, since no older save could hold a process (AGENTS.md's rule, "a missing field would read as something wrong"). Pinned by `tests/test_save_load.gd`.

## 3. Part A — sectors (sub-block a)

### 3.1 Spending lines into sectors

The signed scope: `data/domains/` splits each spending line into sectors with allocation shares; each sector moves a named quantity; personnel is derived; service quality is regional.

- **Today (MEASURED):** five spending lines, and each moves one thing. Health and pensions feed society's quality (`society_constants.gd:46`, `QUALITY_KEYS`), education and infrastructure feed trend growth (`economy.gd:370-375`), defence feeds capability (`defence.gd:71`).
- **Proposed (OPINION), 3 to 4 sectors per line, each with a quantity the player can read:**

| Line | Sectors (allocation share at start, France) | The quantity each moves |
|---|---|---|
| Health | hospitals, primary care, prevention | beds per 1 000, waiting weeks, life-years index |
| Education | schools, universities, vocational training | pupils per teacher, graduates a year, skills index into trend growth |
| Infrastructure | transport, energy networks, digital | network access (already read by regions), outages, coverage |
| Defence | personnel, equipment, operations | capability (today's figure), readiness |
| Pensions | the basic pension, early retirement, minimum income | the retirees' living standard, the deficit |

  The shares and the quantities are Content's to source (one row per sector in `data/domains/`, with its source in `REUSE.toml`). The sector that moves nothing is refused at load, measured by a test (the tier's rule).
- **The player's verbs.** Move a share between two sectors of one line (quantified, the strip shows both quantities and the money, which is zero for a pure reallocation); and, with 3.3, give one sector of one line a **regional priority**.
- **What the player reads before acting:** each line opens as a tile (b's "general and sector tiles"): the line's total, its sectors as bars with their quantity and its trend, and a warning mark on a sector whose quantity is in its bad band ("waiting weeks 14, worst in 10 years"). A warning is a decision arrival (section 7).
- **Personnel derived:** staff = the sector's spending ÷ a cost per head (data), shown on the tile and read by c's running costs. Nothing votes on it in a.
- **Sim numbers it moves:** none if every share stays at its start value, because each line's total feeds the same channel as today (a re-split that sums to the line is byte-identical by construction; the band A1 checks it). A moved share moves its sector's quantity and, through it, the existing channel (society quality, trend growth, capability).

### 3.2 Industry as processes (G2)

The author: "industry panel is incomplete, nationalisation is still instant", "industry is really bad", and G1's "destroy instantly an company ? why ? also its instant with no warning".

**The verbs become four processes** (OPINION; the numbers are first proposals, each tunable in `companies_constants.gd`, and each band in section 9 says what must hold):

| Process | Stages and time | Cost | Consequences |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Minority stake (35 %)** | an offer, 2 months of negotiation, then the transfer | value × stake × (1 + a premium of 5 to 15 %), seeded inside the range shown | dividends from the month after the transfer; the authored bloc moves, half at the offer and half at the transfer |
| **Nationalisation (100 %)** | the bill (today's route), then 2 months of the offer, then a **12-month transition** | value × (1 + a premium of 15 to 30 %) | during the transition the firm's output is 4 % below its path (management turnover), then recovers; dividends start after the transition; half the bloc move at the bill, half at the transfer |
| **Break-up** | a referral to the competition authority, an investigation of 9 to 12 months, a ruling, then a remedy of 6 months | legal costs (0.05 bn); no purchase | the firm can contest: the authority rules for the break-up with a probability shown as a range, rising with the firm's share (about 50 % at a 10 % share, 85 % at 30 %); in the remedy 70 % of the firm's jobs move to the rivals and 30 % are lost, so unemployment rises in the firm's city; the bloc moves land at the ruling |
| **Privatisation** | the bill, then 6 months of sale | the windfall is value × (1 − a discount of 0 to 15 %), the discount larger in a downturn (the cycle's own phase) | dividends stop at the sale; the windfall comes off the debt then, not at the vote |

- **Before the press** the row says whether the verb can run: a check list of its conditions in plain words ("needs 10 % of its sector: has 12 %", "needs another large firm to absorb it: none left") and the verb is greyed with the failing line when one fails. This removes G1's second half by design.
- **Every irreversible verb asks once:** a confirm card with the effects strip (the existing `confirm_card.gd`). Break-up and privatisation are irreversible; a stake can be sold.
- **Stopping:** a nationalisation stopped before the vote costs nothing but the U-turn; after the vote and before the transfer, a break fee of 10 % of the premium. A break-up stopped in the investigation costs the legal fees spent.

**New Industry decisions, so the tab has something to do (OPINION):**
- **A plant closure announced.** When a major's output falls by more than a threshold over 6 months, it announces a closure in its city (the firm's live city, `Companies.live_city_of()`). The player answers: rescue it (a subsidy, the strip shows the cost and the jobs kept), find a buyer (a 3-month process with a seeded chance), or let it close (the jobs lost in that region, the bloc moves). Each closure is a decision arrival and lands on the map's region (3.3). Content authors the closure rule's thresholds as data, never per firm.
- **The "protect industry" law** (signed b): a real law beside the regulation that stands in for one today, tagged by sector, with its strip (prices, jobs, trading partners' relations).
- **The existing windows stay** (a takeover bid, a failing major) and take the strip.

**The tab, rebuilt (OPINION, for Interface; the BUREAU grammar):** the In progress strip first; then three sector cards (agriculture, industry, services), each with its output, its trend against the nation's, its jobs and its warning mark; a card opens into its majors as rows, each row a name, two figures (jobs, worth) and **one** "Act" button that opens the firm's verbs with their strips. The body firms fold into one line per sector, opened on click. The tab stays under its prose budget (145 words, [[v5.3-0-readability]] 3.2); the strips live in the opened card, which gets its own ceiling (section 9, A6).

**Sim numbers it moves (INFERRED):** the debt (the premium is new money), the timing of every price and dividend (later than today by the stages), firm output during a transition, unemployment in a broken-up firm's city, and bloc offsets (the same totals, split in time). Every nationalisation measured by `tools/companies_probe.gd` will cost more than today by its premium. The author accepts the re-baseline before the merge.

### 3.3 Regions (G6)

The author: "i dont understand regions panel, only informative but no decisions inside". Three ways (the call is section 11, 3):

- **(a) Recommended (OPINION): two regional verbs in a, and c's plan named.**
  1. **A regional priority** on a sector (from 3.1): "put hospitals first in Hauts-de-France". It shifts a share of that sector's national spending toward the region: the region's service quality rises, the others' falls by the same money. The strip shows the region's quantity before and after and the other regions' loss. One priority per sector at a time, so it is a choice.
  2. **A regional development fund:** N bn a year to one region for 1 to 5 years, a process. It raises the region's growth deviation (the term `regions.gd` already computes from sites, `:98-120`, gets a fund term) and so its jobs; its money is on the budget's projects line. The strip shows the region's unemployment in 24 months, the cost and, since blocs are national, the bloc move scaled by the region's share of the population (INFERRED: no regional opinion register exists).
  - **What the player reads before acting:** the opened region shows, at its top, the region's **one problem** in one line (its worst figure against the nation: "unemployment 11.2 %, 1.5 times the nation's"), then the two verbs with their strips, then the existing sites and cities.
  - **c's plan, so the tab says what is coming:** c (the construction register) adds "build or demolish N units of a type in this region" to the same opened region, under the two verbs; the five programmes move into it; the region's sites list becomes the register's rows for that region. Nothing of c is built in a.
- **(b)** Regions stays read-only until c, with one line in the tab saying what c will bring. Cheapest, and the next gate will very likely say the same thing again (INFERRED from G6).
- **(c)** Drop the Territory tab and put the region's figures and the two verbs of (a) in the box a map click opens (V5.1-d4's country box). Less to read, but the region verbs are then reachable only from the map.

**Sim numbers it moves (INFERRED):** regional GDP and unemployment (a new fund term in the growth deviation), the spending total (the fund's bn), society's quality by region (the priority; a new per-region read of a national figure). A priority alone moves no national total.

## 4. Part B — the decision grammar's verbs (sub-block b)

### 4.1 Effects before commit: the Budget and the Tax Code (G4)

The author: "need estimation for each changes before commit to budget, Xmilliard in green if positive or red if negative when changing a tax rate for example".

- **Beside every lever and every rate,** while it is moved and before Commit: the change in the balance in EUR bn a year, signed and toned (2.1's money line). A rate raised from 20 to 21 % reads "+6.1 bn" in green; a health line raised by 0.5 point reads "−14.6 bn" in red. The figure is `Budget.preview()`'s difference against the committed state for that one key, so it is exact today.
- **The Budget's table gets a third column, "change",** the difference of the two it already prints, signed and toned on the balance's sign (revenue up or spending down is green). The balance row's change is the sum and comes first.
- **The Tax Code shows each row's yield now and its change,** and its own balance line at the top, so a rate move is read in the tab where it is made (today it shows up only in the Budget tab).
- **The 24-month figure** stays the forecast's (exact, off the frame); the strip says "this year" for the preview and "in 2 years" for the forecast once it has settled.
- **No sim number moves:** this is display. The band B1 checks that the shown change equals the booked change.
- **With e:** the same column becomes a range under the statistics service, and the tax route (a rate applied after the amending finance bill) puts a "voted in N weeks" clause on the third line.

### 4.2 Polls on any measure

The signed scope: every tile (a law, a tax change, a construction programme, a nationalisation) can commission a poll on whether the country likes it, per bloc. V4.2's poll on a named bill (`statute.gd:416`) widens its subject to any measure (a measure key: a law id, a draft id, a process id, a tax change), keeps its rule (bought once, fixed, never a reroll) and takes the fog grammar:
- **Three prices** buy three margins: about ±6, ±3 and ±1.5 points per bloc, the dearest the slowest (2, 4, 6 weeks), and the result arrives as an inbox card (a decision arrival when the measure is still open). OPINION: today's two prices (0.00005 and 0.00015 bn) are symbolic, and a poll the player never weighs is not a decision; the prices should read as a real cost against the party's or the state's money, which Systems sizes.
- **What the player reads before buying:** the strip (the price, the weeks, the margin) and what the poll will answer.
- **Sim numbers it moves:** the debt by the price, nothing else.

### 4.3 The address to the nation

The signed scope: on a pending law or decision, the president goes on air. It costs a half-day of the diary; it moves the country's opinion of that measure per bloc, by the president's persuasion ([[v5.3-skills]] 3: × (1 + 0.5 × f(s′))) and standing, through the outlets' leanings ([[media-model]]); the deputies whose voters moved feel it in the division (4.4); each address in a short span is worth less; going public can harden the other side.
- **Proposed numbers (OPINION):** a base shift of 6 points of support among the blocs that lean toward the measure and 3 among the neutral; the blocs against harden by 30 % of the shift the other way; the k-th address within 6 months is worth 0.6^(k−1) of the first. A shift fades over 3 months.
- **What the player reads before acting:** the strip: the measure's support per bloc now (from a poll, or a wide range without one), the estimated shift, the division's forecast before and after ("short by 12 seats → short by 3 to 7"), the diary half-day, and the hardening.
- **Sim numbers it moves:** a new per-measure opinion register (`absent_ok`: no measure has an opinion in an older save), read by the effects-aware division; the diary.

### 4.4 Effects-aware divisions

The author's call ([[v5]], V5.3 b): forty lycées and four hundred must not divide alike. `resolve_vote()` and its 22 call sites (1) get two new terms, read from data and never per law id (OPINION):
- **The size term:** a graded or quantified law carries its magnitude (the units, the EUR bn); a party's support moves away from zero with the magnitude, in the direction its ideology already gives (a party that likes forty lycées likes four hundred more; one that dislikes them dislikes four hundred more), plus a cost term for the parties whose ideology weighs the deficit, which grows with the bn.
- **The opinion term:** the party's support moves by a weight times its voters' support for the measure (4.3's register, 50 meaning neutral), scaled down by its discipline.
- **Every existing call stays source-identical** through the defaulted-parameter route `support_bonus` already took (`legislature.gd:615`'s comment), and a law with no magnitude and no opinion reads exactly today's support (band B4 checks byte identity on the balance table for laws without the new fields).
- **Measured by a legislature probe** (new, Systems): the same law at two magnitudes, and with and without an address.

### 4.5 The rest of b, in one place

- **The 120 laws tagged by sector** (data, Content), so a sector tile lists its laws.
- **The general and sector tiles:** the tile is 3.1's line tile; the general tile is the nation's one-screen summary of the lines, each with its warning mark.
- **The decree route** as a read of the regime field: under a template that allows it, a decision skips the bill stage of its process, and the strip says "by decree".
- **The statistics service's quality** (2.1's ranges): a setting on the budget (its own small line), whose quality sets every range's width; a starved service widens every strip.

## 5. The audience, rethought (G5)

The author: "audience is still unclear, the gameplay of this is déroutant"; "ce qui se passe dans l'audience, ce n'est pas du gameplay tres bien designé; l'agenda est mieux, mais toujours séparé de ka prise de rendez vous".

**Why it confuses (INFERRED from [[audiences-model]] 5-6):** the visitor's ask is a want, not a thing in the game; six levers and four topics are offered at once; the odds are a range; and the win is "the request settles", loyalty +0.05 and a grudge relieved, which nothing on screen turns into something the player feels. The player picks three times among ten buttons for a prize they cannot see.

**One naming point (INFERRED, to check with the author):** in the French build the "Agenda" domain is called "Feuille de route" and holds the programme tab and the "Calendrier" (the Diary) (`data/i18n/fr/ui.json`; `domains.gd:21`; `main.gd:317`, `:462`). "L'agenda" in his note is most likely the Calendrier grid. His "prise de rendez-vous" is then the request column beside it and the inbox's REQUESTS lane: two more places to book from.

**In all three options, booking moves into the grid (OPINION):** a waiting request is drawn as a pale chip **on each of its offered days** in the Calendrier's grid, with the face and the deadline; a click on a chip books that day; the request column beside the grid goes; the inbox card only says "X wants to see you" with a Go to the first offered day.

**The three options for the scene:**

1. **A deal on one ask (Recommended, OPINION).** Every request names **one concrete ask** tied to an object of the game (vote for bill X, a subsidy to firm Y, a project in region Z, a post, a pardon) and **one thing they offer** (their vote and their faction's, an endorsement, their silence, their union's calm). The scene is one screen in two columns: what they want, what they give, each with its effects strip (2.1). Three answers: **Grant** (pay the ask, take the offer, the strip says both), **Bargain** (a smaller grant for the same offer, with the odds as a range; the levers fold into three approaches: argue, promise a date, pressure; flattery and friendship become modifiers shown on the approach they help), **Refuse** (the price shown). Small talk (learn about them) stays one click. The outcome is a deal written into the Calendrier ("Valcourt votes for the bill on 12 March; you subsidise Arvel by June"). The lever bands of [[audiences-model]] 8 are re-measured on the three approaches.
2. **A card, not a scene.** On the booked day the audience resolves on a card in the Calendrier: the ask, Grant / Bargain / Refuse with their odds and strips. The scene with portraits and voices is kept only for the few who matter most (party leaders, the cabinet). Fewer clicks; loses much of V5.2's people who talk.
3. **The audience prepares a vote.** Requests come mainly from deputies and leaders before a division (4.4), and the scene is whipping votes: each visitor's vote, what they want for it, the division's forecast moving as deals are made. The Calendrier shows the division day and the audiences before it. Strongly tied to b; narrower (unions, press owners and executives would ask less).

**Sim numbers it moves:** option 1 moves the asks into real registers (a subsidy, a vote line, a project) where today a success moves loyalty and grudge only; option 2 the same with fewer scenes; option 3 the vote lines. Each needs the author's acceptance.

## 6. Opponents start against you (G7)

The author: "toute les personnes on un avis neutral de base, ce qui ne devrait pas etre le cas, mes adversaire politiques devraient avoir une plus mauvaise opinion de moi non ?".

## 7. Density: a meaningful decision every N months (G10)

The author: "pour l'instant il n'y a pas grand chose a faire". At speed 3 a month runs in about 74 seconds ([[decision-surfaces]]), so 30 minutes of play is about 24 game months (INFERRED; slower speeds give fewer).

**A meaningful decision arrival (OPINION), counted by a probe:** in a given month, a decision the player was not offered last month (or whose deadline falls this month) such that:
1. it has at least two answers, including the default, and
2. their effects strips differ by at least one of: 0.5 bn a year, 0.5 point of approval (estimated), one bloc arrow, a person's feeling word, a division's outcome, a process stage; and
3. it is not the same subject offered again within 3 months unless its state changed.

A standing lever (a budget slider that is always there) is not an arrival: it does not tell the player that something is waiting. The chief's priorities point at arrivals and are not counted twice.

**The sources counted:** desk events with a choice; audience requests; the firm windows (a bid, a failing major, a plant closure); a process stage that asks something; a sector or region warning with a verb; a poll result on an open measure; a division where an address is possible; e's budget season.

**The probe (proposed, Systems):** `tools/decision_surfaces_probe.gd --density`, through `WorldMonth.tick_month()`'s step hook as the briefing run does, for DEU, FRA, GBR and POL, 120 months, **both regimes**. The **active** regime is the decisive one: a player who answers everything in month 1 must still find arrivals in month 2, since that is exactly what "pas grand chose à faire" describes. Its first run, on main before any a/b build, is the baseline (not measured yet, section 12).

**The band, N = 1 (recommended; the call is section 11, 6):** in both regimes and all four nations, at least one meaningful arrival in at least 90 % of months, a mean of at least 2 a month, no gap longer than 2 months, at least 3 different domains a quarter, and at most 6 arrivals a month (more is the gauntlet the holds budget forbids, [[decision-surfaces]]). The arrivals that hold the clock stay inside RF4's budget ([[v5.3-0-readability]] 5): most arrive as cards.

## 8. Pre-mortem: the next gate scored 2.5 again. Why?

1. **The strips are there, and still unread.** Three lines on every verb became a wall. Prevented by A6 (each card's word ceiling) and by the strip's three-line cap; RF6's budgets still apply to every tab.
2. **Processes made the game slower, not richer.** A nationalisation that takes 14 months and asks nothing in between is a wait. Prevented by each process stage that asks something being an arrival (section 7) and by the In progress strip saying the next decision.
3. **"Something to do" was met by noise.** The probe counted arrivals the player does not care about. Prevented by the meaningful test (section 7, item 2: strips that differ) and the 3-month repeat rule.
4. **The audience changed shape and lost its people.** Prevented by keeping the faces, the voices and small talk in option 1, and by the author choosing.
5. **Regions got verbs nobody needs.** Prevented by the opened region naming its one problem first, so the verb answers a visible need.

## 11. Calls for the author (in simple French, the recommendation first)

1. **L'industrie : des procédures qui prennent du temps.** (a) *Recommandé :* nationaliser, prendre une part, démanteler et privatiser deviennent des procédures avec un coût, une durée et des conséquences (par exemple 14 mois après le vote et une prime de 15 à 30 % pour une nationalisation, une enquête de 9 à 12 mois pour un démantèlement que l'entreprise peut contester), et chaque étape te demande quelque chose. (b) Des procédures, mais sans étape intermédiaire : tu décides, puis le résultat arrive N mois plus tard. (c) Garder l'instantané, avec seulement un écran de confirmation et l'effet affiché avant.
2. **L'estimation avant de valider (budget et impôts).** (a) *Recommandé :* à côté de chaque curseur, le chiffre en milliards par an, vert si le solde s'améliore, rouge s'il se dégrade, plus une colonne « changement » dans le Budget et le rendement de chaque impôt dans le Code des impôts. (b) Seulement la ligne du solde total, en vert ou rouge, en haut.
3. **Les Régions.** (a) *Recommandé :* deux décisions dès a : une priorité régionale pour un secteur (« les hôpitaux d'abord en Hauts-de-France ») et un fonds de développement régional (N milliards par an pendant 1 à 5 ans), et chaque région ouverte dit d'abord son problème ; la construction arrive avec c au même endroit. (b) Les Régions restent en lecture jusqu'à c, avec une ligne qui annonce ce qui vient. (c) Supprimer l'onglet et mettre les chiffres et les deux décisions dans la boîte qui s'ouvre au clic sur la carte.
4. **L'audience.** Dans les trois cas, les demandes s'affichent directement sur leurs jours dans le Calendrier, et un clic sur le jour prend le rendez-vous. (a) *Recommandé :* un marché sur une seule demande concrète : ce qu'il veut (un vote, une subvention, un projet), ce qu'il donne en échange, et trois réponses : accorder, négocier (trois façons : argumenter, promettre une date, faire pression), refuser, chacune avec son effet affiché. (b) Plus de scène : l'audience se règle sur une carte le jour venu, la scène reste pour les chefs de parti et les ministres. (c) L'audience sert à préparer les votes : surtout des députés avant un scrutin, et la prévision du vote bouge à chaque accord.
5. **Les adversaires au départ.** Les personnes des autres partis commencent méfiantes, d'autant plus que leur parti est loin du tien ; les personnes sans parti restent neutres. (a) *Recommandé :* méfiants, au plus froid juste avant « rival » (−0,28) : personne n'est ton rival dès le premier jour, ça se gagne en jouant. (b) Les partis du cordon (par exemple le Front souverainiste pour toi) commencent froids et rivaux. (c) Selon la distance seule, sans plancher : les plus éloignés commencent rivaux (pour toi, la Gauche populaire et le Front souverainiste).
6. **« Quelque chose à faire » : à quel rythme ?** (a) *Recommandé :* au moins une vraie décision nouvelle chaque mois de jeu (environ toutes les 75 secondes en vitesse 3), deux en moyenne, jamais plus de six, mesuré par une sonde sur les quatre pays jouables. (b) Une tous les deux mois (plus calme). (c) Deux par mois au minimum (plus dense, risque d'avalanche).
