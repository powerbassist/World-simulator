# V5.3 c': the construction register and EU co-financing

**Status: proposed 2026-11-17, not built.** A spec for Systems, Content and Interface, written
by Design from Gameplay's brief of 2026-11-15 and the author's four answers of 2026-11-17.
Read on `main` at `6a4cb0cf`. No code changed and nothing measured: every sim number below
is a stand-in until a probe has run and the author has accepted its table. Scope is the
register and co-financing; the AI's use of the register and the Union's law procedure moved
to 0.6 with D12. It builds on [[v5.3-ab]] (the strip, arrivals, bands A4 and B4),
[[finance-bill]] (the rows a bill carries) and [[decision-surfaces]] (the budget of stops).

The author's ask (gate line R12 and his own words): build N lycées, how many, at what
estimated cost, and where. His four answers:

| Call | His answer | What it fixes here |
|---|---|---|
| Need by region | "Vrais chiffres par secteur" | A region's level is a sourced figure, never derived from demography (section 3). |
| Overruns | "Une question" | At most one ask per programme, its odds shown before the vote (section 5). |
| The Union's money | "Enveloppe qui expire" | An envelope per nation and period, paid at completion, lapsing (section 6). |
| What stands at the start | "Vrais équipements" | Real installations per type and region for the four playable nations (section 2). |

## 1. The unit types

A unit is a dated piece of capacity of one type in one region, kept for good at a running
cost. c' ships the types whose sector already carries a physical figure
(`data/domains/<line>.json`), because that figure is what the region shows before and after.

| Type id | Line, sector | The figure it answers | Capacity unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `upper_secondary_school` | education, `schools` | `pupils_per_teacher` (lower is better) | places |
| `hospital` | health, `hospitals` | `beds_per_1000` (higher is better) | beds |

Not in c', with the reason:

- **A care home.** No elder-care sector exists (the pensions line holds `basic_pension` and
  `minimum_income`), so the unit has no figure to answer. It is the next row of the catalogue
  the day a sector is added; its sources are listed in section 11 so the work is not redone.
- **Roads, rail, grid, fibre.** A length along a route: V5.4 d, through the row of section 10.
- **Energy plants.** They keep their own register (`ns.plants`).
- **The five national projects.** They move into the catalogue as payoff kind `potential`
  (section 8), not as types the player counts.

## 2. Data: what Content fills

Four pieces. A source must give, for each figure, the publisher, the table, the year and the
regional level, in the file's `_source` (the pattern of `data/regions/*.json`).

**The catalogue, `data/units/<type_id>.json`, one file per type.**

| Field | Unit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `id`, `name`, `summary` | text | shown through `Strings` rows |
| `line`, `sector`, `figure` | ids | the domain row the type answers |
| `payoff` | `capacity`, `potential` or `network` | `network` is reserved for V5.4 |
| `capacity` | places or beds per unit | the size of one new unit |
| `figure_elasticity` | signed number | the % the region's figure moves per % of capacity: +1 for beds, -1 for pupils per teacher |
| `cost_eur_m` | EUR million per unit, by nation | the estimate's centre, for each nation that has a stock file |
| `build_months` | months | from the vote to the opening |
| `running_eur_m` | EUR million a year per unit, by nation | joins the line's spending when the unit opens |
| `staff` | jobs per unit | shown, moves nothing (personnel is still backlog) |
| `overrun_odds`, `overrun_share` | 0 to 1 | section 5 |
| `demolish_months`, `demolish_cost_share` | months, share of the unit cost | section 7 |
| `cofinanced` | true or false | section 6 |
| `band_units` | units | the programme size band U5 is measured at |
| `bloc_offset_per_pop_share` | bloc figures | what closing units moves, section 7 |

**The start stock, `data/units/stock/<NAT>.json`.** One row per region and type:
`{region, type, units, capacity}`. `units` is the count of installations and `capacity` their
places or beds, both from an official count, never from a spread of a national total. A nation
with no stock file for a type is not offered that type: one general rule, so the other 37
nations need nothing until they are sourced.

**The regional level, in the same file.** One row per region and figure:
`{region, figure, level}`, the region's own value of the sector figure in the figure's unit
(pupils per teacher, beds per 1 000). The loader stores it relative to the nation's figure,
normalised so the population-weighted mean is 1 (the rule `unemployment_rel` already follows).
The nation's figure in `data/domains` stays the single truth.

**The envelope and the category.**

- `cohesion_category` on each region of `data/regions/<NAT>.json`: `less_developed`,
  `transition`, `transition_former_less_developed` or `more_developed`. The real categories are
  set on smaller regions (NUTS 2). A game region made of several takes the category of the part
  holding most of its people. Four game regions are mixed: Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, Niedersachsen,
  Rheinland-Pfalz and Sachsen.
- `data/units/cofinancing.json`: per organisation, `rates` by category; `periods`, each with
  `id`, `first_year`, `last_year`, `pays_until_year` and `envelope_eur_bn` by nation;
  `warn_months` for the lapse note.

## 3. Stock, level and what a region shows

Let a region hold `C` of capacity of a type at the start (its stock row) and `n` net units
built or closed by the player, each of the catalogue's `capacity`.

- **The region's own gain**: `g = figure_elasticity x n x capacity / C`.
- **Netted, so the nation's figure has one writer**: the region shows `g` less the
  mean of `g` over the nation's regions, each weighted by its people x its level, so the
  regions' mean stays exactly the nation's figure. The built region rises and the
  others give a little back, the rule regional priority and plant closures already follow.
- **The nation's figure** moves only through the units' running cost, which joins the line's
  spending and takes the lever's own path (`Sectors.ratio`). No second channel.
- **What the screen shows**: the nation's figure x the region's level x the priority term x
  (1 + the netted gain).
- **What growth reads**: the change since the start (the priority term and the netted gain),
  never the level. So Content's real levels move no number in a game where nothing is built.

"By need" fills the regions whose shown figure is furthest behind the nation's, in that
order, until N is placed. "By population" follows `region_pop_share`. Both only fill the
rows; the player edits them.

## 4. The price

- **Capital** is a rate on the budget while the programme builds, on the projects' line
  (`project_bill_pct_gdp`), spread evenly over `build_months`.
- **The estimate** is `N x cost_eur_m`, plus the concentration term below.
- **The range** is the estimate x (1 - w) to (1 + w). The width `w` follows the minister of
  the type's line: `w = UNIT_FOG_MAX - (UNIT_FOG_MAX - UNIT_FOG_MIN) x competence`, with a
  vacant seat at the widest. Stand-ins: 0.25 and 0.08.
- **The contract price** is drawn once, when the vote passes, uniformly inside the range
  shown at tabling (`Cycle.unit` on the programme's id). It is inside the range in every run by
  construction, which is band A4's reading.
- **Concentration.** Units ordered in one region within twelve months, beyond
  `UNIT_CONC_FREE` of that region's stock (stand-in 0.05), cost more there: each further point
  of the stock adds `UNIT_CONC_SLOPE` points to the unit price (stand-in 1.0). The row says so
  ("+12 % here: 14 units in one year"). Spreading is cheaper; concentrating is faster where
  it is needed.
- **Running cost** starts at the opening and never stops while the unit stands.
- **The vote.** The programme is a row of the finance bill (`finance_rows.gd` already carries
  `programmes` and `funds`; this adds `units`), read as a rise of its line sized by its cost a
  year. No vote of its own.
- **How many at once**: `UNIT_PROGRAMMES_MAX_OPEN` building programmes (stand-in 4).

## 5. The overrun: one ask

- **Odds, shown before the vote**: `overrun_odds` of the type x (1.5 - competence), kept
  between 0.05 and 0.60, fixed at tabling like the range, so a reshuffle before the vote
  changes neither. Stand-ins: 0.15 for a school, 0.30 for a hospital.
- **Drawn once**, when the vote passes. If it falls, it lands in a seeded month of the middle
  third of the build, as one card. A programme never has a second.
- **Its size**: `overrun_share` of the capital still to pay (stand-ins 0.20 and 0.30).
- **Three answers, three different prices**:
  - *Pay*: the bill rises by the overrun over the months left.
  - *Shrink*: units are dropped until the first budget holds, from each region's row in
    proportion; the regions get less.
  - *Delay*: the overrun is paid, but at the first yearly rate over more months, so the
    year's bill does not rise and the opening moves back by the same share of the build.
    If the new date passes the period's `pays_until_year`, the Union's share is lost
    (section 6), and the card says so.
- **Unanswered at its date**: pay, and a note says why the bill rose.
- The tier's "declared share of runs" is then exact: the realised cost is inside the first
  range in every run with no overrun paid, and the share of those is 1 less the odds shown.

## 6. The Union's envelope

- **Who**: a member of the organisation (`Orgs.is_member`). A non-member has no envelope and
  its strip has no Union line.
- **What**: units of a `cofinanced` type. The Union pays the rate of the region's category on
  the capital of the units built in that region.
- **Reserved at the vote**: the expected share is taken from what is left of the period's
  envelope, so the screen can say "0.4 bn left until 2029". A shrink or an abandon gives it
  back. The payment never exceeds the reservation: a contract price above the estimate and
  an overrun paid are the nation's alone.
- **Paid at completion**: the nation carries all the capital first. The Union's share arrives
  over the twelve months after the opening, as a receipt on the existing transfer line
  (`bloc_transfer_pct_gdp`), capped by the envelope.
- **Lapse**: a programme that opens after `pays_until_year` is paid nothing. What is neither
  paid nor reserved at that date is gone. `warn_months` before it (stand-in 12), a note names
  the sum about to lapse and opens the register. That is the dated reason to come back.
- **The council.** The `cohesion_fund` proposal exists in `data/bloc-proposals`. A field on it,
  `envelope_factor`, may enlarge the current period's envelope when the rule is in force.
  Reserved as a shape; Systems says whether it is cheap enough for c'.

What is real and what is the game's: the rates and the categories are the Union's own
(section 11). The envelope in the game is a declared share of a nation's real allocation,
because social buildings are a small part of what cohesion money pays for. Stand-in: one
tenth, and for the period under way at the start, the years left over seven. A type whose
build cannot end before the period's last payment year shows no Union line for that period,
and the sheet says why; Content checks that at least one type can use the first period.

## 7. Demolish

Closing N units of a type in a region takes `demolish_months`, costs `demolish_cost_share`
of the unit cost once, lowers the stock, and takes the units' `running_eur_m` off the line: the line's spending
falls by that sum at the same setting (the row shows it), and the nation's figure follows by
the lever's path. A unit of the start stock saves the same as a built one. The
region's figure falls by the rule of section 3. One bloc price, `bloc_offset_per_pop_share` x
the region's share of the population. It is a row of the finance bill like a build. It is
worth doing where a region stands well ahead of the nation and the money is needed elsewhere.

## 8. The five projects, re-expressed

Each becomes a catalogue row of payoff `potential` with a quantity of 1, its
`build_months`, its cost as the same rate of output a year and its `potential_growth_pct`.
Its site is its authored point. Its range has no width and its overrun odds are 0, so nothing
about it changes. In c' the projects keep their own register, their saves and their own count rule; they do
not count toward `UNIT_PROGRAMMES_MAX_OPEN`. The register's list is one read over both. The
player's unit rows live in `ns.unit_programmes` (absent reads "none"); the start stock is
data and is never saved.

## 9. The decision test

**One surface.** The register's sheet, opened from a region's sheet, from a sector's tab or
from a warning. The player sets the type, N, the rows per region and tables it.

| # | The player sees | does | the game returns | what it unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | a regional warning: "Hauts-de-France: 19.4 pupils per teacher, the nation 17.9" | Build here, Give it priority, or Leave it | the sheet, with "by need" filled | the composer |
| 1 | the type, N, one row per region with its figure before and after | types N, edits rows | the range, the running cost, the Union's share, the odds | a tabled row |
| 2 | the finance bill with the row | votes with the month | passed or lost; the contract price as a note | the build |
| 3 | perhaps one overrun card | pay, shrink or delay | the new bill, count or date | |
| 4 | the opening | nothing | the region's row keeps its start figure ("19.4 → 17.8 since 2027"); the warning clears; a note gives the realised cost against the estimate; the Union's money arrives | the next programme |

**Is it a decision** (Meier's test). Tradeoff: capital and a running cost for good, against
one region's figure, and the other regions give a little back. Situational: which region is
behind differs by type and by nation, because the levels are real. Short against long: the
line's lever raises every region now for no capital; a priority lifts one region for three
years; a unit lifts it for good. Risk against reward: the overrun odds and the lapse date
stand beside the gain, in money and in the figure's own unit. Persistent: the running cost
never stops.

**Feedback before**, the three-line strip: the money ("2.1 to 2.9 bn over 4 years, then
+0.24 bn a year; the Union pays 1.2 bn at the opening, 0.4 bn left until 2029"); who it moves
(the region's figure before and after, the others' small loss); when and what can go wrong
("opens 2030; one chance in five of an overrun; an opening after 2029 loses the Union's
share"). **Feedback after**: row 4 above.

**Goals.** This month: a warning with its answer one click away. This year: the build's
date, the overrun card's date. The term: the envelope's lapse date and the region's row.

**The budget of 4 + 6.** The vote rides the finance bill: no new stop. The overrun card is
quiet and holds in its last month, so it counts toward the 6: under one a year on average at
the stand-in odds, and never more than two in a year: a third waits for the next January. Regional warnings and the lapse note never hold. A
regional warning is an arrival with three answers; at most one a month, the worst region not
warned in the last twelve months.

**The regional warning.** A region whose shown figure is `UNIT_REGION_BAND` worse than the
nation's (stand-in 0.08) is in band. Systems measures how many regions are in band at the
start with the real levels and sets the band so two to four regions per type and nation are.

## 10. What V5.4 d needs from the row (shape only)

- `quantity` is a number with a unit, not only a count (kilometres).
- `cost` names its source: the catalogue x N, or a figure the caller supplies (relief prices
  a route).
- `where` is a list of `{nation, region, share}`, so a line may cross two nations; a
  `cost_share` per nation that can be negotiated before tabling; a `stage` for the partner's
  answer (`processes.gd`).
- The co-financing rate is keyed by the region's category or by a flag on the type
  (cross-border).
- Every row has a state: planned, building, done, damaged.
- The site is derived on read from geometry, a point or a route, never saved as a region id.
  In c' a unit's place on the map is derived from its region's cities, by their share.

## 12. Bands and probes

Declared before any run. A new probe, `tools/units_probe.gd`, drives the real month
(`WorldMonth.tick_month`); the balance table comes from `tools/balance_parallel.py`.

| Band | Pass rule |
|---|---|
| U1 unused | With Content's data loaded and no programme tabled, the balance table is byte-identical to `main`'s. |
| U2 the five projects | Every regime that builds a project gives the same rows as on `main`, to the byte. |
| U3 the range | The contract price is inside the range shown at tabling in 100 % of 200 seeded runs; the share of runs with an overrun is within 5 points of the odds shown. |
| U4 no dominant "where" | For each playable nation and type, the same N placed by need, by population, all in the largest region, and the same money on the line's lever. No one of the four is best on every read (the worst region's figure, the nation's figure, capital paid, the Union's money received). By need wins the worst region's figure; the lever wins the nation's figure per euro. |
| U5 a visible step | A programme of the type's `band_units` (catalogue; stand-ins: forty schools, three hospitals) placed in one region moves that region's figure by at least 5 % in three quarters of each playable nation's regions. If the real stock makes that false for a type, the step is declared from the measured figure before the author sees it. |
| U6 the stops | A regime that tables a programme every year: overrun cards at most two in any year; regional warnings at most one a month; none of them holds the clock outside the card's last month. |
| U7 the envelope | A regime that never builds: the envelope lapses and no number moves. A regime that opens after the date receives nothing. A regime that opens in time receives the rate of each region's category. |

Every stand-in in this document (the widths, the odds, the shares, the concentration
terms, the band, the envelope's share) is measured by these probes and goes to the author in
one table before a merge.
