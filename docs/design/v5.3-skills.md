# V5.3 — skills: one talent per domain, 0 to 20

**Status: proposed (2026-10-26), docs only.** Nothing here is built. Each band in
section 9 is declared before any build.

The author's ask, verbatim (the question tool, 2026-10-26, while accepting
c1c's numbers):

1. "Le pourcentage de chance de découverte doit venir du talent du dirigeant
   incarné dans ce domaine, l'intrigue non ?"
2. "Je voudrais un vrai talent intrigue et aussi pour les autres domaines
   diplôme ainsi etc j'aimerais bien qu'il y ait aussi des compétences un peu tu
   vois des des compétences notées de 0 et le mets moins que rien et ça peut
   aller jusqu'à 20 par exemple ou ça peut dépasser je sais pas c'est toi qui
   vois". "Diplôme" is a speech-to-text slip for "diplomatie".
3. Placement: "V5.3, conception dès maintenant (Recommended)".

Read as: several skills, one per domain (intrigue, diplomacy and the others),
each rated from 0 ("less than nothing") to 20, perhaps beyond. The scale is
Gameplay's to propose.

**The author's changes to three calls (2026-10-30, verbatim, relayed by the
parent):** "tout le monde a les competences, mais ne les utilise que lorsque a
un poste precis; les competences peuvent etre ameliorées par des formation ou
events; les competences des dirigents inspirés du reel son calqué sur leur
competence dans la vraie vie".
- Everyone carries all five; a skill acts only in an office that uses it
  (section 4).
- Skills grow through training and events (section 5).
- Real-inspired leaders' values follow their real competence, from their
  public political record (section 6).

Labels: **MEASURED** means a cited line, a probe or a count over `data/`.
**INFERRED** means read from the code or derived, but not run. **OPINION**
means the designer's call. Source lines are pinned to `701d28f8`.

## 1. What exists (MEASURED)

**The skill field today.**
- Every person record can carry one `social_skill` in [0, 1], "reading and
  deceiving" ([[people-model]], the V5.2a registers table;
  `src/core/person.gd:63`). An absent value reads 0.5.
- Of **2,149 people in 41 files**, **324** author `social_skill`, in 0.34 to
  0.82, mean 0.60. They split as 269 of the 301 met cast (`bench: false`)
  plus 55 bench people.
  - The 41 nation leaders (`leader.person`) are in the met cast and author
    `social_skill`, between 0.42 and 0.80.
  - **79 people hold a head-of-state or head-of-government seat at the start**
    (`government.head_of_state.person` and
    `government.formation.head_of_government_person`). The 41 leaders are
    among them, and so is France's Prime Minister `FRA_aumont`. All 79 are
    met cast and author `social_skill` (QA, TAKE 2 on #188).
  - The four playable leaders: France's Moucron 0.53, Britain's Marchbanks
    0.62, Germany's Vogler 0.62, Italy's Bagnasco 0.66.
  - The "1595 people" in `person_card.gd`'s header is stale.

**Who reads it today.** Mostly the person being persuaded, not the leader:
- the listener's doubt of a promised date (`lever_odds.gd:49`);
- the listener's resistance to flattery (`lever_odds.gd:59`);
- the flatter backfire (`levers.gd:328`);
- the lie catch, which reads the listener against the leader
  (`lies.gd:25-26`, called at `:53`). **The catch is the only place the
  leader's own skill is read.**

**Promised, but no reader yet.**
- [[v5-reflection]] says social skill "sets how fast you read someone". Nothing
  does: knowledge is gained at a flat rate, scaled only by what is not yet
  known (`levers.gd:341-345`, `:426`).
- V5.3b's address to the nation moves opinion "by the president's social skill
  and standing" ([[v5]], V5.3 b). This spec claims that hook.

**Exposure reads no skill.** The author's first point is exactly this gap:
hazard = RATE × reach × modifier (`exposure.gd:69`, `:121`).

**No person holds a portfolio.**
- `ns.cabinet[portfolio]` names a party. A per-person minister register was
  never written (`game_state.gd:164`), and the resign act says "there is no
  person-in-portfolio slot" (`characters.gd:497-499`).
- Effectiveness is the party's `competence` × its affinity to the portfolio,
  as 1 + `COMPETENCE_SPAN` (0.75) × (competence − 0.5)
  (`cabinet.gd:109-116`, `cabinet_constants.gd:45`).

**Discipline.** The party's authored figure is the ceiling. The model only
erodes it, through `APPROVAL_TERM` 0.50 and `ELECTION_TERM` 0.20
(`discipline_constants.gd:34`, `:39`), and inertia pulls it back.

**Knowledge comes only from audience topics.** No summit and no foreign act
teaches the president anything about another leader. So every foreign leader
stays a stranger (B4's widest fog) for the whole game.

## 2. The scale

- **0 to 20, with 10 as today's middling.** 10 is `social_skill` 0.5, which is
  `SKILL_MIDDLING` (`lever_constants.gd:44`). Every formula below gives
  today's number at 10. Write s′ = (skill − 10) / 10, so s′ is −1 at 0, 0 at
  10 and +1 at 20.
- **Data carries one decimal; the screen shows an integer and a word.**
  Migration writes `intrigue` = 20 × `social_skill` to one decimal, so
  France's authored people keep their B5, B8 and L2 rows to float noise. K5
  states the tolerance. Rounding to integers would move them. The words:
  - 0 to 3, *hopeless* ("moins que rien": below middling, the skill does
    harm);
  - 4 to 7, *weak*;
  - 8 to 12, *able*;
  - 13 to 16, *gifted*;
  - 17 to 20, *exceptional*;
  - above 20, *legendary*;
  - below 0, *disastrous*.
- **Beyond 20, and below 0 (OPINION).** Authored and derived values stay in
  [0, 20]. Only play can push a skill past either end, through the modifier
  register (section 5), down to −5 or up to 25.
  - Past the ends each extra point is worth half:
    f(s′) = s′ for |s′| ≤ 1, and sign(s′) × (1 + (|s′| − 1) / 2) beyond.
    So 25 acts like 22.5. That makes "past 20" real but rare, earned in play
    rather than authored, and never a jump.
- **Every effect is a rule over the skill, never an id** (rule 1). Each span
  is sized so that 20 against 10 is worth about one existing step of the
  system it touches, and section 9's bands test that.

## 3. The five skills, and what each moves

A skill is listed only when something exists, or V5.3's signed sub-blocks
build something, that reads it. **Command (defence) is left out until V6's
war machine reads it**, because a skill nothing reads is the "matters
nowhere" failure.

Everyone carries all five (section 4). The middle column names the offices
where each one acts.

| Skill (French) | Acts in (section 4's table) | What it moves (rule; at 0 / 10 / 20) |
|---|---|---|
| **Intrigue** (intrigue) | The incarnated leader; a person received in audience, as a reader | Hiding, reading lies, reading people (below) |
| **Persuasion** (éloquence) | The incarnated leader | Audience odds; the address to the nation |
| **Diplomacy** (diplomatie) | Every nation's leader, AI nations included | Foreign acts and agreements; summits teach |
| **Parliament** (manœuvre parlementaire) | The head of government; every party leader, for their own party | Discipline erosion |
| **Administration** (gestion) | The head of government; a minister, once seated (call 4) | Portfolio effectiveness; V5.3c's cost estimates |

**Intrigue.**
- **The author's point 1.** The hazard of the press, the courts and the
  auditors is multiplied by (1 − 0.5 × f(s′)) for the sitting leader: ×1.5 at
  0, ×1.0 at 10, ×0.5 at 20. It covers every evidence row the leader left: a
  bribe, an offer, an affair, C6's overspend.
  - **A turned person is not covered (OPINION):** a witness talks whatever
    the leader's craft. That keeps c1c's lesson whole: the defence against
    the turned route is not crossing them.
  - France at 10.6 gives ×0.97, a few percent on S1 (INFERRED).
  - **A habit stays found (OPINION):** a route at its `HAZARD_MAX` of 0.25
    is still at 0.25 after the multiplier, whenever 0.5 × the raw hazard
    stays above the cap. So skill hides a single act and slows a habit, but
    it cannot make a habit safe. That is B6's "always ≥ 80 %" kept (GLM's
    finding 11, read as intended).
- **Lies, on both sides.** The catch is 0.33 + 0.75 × (reader − liar) / 20,
  today's formula in the new units. A listener's doubt and flatter read the
  listener's intrigue / 20.
- **Reading people** (the reflection's unbuilt ask). Every knowledge gain is
  × (1 + 0.15 × f(s′)) of the president: ×1.15 at 20, ×0.85 at 0.
  - **Why 0.15 and not the first draft's 0.5:** B4's pacing rows are 2 to 5
    audiences from stranger to acquaintance and at least 8 to known (L1
    reads 4 and 9). The gain is amount × (1 − k), so the counts are steep in
    the multiplier (QA, TAKE 4).
  - A model of L1's loop (one audience 0.05 plus one topic 0.07 per
    audience; it reproduces L1's 4 and 9 at ×1.0) gives, INFERRED:
    - ×1.15 at 20: 3 and 8;
    - ×1.19 at 25: 3 and 8;
    - ×0.85 at 0: 4 and 11;
    - ×0.81 at −5: 5 and 12.
  - Both rows hold at every reachable value. At ×1.25, known falls to 8 at
    the edge, and at ×1.5 to 6, which breaks B4's reason that a term cannot
    know everyone. K2 now guards both rows.

**Persuasion.**
- Argue, promise a date and flatter gain + 0.10 × f(s′) odds, re-clamped.
  Pressure (standing and approval) and bargain (a price) do not read it.
  - **Why 0.10:** half of what a believed lie (`LIE_ODDS` 0.20, c1c) or aid
    buys. The best orator gets honestly half of what a lie or a bribe buys
    (GLM's finding 8: the first draft cited the old 0.10).
- V5.3b's address moves opinion × (1 + 0.5 × f(s′)). Persuasion replaces
  "social skill" in that sub-block's text, and its standing term stays as
  written (GLM's finding 12).

**Diplomacy.**
- An act's relations delta always moves in the acting leader's favour
  (`diplomacy.gd`, execute's `relations`):
  - a gain is × (1 + 0.25 × f(s′));
  - a loss is × (1 − 0.25 × f(s′)).

  A skilled diplomat wins more from a friendly act and pays less for a
  hostile one; a poor one pays more. Hostile acts carry negative deltas
  (`measures.json` −10 and −18, `defence.json` −9 and −4,
  `agreements.json` −6), so a single factor would have made a 20 pay −22.5
  for a −18 measure (QA, TAKE 5).
- `accept_score` gains a fourth term, + `ACCEPT_SKILL_WEIGHT` × f(s′) of the
  proposing leader (`foreign_policy.gd:279-289`). Systems sets its size against
  the three existing weights with `foreign_policy_probe`, and section 9
  bounds it.
  - It is never named as a refusal reason, because the player cannot act on
    it.
- **A summit held teaches** `KNOW_DINNER` (0.10) of the partner's leader, so
  their skills' fog narrows (section 7). This needs no gate change:
  `President.covers` already admits another nation's head of state and head
  of government (`president.gd:35-48`), and `learn` reads it (`:185-189`).

**Parliament.**
- Each party's two erosion terms are × (1 − 0.5 × f(s′)), read from its own
  leader's parliament skill. A party in the governing coalition reads the
  higher of its own leader's and the head of government's, because the
  government whips its coalition too. At 20, an unpopular government's
  parties lose half the cohesion; at 0, half again more.
- The authored figure stays the ceiling, so the bound holds by construction.

**Administration.**
- Every held portfolio's effectiveness gains + 0.10 × f(s′) of the head of
  government, beside the party's competence term.
  - **Why two numbers (OPINION):** competence is the team, administration is
    the one who runs it. Like for like, administration's full swing (±0.10,
    0.20 from 0 to 20) is about a quarter of competence's (0.75 from 0 to 1),
    so the party still matters most (QA, R1).
- V5.3c's construction cost estimate is × (1 − 0.5 × f(s′)) wide, and so is
  its mean overrun. This claims the sub-block's "cost as a range set by the
  ministry's competence".

## 4. Everyone carries all five; an office makes one act

The author, 2026-10-30: "tout le monde a les competences, mais ne les utilise
que lorsque a un poste precis".
- Every person carries five values, authored or derived (section 6).
- A value **acts** only while its holder sits in an office that uses it.
  Anywhere else it is carried but inert: shown on the card, read by nothing.
- That makes a carried skill a reason to appoint someone. Section 8 shows a
  candidate's ranges where the player picks a head of government.

Every office is read from the **live** register, never from the authored
start fields. So a fall, an election, #157's dismissal or #193's resignation
moves the skills with the seat (QA's TAKE on `be1096e0`). Lines pinned to
`aad11d38`.

| Office (read from, live) | Skills that act | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **The incarnated leader** (`President.leader_id(ns)`, `president.gd:54-55`: the head of state where there is a separate executive, else the head of government) | intrigue (hiding; reading speed), persuasion, diplomacy | section 3 |
| **Head of government** (`ns.pm_person_id`, refilled by `Government.form()` at a fall or an election; the leader, where they are the same person) | parliament, on every governing-coalition party whose own leader reads lower; administration | section 3 |
| **Another nation's leader** (`President.leader_id(other_ns)`, AI) | diplomacy: its acts and its partners' acceptance | section 3 |
| **Party leader** (`Characters.party_leader(ns, party_id, ...)`, `characters.gd:538`, which follows the leadership override) | parliament, on their own party's discipline, unless the party is in the coalition and the head of government's is higher (GLM's finding 9) | section 3 |
| **Counterpart in an audience, a call or a summit** (the person across the table, for that scene) | their skills in that scene: intrigue as a reader (the lie catch, a promise's doubt, flatter's resistance), and so on | section 3 |
| **Minister** (once V5.3 seats one per portfolio, call 4) | administration, for that portfolio | call 4 |

- **The counterpart row (decided by the parent, 2026-10-30).** The person
  across the table in an audience, a call or a summit uses their skills in
  that scene: the reader's intrigue catches lies, and so on. Read strictly,
  "un poste précis" would have made every listener inert and collapsed B8's
  rows (catch by the gap) to one reader. B8 keeps its readers.
- **The head of government's skills act from the day they take the seat.** A
  dismissal (#157's removal) moves the two skills with the seat to the
  successor.
- **An empty chair is carried by the leader.** A mid-term premiership
  resignation leaves `ns.pm_person_id` "" until the next fall or election
  (`characters.gd:516-530`; [[people-model]] 14.8, OPEN). Until then,
  parliament and administration are read from the incarnated leader: the
  president takes the government in hand.
  - **Why the leader and not a neutral 10 (OPINION):** an empty chair should
    have a carrier the player can see and answer for. With a flat 10, a
    resignation would be free for a weak president and a loss for a strong
    one, whoever governed.
  - Where the leader is the head of government (no separate executive), an
    empty chair leaves `President.leader_id` "" too. Only then do the two
    skills read a neutral 10.
- **Command (defence)** has no office reading it until V6.

## 5. `social_skill` is replaced, and the save

- **A data migration, not a save migration.**
  - A `tools/` script writes `skills.intrigue` = 20 × `social_skill` for the
    324 people who author it.
  - The loader then refuses `social_skill`, and a lint fails it in `data/`.
  - Every reader moves to `Skills.of(state, loader, person, "intrigue") / 20`.
- **No save field for the skills.** They are content on `Person`.
- **One state register for play:** `person_skill_delta`, {person id: {skill:
  {"trained": t, "events": e}}}, holding what play adds. It lives on **the person's own nation's**
  `NationState` (the loader's person-to-nation map, built at load, says
  which), so an AI leader's diplomacy reads its own nation's delta.
  - The reader is therefore `Skills.of(state, loader, person, skill)`, not a
    read of `Person` alone (QA, R4).
  - The two parts replace the first draft's single ±5 cap:
    - **Trained, t ≥ 0:** never lifts the authored value plus t above **18**
      (below).
    - **Events, e in [−5, +5]:** the only way past 20 or under 0, so the
      effective value stays in [−5, 25].
  - Absent reads 0, which is correct for every older save, so both are
    `absent_ok` and earn no bump.
- **Events (the author: "par des ... events").** An event effect key,
  `skill_delta` {skill: amount}, through the applier, at most ±1 per event.
  - It applies to the person the event names: the leader by default, or a
    speaker (V5.2 d). It is written to that person's own nation, found
    through a person-to-nation map the loader builds once at load (it knows
    the file each person came from). It is never written to the nation
    whose event it is. So a foreign speaker's delta reaches the foreign
    leader's diplomacy (GLM's finding 10).
  - Content authors it where the fiction earns it. A summit carried off moves
    diplomacy, a censure survived moves parliament, a scandal weathered moves
    intrigue. The first batch is about 15 events.

### Training (the author: "par des formation")

- **Who can train:** the incarnated leader, and anyone holding an office in
  the player's own government. Today that is the head of government;
  ministers join once seated (call 4). Not the opposition, and not another
  nation's people.
- **What can be trained:** only a skill that acts in the trainee's current
  office (section 4's table). A president who is not head of government
  cannot train parliament or administration, so no course is spent on a
  skill that can never act. The first draft allowed it as "grooming"; it
  could not groom anyone, because only office-holders train (GLM's
  finding 4).
- **The course:** one skill, **six months**.
  - The trainee's skill grows at the end. A course breaks only if the
    trainee leaves the office before it ends; it then teaches nothing. An
    election the trainee survives does not break it (GLM's finding 13).
- **What it costs:**
  - **Attention.** One course at a time across the whole government: the
    president runs one programme, not one per person. That caps a term at 10
    courses.
  - **The diary, whoever trains.** A course is run from the presidency, so
    it takes four days of the president's own diary, booked like audiences
    and spread over the course. That holds whoever the trainee is. Each day
    is worth `DIARY_DAY` (1.05 asks, B5), so a course costs about **4.2
    asks**: four audiences not held.
    - The first draft charged the diary only when the president trained, so
      sending every course to the head of government cost nothing (GLM's
      finding 3).
  - **No money.** A course is EUR thousands against a budget in hundreds of
    billions; the c1 plan's call 7 dropped the same kind of line for the
    corrupt currencies.
- **The gain, with diminishing returns:** g = clamp((18 − (authored + t)) / 8,
  0, 1) points. `t` is stored at full precision; only the screen rounds, so
  the series below and the 18 cap hold exactly (GLM's finding 12).
  - 1 point at 10 or less, 0.5 at 14, nothing at 18.
  - Training never lifts a skill above **18**. 19 and 20 are authored or come
    from events, and past 20 only from events.
- **The red-team: training cannot make every skill 20 in a term
  (INFERRED).**
  - **At most 10 courses a term** (60 months ÷ 6, one at a time). That is a
    ceiling: a late start or a broken course gives fewer.
  - **One skill, every course, from 10:** 11, 11.88, 12.64, 13.31, 13.90,
    14.41, 14.86, 15.25, 15.60, **15.90**. About +6 in a whole term.
  - **From a weak 6:** four courses reach 10, six more reach about **14.4**.
  - **Spread over all five** (two courses each, from 10): every skill
    **11.88**.
  - **The sum of every gain in a term is at most 10 points.** No person
    reaches 18 by training in one term from 12 or below. Every value stays
    under 20, and K2 bounds what any value does.
- **Where it is read:**
  - **Systems:** a `Training` module, its tick (in `world_month.gd`'s
    `STEPS` and `tools/vault_diagrams.py`'s `GROUPS`, per AGENTS.md), and
    the diary entries.
  - **Interface:** the verb on the leader's sheet and the head of
    government's card ("Send on a course: Administration, 6 months, +0.9").
  - **Content:** the course names.

## 6. The content plan

  heads and all 232 party leaders are among them: 0 of either set is bench
  (MEASURED).

## 7. Fog: what the player sees

- **Another person's skill is a range**, through the knowledge register,
  with B4's grammar:
  - width = 20 × `LeverOdds.fog_width(knowledge)`: **7.2 points** for a
    stranger, **1.6** once known;
  - the seeded chain of `shown_range`, keyed `skill:<s>`, so each window sits
    inside the last at a fixed truth, and the truth is inside 100 %.
- **A modifier moves the truth mid-game.** The window then rides it, as the
  odds' window rides the odds. It is still inside, and still no wider than
  the knowledge allows.
- **The incarnated leader's own skills are exact.** Everyone else, the Prime
  Minister included, is read by knowledge.
- **The windows are not saved.** `shown_range` walks its chain from the seed
  and the knowledge at every read (`lever_odds.gd`, `below()`), so a load
  rebuilds the same window. No field is added (GLM's finding 3 dropped).
- **Repeated lies sample the catch.** A player who lies to one person again
  and again could estimate their intrigue from how often they are caught.
  Each catch costs −0.5 rapport, a grudge and half the knowledge, so the
  price is the guard, as it is for every fogged odds, where repeated attempts
  sample the truth too (GLM's finding 1, recorded, not blocked).
- **Foreign leaders:** a summit now teaches (section 3), so a met partner
  narrows from 7.2 toward the known width over a term's summits.
