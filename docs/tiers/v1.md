# V1 — After the MVP Works

Everything here is planned, sequenced, and expected to be built. None of it starts until [[mvp]] meets its done-when criteria.

Order within V1 is roughly the order below: each block builds on the previous one, and the earlier blocks are the ones that most directly deepen the loop that already exists.

## 1. Political depth

The MVP assembly votes by ideology alignment. V1 makes the assembly an actor.

> [!note] Decomposed into six sub-projects; (a) is built
> Dependency order and status are in [[ROADMAP]]. Built: government formation per
> nation's real rules, coalitions as a point in the existing two-axis ideology
> space, the split between the executive's term and the assembly's, cohabitation,
> minority governments, motions of censure, and Article 49.3 with a cost on use.
> The model is [[government-model]], including an honest note that the current
> balance produces majorities stable enough that neither cohabitation nor censure
> fires in a played game. Next: (b) party discipline and rebellions.

- **Coalition formation** — governing needs a majority assembled from multiple parties, each charging a price in policy concessions.
- **Party discipline** — how reliably members follow the leader's line, and what erodes it.
- **Confidence votes** — the assembly can bring the government down mid-term.
- **Dissolution and snap elections** — the player gambles on a better mandate.
- **Cabinet and ministers** — appointments with competence and loyalty, modifying their department's output.
- **Upper house** — a second chamber that delays and blocks.
- **Judiciary** — courts that can strike down laws, a check the player cannot outvote.
- **Referendums** — put a question directly to the population.

## 2. Political system templates

The headline feature from the original pitch, built once there is a working institutional layer to generalise.

- Political systems defined as **data templates**: presidential republic, parliamentary republic, constitutional monarchy, absolute monarchy, one-party state.
- Each template declares which institutions exist, who holds which power, how leaders are selected, and what the lose condition is.
- **Constitutional amendment** as the mechanism for changing them, requiring a supermajority or a referendum.
- **System transition** — monarchy to republic and back — as the dramatic payoff.
- **Term limits**, defined by the template rather than hardcoded.

This block is also what makes **additional playable nations** meaningful: Germany plays differently from France because its template differs, not because of bespoke code.

## 3. Diplomacy and trade

Neighbours stop being scenery.

- **Relations score** per nation pair, driving what each will accept.
- **Diplomatic actions** — state visits, condemnations, recognition, expulsions.
- **Bilateral trade agreements**, negotiated, with sector winners and losers at home.
- **Tariffs** as both revenue and weapon, with retaliation.
- **Sanctions**, imposed and received, with domestic blowback.
- **Supranational bloc** — EU-shaped membership with shared rules, transfers, and real constraints on domestic policy. Bloc-level voting where the player is one member among many.
- **Defensive alliances**.
- **AI foreign policy** — neighbours pursuing their own interests, which is the actual hard part of this block.

## 4. Private companies

- **Named fictional firms** per nation, per [[fictionalization]], each in a sector with revenue and headcount.
- **Subsidies** — pay a firm to stay, hire, or invest.
- **Sector regulation** — rules that cost margin and buy other outcomes.
- **Nationalisation and privatisation** — take firms in, or sell them off for a windfall and a permanent loss of control.
- **Bond markets and credit rating** — external financial judgement on domestic policy.

## 5. Society and information

- **Media outlets** with political leanings that frame the player's actions.
- **Scandals** requiring a response.
- **Press freedom** as a dial with legitimacy costs.
- **Trade unions, strikes, protests** — organised opposition outside the assembly.
- **Corruption** and **rule of law** as national statistics degrading policy effectiveness.
- **Demographics** — ageing driving pension and health costs on an unavoidable curve.
- **Immigration and emigration**, with high political salience.
- **Inequality and poverty** as distributional statistics driving unrest independently of average prosperity.
- **Healthcare and education quality** as outcomes of spending, feeding back into opinion.

## 6. Economic depth

- **Inflation** as a pressure distinct from growth.
- **Business cycle** — booms and recessions arriving whether or not deserved.
- **Progressive tax brackets**, **wealth and capital gains taxes**.
- **Sectoral investment** and **infrastructure projects**.
- **Bond yields** rising with debt and instability.

## 7. Elections as a phase

- **Campaign phase** before the vote, spending time and money on messaging.
- **Campaign promises** that bind, with a trust cost for breaking them.
- **Electoral system choice** — first-past-the-post, proportional, two-round — producing visibly different assemblies.

## 8. Events

Deliberately last. Scripted and semi-random events are added only once the systemic loop is genuinely working, so they decorate a good game rather than disguising a hollow one.

- Data-defined events with preconditions, choices, and consequences.
- Crisis events that force a response under time pressure.
