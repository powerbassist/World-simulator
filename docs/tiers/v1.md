# V1 — After the MVP Works

Everything here is planned, sequenced, and expected to be built. None of it starts until [[mvp]] meets its done-when criteria.

Order within V1 is roughly the order below: each block builds on the previous one, and the earlier blocks are the ones that most directly deepen the loop that already exists.

## 1. Political depth

The MVP assembly votes by ideology alignment. V1 makes the assembly an actor.

> [!note] Decomposed into six sub-projects; (a) through (d) are built
> Dependency order and status are in [[ROADMAP]]. Built: government formation per
> nation's real rules, coalitions as a point in the existing two-axis ideology
> space, the split between the executive's term and the assembly's, cohabitation,
> minority governments, motions of censure, and Article 49.3 with a cost on use
> ([[government-model]]); then party discipline as live state that erodes with
> unpopularity and with an approaching legislative election, seat-level
> rebellions in both kinds of division, and the disaffection term that lets an
> eroded coalition bring its own government down ([[discipline-model]]). The
> censure branch, which V1.1a recorded as working but unreachable in a played
> game, now fires; then dissolution and snap elections — a real gamble,
> measured before it was built rather than tuned after
> ([[dissolution-model]]); then cabinet and ministers — five portfolios keyed
> to spending lines, competence and portfolio position both derived from
> tables that already existed rather than authored fresh, and a shortfall
> term that makes resignation attributable to the player's own assignment
> rather than to general unpopularity ([[cabinet-model]]). Next: (e) upper
> house and judiciary.

- **Coalition formation** — governing needs a majority assembled from multiple parties, each charging a price in policy concessions.
- ~~**Party discipline** — how reliably members follow the leader's line, and what erodes it.~~ Built, [[discipline-model]].
- **Confidence votes** — the assembly can bring the government down mid-term.
- ~~**Dissolution and snap elections** — the player gambles on a better mandate.~~ Built, [[dissolution-model]].
- ~~**Cabinet and ministers** — appointments with competence and loyalty, modifying their department's output.~~ Built, [[cabinet-model]] — competence and effectiveness are built; named individual ministers are deferred to V1.2.
- **Upper house** — a second chamber that delays and blocks.
- **Judiciary** — courts that can strike down laws, a check the player cannot
  outvote. **This bullet also owns §5's "rule of law", handed here at
  [[ROADMAP|V1.5]]'s decomposition.** [[mechanics-society]] tiers rule of law as
  "institutional strength gating what the player can get away with", which is this
  bullet in different words: two uncontestable gates on player action would be one
  machine claimed twice. §5 keeps **corruption**, which is a statistic degrading
  delivery and shares no state with a court. Fifteenth instance of the stale-row
  failure [[trade-model|V1.3b]] first recorded.
- **Referendums** — put a question directly to the population.

## 2. Political system templates

The headline feature from the original pitch, built once there is a working institutional layer to generalise. Sections 1(a)–(d) are that layer.

- ~~**Additional playable nations** — first, and before the templates.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2a]]: a nation-select surface on the main menu, per-nation starting fiscal policy sourced per nation rather than shared, and Germany playable alongside France as the first test that nothing in the simulation quietly assumed France — chosen for maximum institutional contrast (`chancellor_election` vs `presidential_appointment`, `constructive_absolute` vs `absolute_members` censure, no separate executive clock, `pr_national` with a threshold vs `two_round` with none, dissolution the player can never call). A third nation onward is a one-flag data edit. See [[scope|France-only is temporary]].
- ~~Political systems defined as **data templates**: presidential republic, parliamentary republic, constitutional monarchy, absolute monarchy, one-party state.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2b]], corrected to four: `semi_presidential_republic` (France is semi-presidential, not plain-presidential), `parliamentary_republic`, `constitutional_monarchy`, `directorial_republic` (Switzerland). Absolute monarchy and one-party state are not authored — no nation in the ten-nation roster instantiates either, and building them unattached would be content with no reader. Deferred with a named trigger: the first start scenario or system transition that produces one authors its template then. See [[political-systems]].
- ~~Each template declares which institutions exist, who holds which power, how leaders are selected, and what the lose condition is.~~ Built as a **constraint on nations' own flat fields**, not a defaults table — see [[political-systems]] for why. The head of state is new state (five constitutional monarchies had none before); `lose_condition` names what was previously inferred from `has_executive`.
- ~~**Constitutional amendment** as the mechanism for changing them, requiring a supermajority or a referendum.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2c]]: an amendment is a law with three additions — `constitutional: true`, `systems: [...]`, and a new `effects.institutional` channel that sets (not nudges) `censure_rule`/`dissolution_rule`/`formation_method`/`amendment_rule`/`term_limit`/`term_months`. The threshold is a sixth per-nation dispatch-on-a-data-string field, `government.amendment.rule`, constrained by the template's own `allowed_amendment_rules` — real constitutional fractions (3/5, 2/3, 3/4, an ordinary majority, or a declared-unresolved mandatory referendum for Switzerland), not one global constant. Referendum stays the parked half of "supermajority or a referendum" — [[v1|V1.1f/V1.7]] — with the collision named: a referendum exists precisely because a supermajority is sometimes unreachable, and this block measured exactly that for Germany (see below) rather than assuming it. Full argument: [[amendment-model]].
- ~~**System transition** — monarchy to republic and back — as the dramatic payoff.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2d]]: the same amendment mechanism, pointed at `system_id` itself, plus a completeness rule and a target-side cross-check (not, in the end, the `lose_condition` change first assumed — see [[transition-model]]). Shipped alongside a third playable nation, GBR, added to the scope at plan time once the original scope was found to ship to zero playable nations.
- ~~**Term limits**, defined by the template rather than hardcoded.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2b]]: `permits_term_limit` on the template, `term_limit` on the nation, enforced at the executive verdict. Only France's template permits one.
- ~~**Start scenarios** — a nation plus an authored starting state: debt level, seat composition, approval, coalition.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.2e]], corrected to **three** authored axes: the coalition stays *derived* by `Government.form()` from the seats a scenario already authors, because authoring both authors a contradiction the formation rule exists to resolve — and under GBR's `largest_party` the axis is degenerate anyway. `data/scenarios/*.json`, directory-scanned, every override optional and partial; `scenario_id == ""` is the standard start and has no file. This is the project's answer to difficulty, and the reason there is no difficulty slider: a scenario changes data, a slider would change tuned constants and invalidate every measured balance number. [[declined]]'s falsifier — that the reachable range is too narrow — was **measured and did not fire**. Full argument, including the two content corrections the measurement forced and why an authored approval needed a second write to survive its own inertia: [[scenario-model]].

Templates are what makes a second playable nation *interesting*; the nation itself does not wait for them. Germany plays differently from France because its data and its template differ, never because of bespoke code.

Neighbours stop being scenery.

- ~~**Relations score** per nation pair, driving what each will accept.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3a]], and the block's real schema decision as expected: the first state in the project that is neither per-nation nor global. **Symmetric** — one number per unordered pair, so `rel(A,B) == rel(B,A)` is structurally true rather than maintained — and **derived** rather than authored, from EU co-membership and a shared land border, the only two inputs [[data-sources]] can supply. The third input the roadmap expected, size asymmetry, was dropped as a direct consequence of the symmetry call and handed to (b), where per-partner trade weight is the honest place for it. Full argument: [[diplomacy-model]].
- ~~**Diplomatic actions** — state visits, condemnations, recognition, expulsions.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3a]]: four verbs in `data/diplomatic-actions/*.json`, directory-scanned, on [[legislature-model|the law template's]] preconditions/cooldown/effects shape — but deliberately **not** its `position`, `majority` or `once`, because a diplomatic action is an executive act with no assembly division to score. Surfaced on the Europe tab's existing nation panel, with no new tab. Every content magnitude was set by the measurement rather than tuned after it, including the finding that a domestic price hitting fewer than all six blocs is legible on the polling panel and never in the headline approval figure.
- ~~**Bilateral trade agreements**, negotiated, with sector winners and losers at home.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3b]], and the session that made [[economy-model|`external_impulse_pct`]] **per-partner** — a directed ninety-entry weight from partner size, EU co-membership, a shared border and the pair's relations, replacing one GDP-weighted aggregate that was the same number for every viewer. **This bullet said "unquiets" and that was an overclaim**: the channel is now structurally correct and still numerically small, because the only things that perturb a partner are a bilateral agreement (which does, and which is why the channel is live rather than dormant) and V1.3c's tariffs and sanctions. It is also where V1.3a's dropped size asymmetry landed: a normalised directed weight has two sides where a symmetric pair key has one. The balance harness was re-run and came back **bit-identical across thirty fourteen-year runs**, which the model was shaped to make provable rather than merely likely. Full argument: [[trade-model]].
- ~~**Tariffs** as both revenue and weapon, with retaliation.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3c]]. Revenue is a line of its own on the budget panel, not a fourth entry in `TAX_KEYS`; the weapon is a new additive term in the growth equation scaled by **`w(target → imposer)`**, so who you can hurt is a different question from who matters to you. Both fall out of two constants and neither is authored — including a **real Laffer curve**, with an interior maximum at 0.625 severity, because a tariff destroys some of the trade it is levied on. Retaliation is a deterministic mirror and the block's **third declared stopgap**.
- ~~**Sanctions**, imposed and received, with domestic blowback.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3c]], and the prediction below held exactly: one verb, one pair, two prices — a tariff collects and bites moderately, a sanction collects nothing and bites hard, and they are the same mechanism read at two points on one curve. Imposing pays through `bloc_offset`; **receiving pays through `opinion.gd`'s existing growth and jobs terms**, with no new channel, and whether that was legible at all was the session's named escalation trigger. It did not fire — one embargo by the largest partner moves the headline approval figure by 4.9 to 7.4 points against a ±3.0 noise floor — but only after the measurement forced a model correction. Full argument: [[coercion-model]].
- ~~**Supranational bloc** — EU-shaped membership with shared rules, transfers, and real constraints on domestic policy. Bloc-level voting where the player is one member among many.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3d]], and the **fourth kind of live state**: not per-nation, not a symmetric pair, not a directed pair, but one object binding eight nations at once. A rule in force does not refuse a budget — comply and you pay in the policy you could not run, defy and you pay in a penalty and in politics. The council divides on ideology weighted by the `ep_seats` `data/eu_parliament.json` already carried, which is what makes that file load-bearing beyond the elections it was written for, and **the player can be outvoted and bound**. The `ISSUE_KEYS` question is **answered yes**: `europe` is the twelfth, and the reasons are structural rather than thematic — `bloc_offset` never enters `issue_salience`, so a Europe priced on it could never be named by the polling panel or the result screen; a split price never clears the noise floor in the headline figure; and a flat offset cannot become more *salient*. The cost was measured before it was taken and the S10 ratio moved **+0.39 %**, in the safe direction. The political split then fell out of weights written at S5 for unrelated reasons. Full argument: [[bloc-model]].
- ~~**Defensive alliances**.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3g]], with defence rather than with the bloc exactly as this bullet argued: an alliance is only interesting once there is an obligation that bills you at home, and the obligation is what V1.3g spends its measurement on. `GameState.pacts` is symmetric pair state on the key builder relations and agreements already use, and **the set is a derived read** — an alliance looks like a set and does not need to be stored as one, so this is the first session since (a) to add two registers and no new SHAPE of live state. Full argument: [[defence-model]].
- ~~**AI foreign policy** — neighbours pursuing their own interests, which is the actual hard part of this block.~~ **The row split, exactly as this page predicted, and the letters do run to g.** The **interest model** is built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3e]] — why a nation the player does not govern answers one way rather than another — and it discharged all four declared stopgaps a–d shipped, not the three its own ROADMAP row named. The **acting agent**, the nine acting on their own initiative, is **(f)**. The split was taken on *machine* count rather than on the surface count the trigger named, which had already fired on three sessions that did not split. **The prediction about the inputs was half right, and the wrong half was the load-bearing half**: every nation does carry a `coalition_position`, and the eight non-player members are **clustered** on it — economic spanning 0.87, social 1.13 — so an interest function resting on it would have given nine nations that behave alike, which is [[trade-model|the `GRAVITY_EXPONENT = 1.0` finding]] in a new place. Interest rests on the directed trade weight instead, with ideology as the smallest term in the file. **The acting agent is built at [[ROADMAP|V1.3f]]**: a scheduler on `NationState`, a target and a verb chosen unprompted from the ten verbs already in `data/diplomatic-actions/`, and the message column U5a shipped as furniture taking its first real content. Agreements stay AI↔AI — an AI proposing to the *player* would have the interest model answer on the player's behalf, and an inbox the player replies to is a second machine. The probe corrected the model four times, the largest being that a partner's importance as a standing *addend* is a goodwill ratchet that walked relations onto the clamp. Full argument: [[foreign-policy-model]].

### Defence, as domestic politics

**(g), built.** Scheduled here and nowhere earlier, and deliberately thin. The player's real interaction with defence in a nation-management game is budgetary and political — what it costs, who it upsets, what an alliance obliges you to — and all three run on machinery that already exists: the `defence` spending line, the `defence` opinion issue with its per-bloc weights, the Defence portfolio in [[cabinet-model]], and [[legislature-model|the law effect channels]].

It cannot land before this block, because a deployment with no counterparty and no relations score is a spending line with a different label.

- ~~**Military capability** as an abstract score, *derived* from sustained defence spending rather than authored — the same treatment [[cabinet-model|competence]] gets. No new per-nation data.~~ Built, and the "no new per-nation data" clause held without effort: all ten nations already author `spending_pct_gdp.defence`, so the stock is well defined for the whole roster. **The nine's capability never moves**, because they have no budget model and their spending never changes — that is correct rather than a stopgap, and `tests/test_defence.gd` asserts it over a full run so the `has_budget` lift [[trade-model]] books stays a separate session.
- ~~**Deployments** as reversible commitments with a running budget cost and an opinion cost that differs by bloc, and differs again depending on whether the commitment is one you chose or one an alliance obliged.~~ Built, and **"differs by bloc" needed no new machinery** — `BLOC_SALIENCE_BASE` already weights `defence` differently for every bloc. That turned out to be the measurement's central finding rather than a convenience: five blocs of six weight it at **zero**, so the price is legible on the polling panel's rural row and structurally invisible in the headline, which is [[diplomacy-model|(a)]]'s own finding reached from the other direction. The chosen/obliged multiplier was **decorative at 1.50** and is 1.75.
- ~~**Alliance obligations** that bill you at home — the domestic price of a foreign commitment, which is the part a defence layer in this game is actually for.~~ Built, and it is a **level read** rather than an edge trigger: nothing in the project ever lifts a retaliation, so an obligation that only the player could close would be a permanent monotone charge. The player still cannot close one — only the aggressor lifting its measure, or abandoning the pact, can. **What the obligation actually fires on was not authored**: the nine never coerce each other, so the only thing that reaches an ally is the council's `common_external_tariff`, and a pact *outside* the bloc is what bills you. Your alliance with Britain costs you when Brussels turns on Britain.

Explicitly **not** built here, and staying in [[someday]]: units, theatres, war declaration and resolution, arms sales, espionage, nuclear posture. That is a second game, and the lose condition in this one is an election.

This is also what unblocks the Military dashboard tab [[ROADMAP]] parks — until this block, that tab had a blocker with no scheduled route out. **Unblocked, and deliberately not built**: U5a killed the tab rail, so that tab is now a ninth window, and a session whose own heading is "deliberately thin" is not where it belongs. Pacts and deployments are per-pair and live on the nation panel; the bill is a budget figure row; capability sits under GOVERNMENT on the overview.

> **(a)** the firm register and subsidies, **(b)** sector regulation, **(c)**
> nationalisation and privatisation. The split is on **machine count** — five
> machines across the row, and [[foreign-policy-model|V1.3e]]'s trigger is
> machine count — and the a/b line is architectural rather than thematic: a
> subsidy is an **executive act** and a regulation is a **law**, so (b) reaches
> `effects.company` through the assembly where (a) reaches it directly.
> That is [[diplomacy-model|V1.3a]]'s own distinction, one block later.
>
> **The row double-booked twice and named neither collision.** Bond markets are
> claimed here *and* in §6 — settled below in §6's favour, because a yield writes
> `debt_service_pct_gdp` and shares no state with a firm. And a subsidy *is* §6's

>
> Built at (c), and **the block is complete**: `state_share` as a third field with a
> third single writer, a dividend as a computed revenue line, and a purchase and a
> windfall that write the **debt stock** rather than any budget line — a capital
> transaction is a swap, not income, and it would otherwise close a loop through

> call bought three built machines for free, the loudest being that nationalising a

- ~~**Named fictional firms** per nation, per [[fictionalization]], each in a sector with revenue and headcount.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.4a]], and **revenue is not authored** — it is `share × sector output`, so authoring it would be one quantity written twice. A firm is four fields: id, name, sector, `share` of its sector, and `headcount`. The last is the **differentiator**, and it is what makes this section more than §6's sector lever: two firms of equal share employing different numbers of people is the only thing in the model that creates or destroys jobs on net. Without it every firm in a sector answers alike, which is [[foreign-policy-model|the clustered `coalition_position` finding]] caught at design time rather than by a probe. Authored for the **three playable nations**; an absent file gives unpartitioned sectors, exactly what all ten had before. Firms scale with the roster forever, which is [[declined]]'s sector rule applied to a second content class.
- ~~**Subsidies** — pay a firm to stay, hire, or invest.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.4a]] on `effects.company`, the **fifth effect channel** and the first pointed at something that is not a nation. The bill is a computed spending line rather than a sixth `SPENDING_KEYS` entry — that list is keyed 1:1 to cabinet portfolios, so a sixth line would be a sixth minister — which is the route V1.3c's tariff revenue and V1.3g's deployment bill both took. **The persistent channel is market share**: a subsidised firm takes share from its named rivals and *keeps it* when the money stops, so the decision has a tail. The measurement came in where §3's did: the headline approval figure moves +0.76 against a ±3.0 noise floor while workers move +5.88 and urban −3.57.
- ~~**Sector regulation** — rules that cost margin and buy other outcomes.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.4b]] as a *law*: the assembly divides on it, which is why it was its own session rather than a second verb in (a). **"Cost margin" named a quantity the model does not have** — there is no margin, profit or productivity term anywhere in `src/sim/` — and the corporate-receipts channel that would have invented one was cut before it was written, because it would have handed (c)'s dividend line a half-built profit to read or contradict. What ships instead is `regulation_burden`, one signed per-sector quantity read by the **two channels (a) already built**, so the session added no price, no budget line and no clamp. The row's second miss is the §6 collision above, and it is the **twelfth** instance of the stale-row failure. Deregulation is the negative direction on the same accumulator rather than a repeal verb — this project has none, and a one-way burden would be the permanent monotone charge [[defence-model]] names.
- ~~**Nationalisation and privatisation** — take firms in, or sell them off for a windfall and a permanent loss of control.~~ Built at [[ROADMAP|V1.4c]]. **"A permanent loss of control" named a control the model did not have** — a subsidy works on any firm whoever owns it and a regulation targets the sector — which is the **fourteenth** stale-row instance and [[companies-model]] §13's "cost margin" one session on. What ownership controls is one factor at one existing site: a state holding absorbs the sector's compliance cost so the payroll does not, and the shield is **signed**, so a public firm in a deregulated sector forgoes the hiring too. A dividend is a yield on **output** and not on profit, as §13 argued a session in advance; the purchase and the windfall are a **balance-sheet write** on `debt_eur_bn` rather than any budget line, which is what lets a nationalisation put the player in breach of the bloc's `debt_to_gdp_pct` rule with no new machinery at all. **The `ISSUE_KEYS` question is answered NO**, and the deciding number is that the S10 ratio drifts −1.46 % where [[bloc-model|V1.3d]]'s moved +0.39 % — structurally, because `business` carries `public_finances` at 0.34 and is the bloc that makes ownership divisive at all, so **the mechanic and the cost are the same number**. A regulation had the same claim to a key and V1.4b took none. Full argument: [[companies-model]] §20–29.
- ~~**Bond markets and credit rating** — external financial judgement on domestic policy.~~ **Moved to §6**, which claimed the same mechanism. A bond yield writes `debt_service_pct_gdp` against `DEBT_REAL_RATE_PCT` and shares no state with a firm; §6 is also where inflation and the business cycle re-baseline the balance harness anyway, so endogenising the rate there is one re-run instead of two. A credit rating is the same machine read at a second point — [[coercion-model|V1.3c]]'s "one verb, two prices" shape — so it does not split off back into here.

## 5. Society and information

> [!note] **U6** builds the roster this section keeps assuming
> Four of the bullets below — media outlets, scandals, union leaders, and the
> named figures a corruption story needs — are about *people*, and the game has
> none. It knows a leader's name, a head of government's name and a party
> leader's name, and nothing else; [[cabinet-model|V1.1d]] deliberately made a
> portfolio point at a party rather than at a minister, and that deferral has
> been quietly load-bearing ever since. **U6b** builds the roster, which means
> this section inherits it rather than having to author it. The banned-names
> check and a `SAVE_VERSION` bump are U6b's cost, not V1.5's.
>
> **U6 split, and the scope named above shrank on an argument rather than on
> effort.** [[calendar-model|U6a]] shipped the diary; U6b authors **political
> faces only** — foreign leaders, party leaders, ministers. The row's own list
> and its "no new effect channel" clause cannot both hold: a foreign leader is
> the face of a nation `data/nations/` already ships, so meeting one is
> `state_visit` in a hat, but a **journalist has no target that exists** and
> meeting one *is* a new effect channel. Journalists, celebrities and business
> therefore belong to this section and §4 rather than to U6b, and each arrives
> with the mechanism that gives it something to do. See [[ROADMAP]].
>
> **[[people-model|U6b]] is built, and this section inherits the register as
> promised — with two corrections to what was booked against it.** The roster
> was not authored from nothing: ~70 people already existed as loose strings in
> five places, and every nation file wrote one person's name twice, so the

### Why last survives re-examination

The obvious argument for moving it forward is that a consequences-and-reactivity layer is the direct vehicle for making cause and effect visible. Two things answer that:

- **That goal already shipped its vehicle, and it was not events.** U4 built decision deltas — a baseline captured at commit, measured against the next history entry, printed as `recent_effects`. Events add *authored* consequences on top of systemic ones, and authored consequences are only legible if the systemic ones already are.
- **The event schema's shape is the union of the effect channels sections 3–7 create.** Written before them, it gets written twice.

**What would move it forward:** playtesting finding the loop legible but **inert** — the player understands what happened and nothing ever happens *to* them. That is a measurement, and nothing weaker counts. Same discipline [[decision-log|V1.1b]] arrived at for balance goals: name the measurement that would confirm it.
