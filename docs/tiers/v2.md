# V2 — The World Becomes Visible and Located

Everything here is planned and sequenced. None of it starts until [[v1]]'s rows are
closed, and they are: V1.1e shipped the upper house and the judiciary, so **nothing is
left in that tier at all** — see [[upper-house-model]] and [[judiciary-model]].

> [!note] Why this tier exists, and what it is not
> V1 built machines. Every one of them is legible only as a number on a panel: a firm is
> a share, a strike is a percentage, a project is a countdown, a trade partner is a
> weight. **The country the player governs has no places in it.** V2 gives the same
> simulation geography, faces, a calendar that costs something, and motion. It is not a
> second game and it opens no new front — war stays in [[someday]] where V1.3g left it.

Order within V2 is the order below, chosen so the visible payoff lands before the heavy
rewrite. One track at a time per session, interleaved, as since U1.

> [!warning] Blocks 5 and after are rows to challenge, not commitments
> This project counted **forty-nine stale-row instances** across V1 — rows that named a
> quantity the model did not have, double-booked a mechanic two sections claimed, or
> described a machine already built under another name. Every one was found by counting
> rather than by being told. A row written this far ahead is a hypothesis, and each block
> gets a decomposition session that re-counts machines and surfaces before it splits.

## The four calls made before any code

**A city is to population what a firm is to sector output.** V1.4a authored a firm as a
`share` of its sector with an unnamed residual, so the partition is exact by construction
and there is no reconciliation to get wrong. Cities take that shape: a city holds a share
of its region, a region a share of the nation, rural and unnamed is the residual. This is
why §2 can ship the register before §5 rewrites the economy.

**Bottom-up and bit-identical are not opposites.** If national = Σ regions *exactly* and
every region is initialised on the national trajectory, the balance regimes stay
bit-identical until the first regional divergence, and every constant measured since the
MVP keeps its calibration. What changes is that the **spine becomes regional**, so every
later lever may be regional. §5 must write that condition into its plan before writing
code, and `tests/test_balance.gd` is what enforces it.

**The day becomes real for the president, not for the country.** `clock.gd` has emitted
`day_advanced` since the MVP and nothing in `src/sim/` has ever listened. A journey that
takes four days because Rome is far is physical rather than a preference, so day
resolution enters the *agenda* and every system stays monthly. See §4.

**Placing a power station is the first verb where the player writes a coordinate.** Every
lon/lat in the game today is authored ([[ROADMAP|U6c]]'s `project_sites`). U6c's rule —
*a lon/lat nothing reads carries no information* — is satisfied in its strongest possible
form when the player's own click is what reads it.

## 0. The three V1 rows still standing

Not a block and not a session of its own until someone counts them again. The `| V1 |`
column of the four [[mechanics-governance|mechanics]] tables holds rows that were recorded
rather than scheduled, and after the V1 tier closed there are **three**, not four.

- **Embassies** — presence that improves information and relations over time.
- **Foreign aid** — money for influence.

Both are tiered V1 in [[mechanics-foreign-policy]], appear in no row of any tier doc, and
were found by [[ROADMAP|V1.8]]'s last sweep of the column — **instance forty-nine**. Cheap:
a fifteenth and sixteenth entry in `data/diplomatic-actions/` on the existing
`effects.relations` channel, which fourteen verbs already use. They do not need a block.

- **Honeymoon and fatigue** — time in office as its own drag on approval. Deferred **in
  code** since S5: `opinion_constants.gd` carries `APPROVAL_NEUTRAL` under the comment
  *"No honeymoon bonus — time in office as its own drag is V1."* The oldest open deferral
  on the project.

> [!note] The fourth row is not orphaned any more, and V2's own spine is what freed it
> **Birth rate and life expectancy** was filed as having no dataset.
> [[demography-model]] §14 already corrected half of that — *"there is now data, and there
> is still no mechanism that would read them that §2 did not cut"* — because V1.5b cut the
> cohort machine on the finding that `population` enters no equation in `economy.gd`.
> **§5 of this tier is exactly the change that makes a head count load-bearing.** So the
> row is not an orphan needing a session; it is a bullet of §6, and it arrives with the
> mechanism that reads it.

## 1. The shell, re-cut

`src/ui/` is 25 files and ~11 700 lines in which every panel invents its own rows. This
block comes first on U5a's own precedent: U5a retuned the theme and moved the shell
*before* U5b, U5c, U6a, U6b and U6c built into it. V2 adds at least five new surfaces, and
designing them in the old grammar and refactoring after is drawing twice. Unlike U5a, this
pass knows the full list of surfaces in advance.

> [!note] Decomposed into three sessions, V2.1a–c
> ~~(a) the panel system~~ **built**, ~~(b) the twenty-five panels~~ **built -- there
> were sixteen left, not twenty-five**, ~~(c) navigation and the resting screen~~
> **built**. **The block is complete.** (b) turned the fold contract from a report into
> a test: ten of the fourteen registered panels declare a verb and every one clears the
> fold. (c) measured the resting screen rather than redesigning it -- **42 of 74
> pending-months silent**, and the response is one brass mark on the cluster where the
> answer is given. See [[panel-system]] sections 9-17.

- **A panel system** — section, row, figure, delta and ledger primitives, so a panel stops
  writing its own. Three shipped bugs trace to their absence: a format copied from a
  neighbouring row (V1.6b), a `.get(key, 0.0)` default printing a confident **0.00**
  against a live 31.80 (V1.6d), and two figures sizing their own columns until one ran off
  the window (V1.6f).
- **A derived dirty registry** — `main.gd` keeps a hand-written list of panels to mark
  dirty under a comment predicting its own failure, and it has been wrong twice:
  `_people_panel` from U6b until V1.4a, then `_projects_panel` and `_campaign_panel` until
  V1.8. `WindowManager.register()` already knows all fifteen. Deriving the sweep from the
  registry makes the failure **structurally impossible rather than remembered**.
- **A fold contract** — a panel's primary action must be reachable at the shipped window
  size. `nation_panel.gd` paid for a fold at V1.3e and again at V1.3g, and V1.3g proved
  **height alone does not fix one**. The signature is the plate's own brass hairline drawn
  along the **bottom** edge when content exceeds height: the existing visual rule, reused
  to say something true.
- **Navigation and the resting screen** — what is on screen when nothing is open, and what
  the four clusters mean once V2 has added five surfaces to them. **Built: the four hold**,
  because the bar may use 1384px of 1920 and the widest plausible drawer uses half of it, so
  width can neither force a fifth cluster nor choose one. Region, city and energy land under
  Economy and climate under Society, on the rule that *a cluster is the domain of the
  decision rather than the shape of the object*. Two of the five named surfaces are not
  drawer entries at all: §4's day agenda is a ribbon and §2's city is reached by clicking
  the map.

## 2. The geographic register

The block that makes every later one possible, and the one that satisfies a condition
written at the MVP: [[data-sources]] files GeoNames with the status *"Not imported at MVP.
Listed here so the decision is already made when cities become gameplay rather than
decoration."* That condition is met here.

> [!note] Decomposed into two sessions, V2.2a–b, on the two-track rule
> Counted cold the block is ~36 surfaces and **four machines** — import pipeline,
> register and loader, map draw and hit test, two panels — across the `S + data` track
> this table names and the U track the last two bullets are. That is the cut V1.6e,
> V1.6f and U6c each took. ~~(a) the import and the register~~ **built**; ~~(b) the map
> and the two panels~~ **built**. See [[geography-model]].

- ~~**Regions**~~ **built — 112 of them, and THE LEVEL IS PER NATION**, which the row
  could not have known: NUTS 2 for France is the régions abolished in 2016 (`Picardie`,
  `Nord-Pas-de-Calais`), while NUTS 1 for Italy is five statistical macro-areas and for
  Switzerland is one country. Figures from Eurostat `demo_r_pjangrp3`, geometry from
  GISCO — and **the geometry is the authority on the roster**, which drops extra-regio,
  the overseas regions and a duplicate NUTS vintage with one rule.
- ~~**Cities**~~ **built — 581 of them, a share of their region, filed by
  point-in-polygon** rather than by GeoNames' own `admin1`, which for France is coarser
  than the level the row asked for.
- ~~**Firms domiciled**~~ **built**, and the measured consequence is one the row did not
  ask for: **one city holds a third to a half of a nation's named payroll**.
- ~~**Finer map geometry**~~ **built** — the roster's fill is its 1:10m GISCO regions at
  every zoom, over a same-hue 1:50m base and under the 1:50m national stroke; the
  two-pass fill/hairline draw is what renders the enclaves, because the roster carries
  one of each direction (Berlin outpopulates Brandenburg; Bremen is a third of
  Niedersachsen) and no single order renders both.
- ~~**Cities drawn and clickable**~~ **built — radius ∝ √population (`sqrt(pop / 8 M)`
  against `RADIUS_BASE`), ascending so the largest wins an overlap, above `CITY_MIN_ZOOM
  = 2.0`, which sits just UNDER the opening Europe fit on `LABEL_MIN_ZOOM`'s own
  discipline — the volume clause was measured for the ~1000 px opening fit. Brass
  crosses onto the map for the first time and only for where people are**; everything
  else stays navy, white and roster hue. The hit test is screen-space and gated on the
  same zoom constant, so a glyph that is not drawn is never a target.

> [!warning] Regional GDP is NOT here, and the row's Britain problem was half wrong
> V2.2a's share is a share of **population**. `nama_10r_2gdp` carries zero `UK` codes at
> every year — worse than [[ROADMAP]] recorded — but `demo_r_pjangrp3` carries all 41 UK
> NUTS 2 codes with values at 2019, summing to the national figure exactly. **Regional
> population is measured for all ten; only regional GDP is short**, and regional GDP is
> what §5 makes load-bearing. Importing it here would ship a number nothing reads, so the
> gap and the ONS finding pass to §5 whole.

> [!warning] The hit test does not go through the roster, and U6c already said why
> `_nation_at_lonlat()` iterating `_polygons` alone is what makes a non-simulated country
> unclickable **structurally**. A city test routed through it would make that function
> answer for two things and the property would die the day someone widened it. Cities take
> `_project_at_screen_point()`'s shape — a radius test in **screen space**, because a glyph
> is a fixed number of pixels at every zoom.

## 3. The face of power

- ~~**A portrait per person in `data/people/`**, generated outside the game and committed
  as raster assets.~~ **Built — 71 committed rasters under `assets/portraits/`, found by
  person id, swept off the register.** Generated outside the game on the author's local
  ComfyUI (Juggernaut X Hyper) through a committed recipe — `tools/portraits_generate.py`
  drives the API, `tools/portraits.gd` imposes the duotone and writes the assets — so the
  *recipe* is the artefact and person 72 is one register edit and a re-run; the committed
  PNGs are canonical where the machine drifts. **Likeness policy, the author's call at plan
  time: a portrait EVOKES the real politician its fictional name plays on, it never depicts
  them** — prompts are described archetypes, never a name. The **missing-portrait call this
  box left open: SILENT fallback in game** — `portrait_card.gd`'s silhouette moved into
  `src/ui/portrait.gd` and became the fallback every surface shares, exactly the lenient
  precedent this box argued for; stale art nobody references is the refused direction, by
  the tool and by `tests/test_portraits.gd`. The author's "background per situation" call:
  a **nation-hue band** behind each portrait, display-side (`framed_for()`), the same
  PALETTE the map fills that nation with. **Faces live on the portrait card and the People
  window** (executive, party leaders, Abroad) — a name in a sentence is not identity
  content; later spread is one `framed_for()` per surface.

> [!warning] The count is 71 TODAY and it is derived, never authored
> `data/people/` holds 71 people across ten files at V2.2, and every one of §4's summit
> scenes, §6's sited campaigning and any later session naming somebody adds more. **A
> fixed roster of 71 portrait filenames is this project's oldest failure class in a new
> place** — five hand-written lists of panel names, `EXPECTED_PANELS` at seven of
> fourteen, `_unhandled_input()`'s six keycodes of fourteen windows. The register is the
> list: a portrait is found by the person's own id, the sweep iterates
> `loader.person_by_id`, and no number appears in `src/`.
>
> **So the design question is not "71 portraits", it is what a person WITHOUT one looks
> like** — and the answer already exists. `portrait_card.gd`'s placeholder silhouette is
> not deleted when art arrives; it becomes the fallback, which is what lets a later
> session author a person and ship them before the art run catches up. Whether a missing
> portrait is an error, a warning or silent is the session's call to make and record —
> `_validate_people()`'s refusal of an orphan person is the precedent in one direction and
> `data/companies/`'s optional file the precedent in the other.

- ~~**Duotone, imposed at generation**~~ **Built — imposed at the duotone PASS rather
  than in the model prompt: one crop, one eyeline, one posterized navy-to-brass ramp for
  every person, so any drift in the raw generations is erased rather than shipped.**
  This single decision is what stops a wall of generated images
  reading as stock pasted into a HUD. It also has to survive the roster growing: whatever
  produces them is run again for person seventy-two, so the *recipe* is the artefact to
  keep, not only its output. And the first wall paid for the distinction: a prompt that
  pinned everything but the seed produced 71 near-siblings the author rejected on sight —
  **"one crop, one eyeline" constrains the FRAME; the FACE is where variety lives**, and
  the recipe now varies age, hair, facial hair, glasses, face shape, expression and one
  quirk per person, each decorrelated by its own hash of the id.
- ~~**Icons across every panel**~~ **Built — all sixteen registered windows carry an
  at-icons glyph, committed individually (`git add -f`; the .gitignore exception took them
  with no edit), rendered in the drawer entries and the title bars, and carried on
  `register()` itself** so no second table can drift — `tests/test_portraits.gd` asserts
  every registered window has one and every referenced SVG is committed, which is the
  fresh-clone rule as a test. Read as `.svg` text and rasterised
  tinted, never `load()`ed.
- ~~**People in the UI**~~ **Built — `portrait_card.gd` shows the player's leader's art
  on the nation band, and the People window's executive, party-leader and Abroad rows
  carry faces: the wall this section's duotone rule exists for.** There is art now.

## 4. The president's day

> [!note] Decomposed into two sessions, V2.4a–b, before either ran
> Counted cold at plan time: six bullets, **~5 machines and ~35–45 surfaces** — travel
> duration and day-block occupancy, a meeting-unlock register, the ribbon, the summit
> dialogue surface and the `dialogue_manager` adoption it carries, and the `interrupt`
> class — across both tracks at once, so the block cut on the rule that cut V2.2, with the
> author's standing instruction beside it. **(a) The day becomes real** — day resolution,
> travel, meetings that unlock verbs, the ribbon. **(b) The scenes** — summit dialogues and
> `interrupt: true`, chained off (a)'s meeting arrival. Three calls the author took at plan
> time: **`dialogue_manager` is adopted**, the parked addon, its price paid knowingly — a
> committed `addons/` and a stated exception to the no-autoloads rule; **the ribbon sits
> under the action bar** as a standing strip — not a drawer entry (V2.1c's own call) and not
> inside the Diary; and **scarcity is occupancy only** — a journey occupies its days and
> overlapping blocks are refused, no ceiling constant, and U6a's deleted currency stays
> deleted.

> [!note] This discharges a debt U6a wrote down by name
> U6a swept a monthly budget of the player's days and **deleted** it, on a reason that is
> general rather than about calendars: *"`available()` returns what is **legal** and
> nothing models what the player **wants**, so any ceiling priced against legality binds in
> 100 % of months. That gap is a session, not a constant."* A ceiling priced against
> **travel and ceremony** is not priced against legality. This is that session.

- ~~**Day resolution for the agenda**~~ **built — and for nothing else; every system stays monthly.** See [[calendar-model]] §12.
- ~~**Travel with real duration**~~ **built — a state visit is longer or shorter by where it goes, priced off the V2.2 register's own coordinates** at booking time; the number rides the entry and the scheduler stays a leaf. Paris–Rome is the anchored four days.
- ~~**Meetings that unlock verbs**~~ **built — a symmetric `met_months` stamp, a level read, and `met_recently` as ordinary precondition content.** The window is 12 because that is the visit's own cooldown: below it the gate has a dead zone no visit can reopen, at twice it the gate never closes. The treaty-class verbs ask for a meeting in their own files; the lock and the key are both data.
- ~~**Summit scenes**~~ **built — and the adoption was gated before it was priced.** `dialogue_manager` proved it runs headless (the `--headless --script` regime the tests use) BEFORE any content was authored, then landed whole: committed into `addons/` (790 KB, MIT), `DialogueManager` in the committed `project.godot`, the **first stated exception to the no-autoloads rule**, the reason beside the `.gitignore` exception and in CLAUDE.md's Gotchas. A summit is ephemeral — drawn deterministically off the meeting stamp, applied through the shared effects applier, serialising nothing. See [[summits-model]].
- ~~**`interrupt: true` events**~~ **built — one boolean on the closed key list, a pause gated on the flag, and the reload re-raise from the same code path as the tick.** Every event without the flag pauses nothing; V1.8's non-modal argument survives everywhere but the flagged class. The shipped member is `blackout_night`, `respond_months: 1` — the flag's meaning compressed.

> [!warning] This reverses a V1.8 call, and only for the flagged class
> [[events-model]] argues the Dispatches window is non-modal because *a crisis you can
> ignore and pay for is the whole of "time pressure"; a modal removes the choice by
> removing the option of not making it*. That argument survives for every event without the
> flag, which is most of them. What the flag buys is the case the argument does not cover:
> an event nobody could reasonably choose to ignore.
- ~~**A ribbon, not a grid**~~ **built — the bottom-most plate, under the action bar.** Travel days hatched, the meeting solid brass, today underlined; `Calendar.day_cells()` is the whole rule about occupancy, so the ribbon and the diary cannot disagree. Scarcity is occupancy only — overlapping blocks are refused, U6a's deleted currency stays deleted.

## 5. The economy from below

The block that changes the spine. `economy.gd` computes a national growth as a trend plus
eight impulse addends, then `_apply_growth_to_sectors()` reconciles three sectors against
the month's GDP target exactly.

- **National = Σ regions, exactly** — the partition is the model, not a correction on top
  of one. §2 shipped it as a partition of **population**, so what this block adds is the
  quantity: regional GDP, measured for nine nations on Eurostat `nama_10r_2gdp` and
  **absent for Britain at every year**. [[geography-model]] §4 records the ONS probe so
  this session does not repeat it — at NUTS 1 the authored fallback is twelve numbers
  rather than forty-one.
- **Regional unemployment** — the quantity §6's migration reads.
- **Regional sector mix** — a region is agricultural, industrial or service-heavy, and
  differently from its neighbours.

> [!warning] The cheaper version was offered and declined, and the record should say so
> A ninth `economy.gd` addend holding a city partition — V1.4a's idiom, reading exactly 0.0
> until something moves — delivers the same thing from the player's seat at a fraction of
> the cost, and the difference is invisible in play. The full bottom-up was chosen anyway.
> What makes it affordable is the second call above: exact aggregation plus regions
> initialised on the national trajectory means the re-run can still be bit-identical, and
> **that is a claim to measure rather than assert**.
