# V4 — The World Wants Things (DRAFT, unsigned)

> [!warning] Drafted, not signed
> Drafted 2026-09-07 by a planning session, off a cold count of the repository
> against the author's own list of asks, the V3.0 agency probe's recorded verdict,
> and a read of what Paradox actually ships (sources at the foot). **[[v3]] is not
> finished** — V3.3 through V3.6 are still open and the author's call at this
> draft's gate was to finish V3 as written before any of this starts. Every row
> here is a hypothesis on [[v2]]'s own rule: each block gets a decomposition
> session that re-counts machines and surfaces before it builds. Nothing below is
> committed until the author signs the tier, as V3 was signed at the V3.1 plan
> gate.

The organising goal: **V1 built the machines, V2 made them visible and located,
V3 makes Europe whole — and V4 makes the world want things.**

Today nothing in this game wants anything. Seventy-one portraits are inert. The
player is never told what they are playing for. A hundred verbs sit on the action
bar with no reason to prefer one over another. Events arrive seven times in a
career and the nine AI nations never get one. V4's test is not *is there more
content*. It is: **can the player say out loud what they are trying to do this
term, who is trying to stop them, and what it cost when they lost.**

## The evidence this tier is built on

Counted cold on 2026-09-07 against `main` at 75fe6fe, not estimated:

- **Events are seven.** `data/events/` is 2 files; `src/core/event.gd` is nine
  fields; `src/sim/events.gd` holds **one pending slot**. No weight, no severity,
  no category, no per-event cooldown, no chain, no art, no per-nation targeting,
  and events fire for the player alone — the "no AI chooser" limitation
  ([[events-model]] §11) recorded for the sixth time.
- **The council agenda is seven proposals** across 3 files, rotated on a
  deterministic index (`src/sim/orgs.gd:362`). The player votes yes or no. No
  sponsor, no lobbying, no bargaining, no veto, no ratification at home.
- **Industry is eighteen firms in three nations.** `companies-model` §8 is titled
  *"Three nations, not ten"*, and §2 *"A firm is four fields, and the count is the
  design"*. A firm has never done anything on its own.
- **The top bar carries four numbers** — deficit, GDP, unemployment, months to
  vote (`src/ui/top_bar.gd`). There is no alert surface, no outliner, no nested
  tooltip, and no message settings.
- **There are zero audio files in the repository.** Zero loading screens, zero
  progress bars. Two `create_tween` calls in the whole project, both inside
  vendor `src/ui/vendor/MapCamera2D.gd`.
- **The satellite is upscaled 6.4× at max zoom.**
  `assets/map/blue_marble_5400x2700.jpg` is 15 px/degree; `MAX_ZOOM` 8.0 against
  `BASE_SCALE` 12.0 draws 96 px/degree. [[map-platform-model]] refused per-tile
  raster LOD at V3.1 with a reason — the raster is one quad. There are no shaders
  in `src/`.
- **The roster is V3.3's job, not this tier's.** 11 nations and 3 playable today;
  the EU-27 plus GBR and CHE, all playable, is [[v3|V3.3]]'s own row.

### And the root, which was measured a tier early

**[[v3|V3.0]]'s gate FAILED and the author's route call is still open.** The best
active play style scored **−2.00 approval points against idle** at month six
(improved from −7.57), with the vote deficit closing 3.0 → 0.2. The probe's own
reading of why: *idle is locally optimal by construction* — every satisfaction in
this model is a delta against the start, so change is priced and inactivity is
free. Three routes were recorded for the author; V3.1 and V3.2 were each told not
to pick one up silently, and neither did.

That is "the game does not feel like a game" written in the game's own numbers.
**A hundred and fifty events and forty agenda entries authored on top of a loop in
which playing loses are a hundred and fifty events and forty agenda entries of
mush.** So the call opens the tier rather than riding inside a content block —
which is [[v3]]'s own *agency before content* argument, restated one tier later
because the argument was right and the gate was not met.

## V4.0 — The agency call, settled

**Track S + M. The gate the whole tier stands on.**

*Today:* the V3.0 probe's verdict, unacted. Three routes on the table.

*What it does:* picks a route and measures it on `BALANCE_PLAN`'s pre-declared
form — bands written before the first run, because a balance goal without a
measurement is a promise rather than a plan.

The three routes, restated from the probe report:

1. **A competence-credit channel.** Activity earns a satisfaction term that
   inactivity does not — the missing half of a model in which every satisfaction
   is a delta and change is the only thing priced.
2. **An activity-keyed drag on idleness.** The mirror: doing nothing accrues a
   cost — drift, decay, a coalition that stops believing in you.
3. **Accept the bound**, and make the game about something other than the
   approval number — which V4.1's agenda would then have to carry alone.

Routes 1 and 2 are not equivalent and the difference is legible in play: (1)
rewards the player who acts and leaves the idle player where they were; (2)
punishes the idle player and leaves the active one where they were. **The choice
is the author's.** The block measures whichever is taken against a pre-declared
band and reports honestly if it is not met, exactly as V3.0 did.

Two standing obligations are discharged here rather than left to drift: the
**`QUALITY_SCALE_PP` re-fit-or-retire** (V1.5a's delivery-under-announcement rule
has been inverted at 0.75 since V3.0 and was accepted rather than re-swept), and
the **Eurozone/Schengen zero-price** band [[orgs-model]] named a trigger for.

*Refuses:* new content. This is a measurement and tuning block; nothing it ships
is authored.

*Gate:* at least one policy family moves overall approval **positively against
idle** by month six, at a margin declared before the first run. If the route taken
cannot reach it, the block says so and hands the next route back rather than
tuning the failure away.

## V4.1 — The programme

**Track S + U. The cheapest large gain in the tier.**

*Today:* nothing tells the player what they are trying to do. [[pledge-model]]'s
campaign promises are the only forward-looking commitment in the game and they are
one file — a promise made at an election, judged every month against the world.

*What it adds:* an **agenda register** on Victoria 3's journal-entry shape, named
for this game as *the government's programme*. An entry is a live goal with a
condition, a clock and a progress reading:

| field | note |
|---|---|
| `id`, `name`, `summary` | as an event's |
| `group` | economy / institutions / Europe / society / the office itself |
| `possible` | the trigger that makes it offerable — `PRECONDITION_FIELDS`' shape |
| `activation` | how it opens: authored, event-opened, player-chosen, or forced |
| `immediate` | effects on activation |
| `complete` / `fail` / `invalid` | the three conclusions, on [[referendum-model]]'s own three-outcome precedent |
| `timeout_months` | 0 = no deadline |
| `current_value`, `goal_value`, `progressbar` | the progress read, DERIVED from a live figure rather than stored |
| `on_complete` / `on_fail` / `on_timeout` | `Law.extract_shared_effects()` — the one applier events already use, not a second |
| `weight`, `pinned` | ordering, and whether it rides the outliner by default |

**The player chooses.** Not every entry is handed down: at a term's start the
player picks a programme out of what is offerable, which is the first time this
game asks *what are you for* rather than *what will you do this month*. Failing
your own programme is priced; succeeding is the competence credit V4.0 may have
just built a channel for — **the two blocks must agree on that channel or state
why they do not.**

**Pledges are absorbed or explicitly kept apart, and the decomposition session
decides which.** A campaign promise and a programme entry are the same shape
wearing two names — one made to voters at an election, one made to yourself at a
term's start. Either resolution is defensible; drifting into two half-machines is
the failure this project has caught six times.

*Content:* **30–40 entries** across five groups, each with 2–4 authored
conclusions.

*Surfaces:* an Agenda panel; pinned entries in a strip the top bar can read;
progress on Overview; a card when an entry opens, completes or fails.

*Refuses:* branching quest trees. An entry concludes, it does not unlock a
sequel — chains are V4.3's machine, and two machines pointed at one job is exactly
what `legislature.gd`'s single-applier argument exists to prevent.

## V4.2 — People who want things

**Track S + data + U. The largest block in the tier, and the one that overturns a
written rule.**

*Today:* 71 portraits across 10 nations; `data/people/` is 11 files.
[[events-model]] §3 states the rule this block breaks: *"The subject is an OFFICE
and never a person — [[people-model]]'s rule verbatim — so nothing in `src/sim/`
ever touches a person id and no mechanism on a person stays true by
construction."* `src/ui/events_panel.gd` is the only file in the project that
resolves an office to a name.

**That rule was right for V1.8 and it is what makes the same scandal read
correctly in a republic and in a monarchy. V4 overturns it deliberately.** The
office resolution stays — a law still names an office, and a scandal still lands
on whoever holds it — but `src/sim/` gains a person register and people acquire
wants. **The session that builds this rewrites [[events-model]] §3 at the rule
itself, with its reason.** This tier doc does not pre-emptively edit it: a roadmap
that rewrites a model doc is a roadmap claiming work it has not done.

*What it adds:*

- **A person gains state.** Two or three authored traits from a fixed set,
  ambition, loyalty to the player, standing, and a grudge ledger. Traits are the
  Crusader Kings lever: they colour what a person will do and what it costs them
  to act against type.
- **A political register.** Who holds what office, who wants which office, who
  owes whom. [[cabinet-model]] already exists and is the natural spine.
- **Five populations of person**, each already half-present in the data:
  ministers and the cabinet; party leaders and rivals inside the player's own
  party; union leaders ([[unrest-model]] is the meter); press owners
  (`data/outlets/`, 3 files, [[media-model]]); and — once V4.5 lands — company
  chiefs.
- **People act.** Resign, defect, brief against you, leak, demand a portfolio,
  threaten a leadership challenge, endorse or refuse to endorse at an election.
  The collective version takes Victoria 3's escalation shape — a movement runs
  Passive → Agitating → Protesting → Rioting — and [[unrest-model]]'s grievance
  stock is the meter it reads rather than a second one.
- **Portraits scale.** 71 today against a 29-nation roster at ~7 people each plus
  the new populations: target **~220–260**, on V2.3's committed recipe.

*Surfaces:* the People panel rewritten around wants rather than a list; a portrait
on every event card and agenda entry with a subject; hover cards on any named
person; a relationship read on the cabinet.

*Size:* the block most likely to split at its own decomposition session. The
honest cut is **(a) the model** — person state, traits, the loyalty tick, the
register, portraits — and **(b) the acts** — defection, resignation, the leak, the
challenge. Decided cold at that session, not here.

*Refuses:* dynasties, marriage, inheritance, personal lifespan. This is a modern
republic and people leave politics rather than dying of it. Also refuses **the
leader's personal wealth**, which stays in [[someday]] on its own existing
reason: a money pool with no exposure risk is a second currency with no lose
condition attached.

## V4.3 — Events at scale

**Track S + data + U.**

*Today:* seven events, eighteen-plus choices, two files, one pending slot.

*What it adds, machine before content:*

- **Weight and category.** The flat `likelihood` becomes a weighted draw inside a
  category, so a scandal cannot crowd out a fiscal crisis on hazard alone.
- **Severity.** The field V1.8 explicitly did not carry, and the one that drives
  V4.6's auto-pause and message settings.
- **Hooks.** Today an event fires only on a monthly hazard against a precondition.
  Add Paradox's `on_action` shape: an event fires *because* a law passed, an
  election was lost, a firm was nationalised, a minister resigned, an accession
  referendum was won. **This single change is most of what makes a Paradox game
  feel as though it is watching you.**
- **Chains.** A choice can arm a follow-up at a delay — the machine V4.1
  deliberately refuses, built once, here.
- **A queue, not a slot.** [[events-model]] §5 argues that one slot *is* what "the
  matter on your desk" means, and at seven events that argument holds. At a
  hundred and fifty it does not. A short queue ordered by severity, plus the
  per-event cooldown designed and cut at V1.8 — cut on the grounds that one slot
  already bounded it, an argument that dies with the slot.
- **The AI gets events.** A minimal chooser scoring choices against the nation's
  own position, so the other twenty-eight nations have politics that happen to
  them. Sixth recorded instance of "no AI chooser", and the one that closes it.
- **Art.** Themed illustrations on CK3's `theme` shape — **40–60 images reused by
  category**, not one per event, on V2.3's generation recipe.

*Content:* **7 → 120–150 authored events**, 350+ choices, across eight categories:
scandal, fiscal crisis, social unrest, personal (needs V4.2), European, foreign,
industrial, and disaster ([[v3|V3.5]]'s sited disasters are the event schema with
a place).

*The balance constraint that survives the growth:* [[events-model]]'s E1b finding
still binds — holding a choice's size-weighted mean `bloc_offset` near zero pushes
the cost onto the **smallest** bloc, so `business` (size 0.10) becomes the natural
loser far more often than its share unless the content is spread deliberately. At
seven events that was a note. At a hundred and fifty it is an authoring constraint
with a probe attached.

*Refuses:* `overall_approval_pct` as a precondition, still. An event gated on low
approval and priced in approval is positive feedback with nothing damping it, and
[[events-model]] and [[pledge-model]] already refuse that field for two different
reasons — which is worth more than either.

## V4.4 — The council, contested

**Track S + U.**

*Today:* `data/bloc-proposals/` is three files and seven proposals, rotated on a
deterministic index. The player votes yes or no; member votes are auto-scored.

*What it adds:*

- **Proposals arrive with sponsors** — a nation, led by a person (V4.2), who wants
  it and will spend relations to get it.
- **Bargaining.** Support in exchange for something: a vote elsewhere, a
  concession, aid, a posting. The relations channel exists — V3.2 landed
  embassies and foreign aid on it.
- **Coalitions and vetoes** on the org's own authored rules: unanimity for some
  classes, qualified majority for others, which is what the Union actually is and
  what `org_seats` was generalised for.
- **Presidency rotation**, so the player sometimes sets the agenda instead of
  answering it.
- **Ratification at home.** [[someday]]'s *treaty ratification fights in your own
  assembly*: a council decision the player voted for still has to pass their own
  legislature. **The strongest single link between this game's two halves, and it
  costs no new machine** — [[legislature-model]] is built.
- **Climate accords** land here if [[v3|V3.5]] has not already spent the
  machinery on them.

*Content:* 7 → **~35 proposals** across the four orgs, with authored sponsors and
prices.

*Refuses:* org-side consent for accession, and AI desire for membership. Both were
refused at V3.2 with reasons in [[orgs-model]]; this block does not make either
reachable.

## V4.5 — Industry

**Track S + data + U.**

*Today:* eighteen firms in three nations. `id`, `name`, `sector`, `share`,
`headcount`, plus a `city` since V2.2a. Six verbs — subsidise (two), withdraw
support, minority stake, nationalise, privatise — and seven sector-burden laws.
A firm's entire behaviour is share-shifting under subsidy and regulation.

*What it adds:*

- **The roster.** 18 → **~170–210 firms**, six to eight per nation across the
  EU-27 plus GBR and CHE, on V3.3's import discipline and V1.4a's naming rules.
  This is the "many more companies" ask, and **the count is the design** exactly
  as `companies-model` §2 said at eighteen.
- **Firms act** — [[someday]]'s standing rows, finally reachable: invest or
  disinvest, relocate between regions (V2.2's regions and [[v3|V3.4]]'s network
  are what make a relocation *mean* something), hire and fire, fail, be bought by
  a foreign rival.
- **Firms lobby.** [[someday]]'s *corporate lobbying shifting policy*: a firm
  spends against a law it dislikes or for one it wants, through the assembly
  arithmetic that already exists.
- **New player verbs:** block a foreign takeover ([[someday]]'s strategic-asset
  protection), bail out or let fail ([[someday]]'s bankruptcies and bailouts),
  break up, court an investor, appoint to a state-owned board ([[someday]]'s
  state enterprises with patronage — and patronage needs V4.2's people to mean
  anything).
- **A firm gets a chief**, from V4.2's register, which is what makes a bailout a
  conversation rather than a line item.

*Refuses:* **a fourth sector.** Three sectors are load-bearing on GDP with one
reconciliation ([[sector-model]]), and a fourth is a model rewrite bought for
flavour that firm-level tags carry instead. Also refuses per-firm balance
sheets — a firm is a share of a sector, not an accounting entity, and
`companies-model` §2's rule survives the growth in number.

## V4.6 — The reading surface

**Track U. The block that makes everything above legible.**

*Today:* four numbers in the top bar. No alert surface, no outliner, no nested
tooltips, no message settings, no auto-pause preferences.

*What it adds:*

- **The top bar, whole.** 4 → **10–12** readings with hover breakdowns: approval
  and its trend, treasury and the debt path, unrest, the assembly arithmetic as it
  stands right now, months to vote, agenda progress, pending obligations, European
  standing, GDP with a growth arrow, unemployment, inflation, the energy margin.
  Each block above ships its own entry as it lands; **this is the block where the
  strip becomes one thing rather than eleven.**
- **Alerts that say what is wrong and what to do about it.** The specific
  criticism levelled at Paradox's own warnings, and this game has its own version
  of it: [[playability-audit]]'s Register B is full of surfaces that print `0%`
  where they mean *absent*. `panel_kit`'s em-dash-not-zero rule is the precedent to
  extend from one primitive to the whole shell.
- **Nested tooltips.** Every number explains its inputs and every input explains
  its own. `Trends`' attribution machine already computes the explanation; nothing
  surfaces it outside the election screen.
- **An outliner** of pinned things: agenda entries, watched firms, people you are
  worried about.
- **Message settings** on Victoria 3's shape: per-category notify / feed / popup /
  auto-pause. With V4.3's hundred and fifty events this stops being polish and
  becomes load-bearing.
- **A legend that changes with the map mode**, and a keyboard shortcut for the
  five windows that have none — `diplomacy`, `orgs`, `calendar`, `people`,
  `companies`, [[playability-audit]]'s own row.

*Refuses:* a rebindable-key system. Named, sized and parked — it is a settings
subsystem rather than a reading surface, and nothing above needs it.

## V4.7 — Ceremony, motion and sound

**Track U.**

*Today, counted:* zero audio files in the repository. Zero loading screens, zero
progress bars. Two tweens, both in vendor code. [[v3|V3.6]] will have landed menu
presence, an election sting and a tally.

*What it adds:*

- **A loading screen with a real progress bar.** `DataLoader` walks a known file
  set, so the progress is measurable rather than faked — and after V3.3 and V4.5
  there is enough to load that the bar is honest rather than decorative.
- **Motion as feedback.** Panels open and close rather than appear. Numbers roll
  to their new value. A day tick has a pulse. A card arrives rather than existing.
  The clock cluster shows its speed rather than stating it.
- **Election night as a sequence** rather than a screen: results by region as they
  arrive, the seat bar filling, the verdict last.
- **A sound set.** Ambient beds per screen, UI cues, an event sting keyed to
  V4.3's severity, an election sting. CC0 or equivalently clean, recorded in
  `ATTRIBUTION.md` as every asset in this project already is.
- **Map-mode transitions**, which V4.8 needs anyway.

*Refuses:* voice, licensed music, and a composed score. Named and parked.

## V4.8 — The map, close up

**Track U + data.**

*Today:* one raster upscaled 6.4× at max zoom, plus pure vector. [[map-platform-model]]
refused per-tile raster LOD at V3.1 with a measured reason. No shaders exist.

*The author's call is a stylised political map, not satellite tiles* — which is
what Crusader Kings and Victoria actually draw, is licence-clean, needs no tiling
pipeline, and **does not overturn V3.1's refusal**: it makes the refusal
irrelevant by retiring the satellite at depth instead of scaling it.

- **A zoom-banded ground.** Satellite at globe view, where it is honest; a paper
  and terrain ground at depth, with **hillshade derived from public-domain
  elevation** — one texture generated once by `tools/`, not a tile server.
- **Region fills gain material**: subtle pattern, edge darkening, a fill that
  reads as land rather than as a colour key.
- **Borders weighted by kind** — national, regional, org. Today every line is a
  line.
- **City glyphs in tiers** by population, with labels that avoid collision, which
  is what actually makes a map look like a map at depth.
- **Roads at their proper weight.** [[v3|V3.4]] draws the network; this makes it
  read as infrastructure rather than as lines.
- **The first shaders in the project**, and the frame budget is the constraint.

*Gate:* V3.1's own, re-declared with the texture pass added — **deep-zoom map cost
stays inside 2.0 ms** (1.33 ms today), and deep zoom is viewport-bounded by
construction, which is the property that makes this affordable at all.

*Refuses:* 3D, a terrain mesh, and any streamed tile source. All three named here
so a later session does not rediscover them as ideas.

## V4.9 — The neighbourhood

**Track S + data.**

*Today, after [[v3|V3.3]]:* 29 simulated nations, all playable. About 160 further
countries drawn as outlines with no hit-test and no state.

*What it adds:* the roster past the Union, **in two grades rather than one** —
because "simulated" and "visible" are two different asks and conflating them is
how a roster becomes unaffordable.

- **Simulated, not playable** — the European neighbourhood: NOR, ISL, SRB, UKR,
  TUR, BIH, ALB, MKD, MNE, MDA, XKX, BLR. Full geometry, economy, trade, relations
  and org standing; domestic politics only as deep as the AI needs. ~12 nations.
- **A diplomatic surface for the rest of the world.** The ~160 outlines gain a
  name, a relation, a trade weight and a seat in whatever org they belong to — so
  the diplomacy panel has a world in it, and V4.4's council is not the only
  foreign thing that exists. No domestic model, and this doc says so plainly
  rather than leaving it to be discovered.

*Refuses:* global scope as a *simulation* (~195 nations with domestic politics)
and non-European playable nations. Both stay in [[someday]], and this block is the
reason they can stay there without the world feeling empty.

## What V4 deliberately does not open

- **War.** Still [[someday]], where V1.3g left it. An alliance system is not a war
  system, and a character system is not one either.
- **A second simulation loop** — provinces-as-nations, sub-national play. The
  roster grows; the game does not fork. [[v3]]'s own line, unchanged.
- **Global domestic scope.** V4.9's diplomatic surface is the answer to the pull,
  not a down payment on it.
- **3D, streamed map tiles, multiplayer.**
- **Localisation, modding tooling, achievements.** [[someday]]'s presentation
  rows; V4.7 lifts audio and presentation polish out of that list and nothing else.
- **Dynastic simulation.** V4.2 gives people wants, not lifespans.
