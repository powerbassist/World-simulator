# V4 — The World Wants Things (SIGNED 2026-09-13)

> [!note] Signed by the author, 2026-09-13
> Drafted 2026-09-07 off a cold count of the repository against the author's
> own list of asks, the V3.0 agency probe's recorded verdict, and a read of
> what Paradox and Geopolitical Simulator actually ship (sources at the foot).
> The gate calls were taken 2026-09-12 and are recorded in [[v3]]'s close-out;
> the signing was taken with them in hand, as V3 was signed at the V3.1 plan
> gate. **V3.7's Phase B assets PR was still unmerged at signing** — it gates
> the first V4 build, not the signature. Every row here stays a hypothesis on
> [[v2]]'s own rule: each block gets a decomposition session that re-counts
> machines and surfaces before it builds, and the per-block estimates live in
> the signed execution order below.

The organising goal: **V1 built the machines, V2 made them visible and located,
V3 makes Europe whole — and V4 makes the world want things.**

Today nothing in this game wants anything. Two hundred and fourteen people are
inert. The player is never told what they are playing for. A hundred verbs sit on
the action bar with no reason to prefer one. Events arrive seven times in a
career. Trade exchanges no goods. And the author's own verdict at this draft's
gate, recorded because it is the reason the expensive routes below were taken
rather than the cheap ones: **"i dont play the game right now, its unplayable and
not fun."**

V4's test is not *is there more content*. It is: **can the player say out loud
what they are trying to do this term, who is trying to stop them, what their
country actually makes, and what it cost when they lost.**

## The evidence this tier is built on

Counted cold on 2026-09-07 against `main` at 75fe6fe, not estimated — and
the counts below are FROZEN at that date by design. The live counts, measured
2026-09-13 by the tier-planning challenge pass: **events 36 in 3 files**
(climate/crises/scandals), **council proposals 9 in 4 files**, **firms 26 in
4 nations** (DEU/FRA/GBR/POL), people 213, laws 42. The growth itself is
content shipped since the draft; the block targets below are re-anchored per
block in the local sdd handoffs:

- **Events are seven.** `data/events/` is 2 files; `src/core/event.gd` is nine
  fields; `src/sim/events.gd` holds **one pending slot**. No weight, no severity,
  no category, no per-event cooldown, no chain, no art, no per-nation targeting,
  and events fire for the player alone — [[events-model]] §11's "no AI chooser"
  limitation, recorded for the sixth time.
- **The council agenda is seven proposals** across 3 files, rotated on a
  deterministic index (`src/sim/orgs.gd:362`). The player votes yes or no. No
  sponsor, no lobbying, no bargaining, no veto, no ratification at home.
- **Industry is eighteen firms in three nations.** `companies-model` §8 is titled
  *"Three nations, not ten"*, §2 *"A firm is four fields, and the count is the
  design"*. A firm has never done anything on its own.
- **Laws are forty-two, and there is no repeal verb in the project.**
  `companies-model` §14 says so outright — *"There is no repeal verb in this
  project and a `once` law never returns"* — [V4.2 built the repeal verb — the
  per-law effect ledger and `Legislature._subtract_ledger`; companies-model §14's
  record is updated there.] which is why deregulation is a
  negative number on the same accumulator rather than an undo. Every law is
  **standing**: [[legislature-model]]'s own words, *"an accumulator a law writes
  once and a formula reads every month afterward."* Only the player proposes
  (`src/sim/legislature.gd:795` opens `var ns = state.player()`); the assembly
  never proposes at you and no AI nation legislates. Amendments are the single
  exception that can already reverse an earlier one, because the institutional
  channel sets absolute values rather than accumulating.
- **Tax is five keys inside the Budget panel.** `income`, `corporate`, `vat`,
  `capital_gains`, `wealth` plus a progressivity lever, mixed in with the six
  spending lines, and four authored tax laws. `src/core/nation_state.gd:115`
  carries the refusal this tier overturns in as many words: the law channel
  reaches *"`tax_rates_pct`'s SHAPE AND NOT A SIXTH TAX KEY."*
- **Trade exchanges nothing physical.** `src/sim/trade.gd` is a directed weight
  table — each vector sums to 1.0 — and `external_impulse()` is a multiplier on a
  partner's growth deviation. No commodity, no tonnage, no cargo.
  **`src/sim/energy.gd` is the only place in the whole simulation where a physical
  quantity exists** (`output_twh`), and it reaches the economy through exactly one
  growth addend.
- **GDP is the region sum.** `src/sim/economy.gd:289` — *"GDP IS THE REGION SUM,
  not the sector sum"* — with the three sectors reconciling to it exactly by a
  cyclical residual distributed over weights that sum to 1.0, and
  `_apply_growth_to_sectors()` the single monthly writer.
- **The top bar carries four numbers** (`src/ui/top_bar.gd`). No alert surface, no
  outliner, no nested tooltips, no message settings.
- **There are zero audio files in the repository.** Zero loading screens, zero
  progress bars. Two `create_tween` calls in the project, both in vendor
  `src/ui/vendor/MapCamera2D.gd`.
- **The satellite is upscaled 6.4× at max zoom.**
  `assets/map/blue_marble_5400x2700.jpg` is 15 px/degree; `MAX_ZOOM` 8.0 against
  `BASE_SCALE` 12.0 draws 96 px/degree. [[map-platform-model]] refused per-tile
  raster LOD at V3.1 with a measured reason: the raster is one quad. No shaders
  exist in `src/`.
- **The roster is V3.3's job, not this tier's.** 214 people across 29 nations
  today; the EU-27 plus GBR and CHE, all playable, is [[v3|V3.3]]'s own row.

### And the root, which was measured a tier early

**[[v3|V3.0]]'s gate FAILED and the author's route call is still open.** The best
active play style scored **−2.00 approval points against idle** at month six
(improved from −7.57), with the vote deficit closing 3.0 → 0.2. The probe's own
reading of why: *idle is locally optimal by construction* — every satisfaction in
this model is a delta against the start, so change is priced and inactivity is
free. Three routes were recorded for the author; V3.1 and V3.2 were each told not
to pick one up silently, and neither did.

That is "the game is not fun" written in the game's own numbers, one tier early.
**A hundred and fifty events, forty agenda entries and eighty commodities
authored on top of a loop in which playing loses are a hundred and fifty events,
forty agenda entries and eighty commodities of mush.** So the call opens the tier
rather than riding inside a content block — which is [[v3]]'s own *agency before
content* argument, restated one tier later because the argument was right and the
gate was not met.

### Why the expensive route on commodities, stated once

The standing argument against replacing the growth-rate model is that it
invalidates every constant measured since the MVP and re-baselines all fifty-one
balance regimes. **That argument has most of its force when the current game is
worth protecting, and the author's verdict at this gate is that it is not.** A
re-baseline of a game nobody plays costs the re-baseline and nothing else.

The rewrite is also smaller than it first looks, and the reason is
`economy.gd:289`. **GDP is already the region sum**, so the region spine
[[region-economy-model]] built survives untouched: what changes is the layer
*underneath* a region — its output stops being a trend rate and becomes what its
firms actually produce, priced. [[energy-model]]'s TWh stops being the sim's one
lonely physical quantity and becomes electricity, a commodity like any other.
And [[trade-model]]'s abstract weight table becomes bilateral flows of named
goods, which is the entire point of a geopolitics game: **who holds the lithium.**

**Track S + M. The gate the whole tier stands on.**

> [!note] Route taken 2026-09-12, recorded in [[v3]]'s close-out

> route-choice — it designs that channel and measures it. The other two routes

> accept-the-bound) if the gate is missed.

> [!warning] Measured 2026-09-14 — the LEVEL form MISSED its gate
> The channel shipped as designed (`Opinion.competence_credit`, three terms
> vs the inauguration figures, `COMPETENCE_SPAN` 1.0 as the C3 bound) and the
> sweep found the **form**, not the span, wrong: it prices the inherited
> business cycle, not the record's policy component, so C1 (+1.0 vs idle at
> month 6) is unreachable at any span (needs ≈ 45; C3 caps at ≈ 1.02). The
> negative control held everywhere. **The declared next route is the
> decaying-credit form ([[agency-model]] §2's refused form 2), then
> activity-keyed drag — its own scoped block, never a silent extension.**
> The two standing obligations discharged: `QUALITY_SCALE_PP` re-fit to 6.75
> (the V1.5a rule restored) and the Eurozone/Schengen zero-price measured to
> a bound. Full record: [[agency-model]].

*Today:* the V3.0 probe's verdict, unacted. Three routes on the table.

*What it does:* picks a route and measures it on `BALANCE_PLAN`'s pre-declared
form — bands written before the first run, because a balance goal without a
measurement is a promise rather than a plan.

The three routes, restated from the probe report:

1. **A competence-credit channel.** Activity earns a satisfaction term that
   inactivity does not — the missing half of a model in which every satisfaction
   is a delta and change is the only thing priced.
2. **An activity-keyed drag on idleness.** The mirror: doing nothing accrues a
   cost — drift, decay, a coalition that stops believing in you.
3. **Accept the bound**, and make the game about something other than the
   approval number — which V4.1's agenda would then have to carry alone.

Routes 1 and 2 are not equivalent and the difference is legible in play: (1)
rewards the player who acts and leaves the idle player where they were; (2)
punishes the idle player and leaves the active one where they were. **The choice
is the author's.** The block measures whichever is taken against a pre-declared
band and reports honestly if it is not met, exactly as V3.0 did.

Two standing obligations are discharged here rather than left to drift: the
**`QUALITY_SCALE_PP` re-fit-or-retire** (V1.5a's delivery-under-announcement rule
has been inverted at 0.75 since V3.0 and was accepted rather than re-swept), and
the **Eurozone/Schengen zero-price** band [[orgs-model]] named a trigger for.

*Refuses:* new content. This is a measurement and tuning block; nothing it ships
is authored.

*Gate:* at least one policy family moves overall approval **positively against
idle** by month six, at a margin declared before the first run. If the route taken
cannot reach it, the block says so and hands the next route back rather than
tuning the failure away.

## V4.1 — The programme

**Track S + U. The cheapest large gain in the tier.**

*Today:* nothing tells the player what they are trying to do. [[pledge-model]]'s
campaign promises are the only forward-looking commitment in the game and they are
one file — a promise made at an election, judged every month against the world.

*What it adds:* an **agenda register** on Victoria 3's journal-entry shape, named
for this game as *the government's programme*. An entry is a live goal with a
condition, a clock and a progress reading:

| field | note |
|---|---|
| `id`, `name`, `summary` | as an event's |
| `group` | economy / institutions / Europe / society / the office itself |
| `possible` | the trigger that makes it offerable — `PRECONDITION_FIELDS`' shape |
| `activation` | how it opens: authored, event-opened, player-chosen, or forced |
| `immediate` | effects on activation |
| `complete` / `fail` / `invalid` | the three conclusions, on [[referendum-model]]'s own three-outcome precedent |
| `timeout_months` | 0 = no deadline |
| `current_value`, `goal_value`, `progressbar` | the progress read, DERIVED from a live figure rather than stored |
| `on_complete` / `on_fail` / `on_timeout` | `Law.extract_shared_effects()` — the one applier events already use, not a second |
| `weight`, `pinned` | ordering, and whether it rides the outliner by default |

**The player chooses.** Not every entry is handed down: at a term's start the
player picks a programme out of what is offerable, which is the first time this
game asks *what are you for* rather than *what will you do this month*. Failing
your own programme is priced; succeeding is the competence credit V4.0 may have
just built a channel for — **the two blocks must agree on that channel or state
why they do not.**

**Pledges are absorbed or explicitly kept apart, and the decomposition session
decides which.** A campaign promise and a programme entry are the same shape
wearing two names — one made to voters at an election, one made to yourself at a
term's start. Either resolution is defensible; drifting into two half-machines is
the failure this project has caught six times.

*Content:* **30–40 entries** across five groups, each with 2–4 authored
conclusions.

*Surfaces:* an Agenda panel; pinned entries in a strip the top bar can read;
progress on Overview; a card when an entry opens, completes or fails.

*Refuses:* branching quest trees. An entry concludes, it does not unlock a
sequel — chains are V4.4's machine, and two machines pointed at one job is what
`legislature.gd`'s single-applier argument exists to prevent.

## V4.2 — The statute book

**Track S + data + U. The block where the player stops picking from a menu.**

*Today:* forty-two authored laws, **player-only proposal**, **no repeal verb
anywhere in the project**, and every law standing forever once passed. Tax is
five keys on sliders buried in the Budget panel beside the spending lines, with
four authored tax laws. The forecast of how the assembly would vote exists and is
**deliberately blurred** (`src/ui/law_panel.gd:11`); the polling numbers the
player reads are **deliberately noised** (`src/ui/poll_noise.gd`). Neither blur
is reachable by the player, and `src/sim/` knows about neither.

*What it adds:*

### Composition — you draft the bill

The player builds a law rather than choosing one: pick the effect channels, set
how far each goes, set its position in ideology space, name it. **The machine is
half-built already** — [[legislature-model]]'s vote resolution scores *any*
position against the parties' own, so it does not care whether a position was
authored or composed.

**The thing that must be designed, and it is the whole block's risk, is the
cost.** Nothing stops a player composing a bill that is all upside. The shape to
settle at the decomposition session: a **drafting budget** — a bill has a size,
each channel and each point of reach spends from it, and a bigger bill is both
harder to pass (it offends more parties at once) and slower to draft. That is the
`reach` field `src/core/law.gd` already carries, promoted from a derived number to
a thing the player spends.

### Repeal — and why it is a machine rather than a button

There is no repeal today because **effects are accumulators**: a law writes once
and formulas read forever, so nothing anywhere records what any individual law
contributed. Repeal therefore needs a **per-law effect ledger** — what this law
wrote, kept so it can be subtracted — and that ledger is the block's one genuinely
new piece of state.

Repeal is a law like any other: it is proposed, it is voted on, and it can fail.
Repealing your predecessor's signature act is a fight, not an undo. **The
symmetric-clamp rule stands** — `companies-model` §14's deregulation-as-negative
survives as the way a law moves a channel *further*, and repeal is the separate
verb that removes one law's own contribution.

### The tax panel

Tax comes out of the Budget panel into its own surface, with **the rate shown as
a number, not only as a slider position** — the specific ask, and the same
complaint [[playability-audit]]'s Register B already registered against the
budget levers, which show a pre-law position after a tax law passes because only
`_on_revert()` re-syncs them.

**And a new tax can be proposed.** `src/core/nation_state.gd:115` refuses exactly
this today — the law channel reaches *"`tax_rates_pct`'s SHAPE AND NOT A SIXTH
TAX KEY."* The composition machine overturns it: a proposed tax is a **new key**
with an authored base, a rate and an erosion, so a carbon tax, a financial
transaction tax or a windfall levy is something the player invents and fights for
rather than something an author remembered to write. [[tax-model]]'s existing
rule that the wealth tax is not on the other four's rate scale is the precedent
that a new key brings its own base with it.

### The bill briefing, and the two polls

**A popup opens on every proposal** and says, in words, what the bill does: which
channels it touches, in which direction, how much, what it costs, who it offends.
This is the same debt [[playability-audit]] logs against the whole shell — the
attribution machine already computes the explanation and nothing surfaces it.

**Two polls can be commissioned from that popup, and both are purchases:**

| Poll | What it buys | Reuses |
|---|---|---|
| **The assembly poll** | *Will it pass?* A sharper read of the vote than the standing blurred forecast | `law_panel.gd`'s blur, narrowed |
| **The popular poll** | *Are people happy with it?* A per-bloc read of how the country would take it | `poll_noise.gd`'s noise, narrowed |

**A poll is a paid reduction in blur, and that is why it costs almost no new
machine.** Both blurs already exist, both are UI-side, and `src/sim/` learns
nothing — `opinion.gd:11` and `law_panel.gd:16` both state that rule about
themselves and both keep it. The only new state is what was paid for and when.

**`legislature.gd:230` sets the rule the polls must respect** — *"a forecast that
mutates is a forecast that can only be asked once."* So a commissioned poll is
bought once and returns a **fixed** sharper number, never a reroll. Money buys
precision, not a second draw.

*Content:* **42 → ~120 authored laws** across more themes, which is the "both"
half of the author's call: composition is the machine, and a large authored set is
the starting menu, the AI's repertoire, and what the assembly proposes at you.

*Also here:* [[someday]]'s **private members' bills** — the assembly proposing
laws *at* the player — which is reachable the moment proposal stops being
hardcoded to `state.player()`.

*Refuses:* AI nations legislating their own domestic programmes. Named and
parked; the nine have no legislature loop and giving them one is a second game's
worth of tuning for something the player never sees.

## V4.3 — People who want things

**Track S + data + U. The largest content block in the tier, and the one that
overturns a written rule.**

*Today:* **214 people across 29 nations** — six to eight each, counted from
`data/people/`; 71 portraits, from before the roster grew. [[events-model]] §3
states the rule this block breaks: *"The subject is an OFFICE and never a
person — [[people-model]]'s rule verbatim — so nothing in `src/sim/` ever touches
a person id and no mechanism on a person stays true by construction."*
`src/ui/events_panel.gd` is the only file in the project that resolves an office
to a name, and `src/core/person.gd` is *"a person ID + name; minimal entity."*

**That rule was right for V1.8 and it is what makes the same scandal read
correctly in a republic and in a monarchy. V4 overturns it deliberately.** The
office resolution stays — a law still names an office, and a scandal still lands
on whoever holds it — but `src/sim/` gains a person register and people acquire
wants. **The session that builds this rewrites [[events-model]] §3 at the rule
itself, with its reason.** This tier doc does not pre-emptively edit it: a roadmap
that rewrites a model doc is a roadmap claiming work it has not done.

*What it adds:*

- **A person gains state.** Two or three authored traits from a fixed set,
  ambition, loyalty to the player, standing, and a grudge ledger. Traits are the
  Crusader Kings lever: they colour what a person will do and what it costs them
  to act against type.
- **A political register.** Who holds what office, who wants which office, who
  owes whom. [[cabinet-model]] already exists and is the natural spine.
- **The deep bench: 214 → ~1500–2000**, fifty to seventy per nation. Not only the
  political cast — **the author's call was explicitly "political or not"**: the
  front bench and the back benches, party rivals who can actually replace you,
  regional figures, union leaders ([[unrest-model]] is their meter), press owners
  ([[media-model]]), judges ([[judiciary-model]]), activists, and the chiefs of
  V4.7's firms.
- **People act.** Resign, defect, brief against you, leak, demand a portfolio,
  threaten a leadership challenge, endorse or refuse to endorse at an election.
  The collective version takes Victoria 3's escalation shape — a movement runs
  Passive → Agitating → Protesting → Rioting — reading [[unrest-model]]'s
  grievance stock rather than a second meter.
- **Portraits at that scale are a pipeline question, not a design one.** V2.3's
  ComfyUI recipe is committed and the run is a batch; what the block must decide
  is the **tiering** — full portraits for the cast the player meets, a cheaper
  treatment for the bench, on the same reasoning V4.7 tiers its firms.

*Surfaces:* the People panel rewritten around wants rather than a list; a portrait
on every event card and agenda entry with a subject; hover cards on any named
person; a relationship read on the cabinet.

*Refuses:* dynasties, marriage, inheritance, personal lifespan. This is a modern
republic and people leave politics rather than dying of it. Also refuses **the
leader's personal wealth**, which stays in [[someday]] on its own reason: a money
pool with no exposure risk is a second currency with no lose condition attached.

## V4.4 — Events at scale

**Track S + data + U.**

*Today:* seven events, two files, one pending slot.

*What it adds, machine before content:*

- **Weight and category.** The flat `likelihood` becomes a weighted draw inside a
  category, so a scandal cannot crowd out a fiscal crisis on hazard alone.
- **Severity.** The field V1.8 explicitly did not carry, and the one that drives
  V4.8's auto-pause and message settings.
- **Hooks.** Today an event fires only on a monthly hazard against a precondition.
  Add Paradox's `on_action` shape: an event fires *because* a law passed, an
  election was lost, a firm was nationalised, a minister resigned, a commodity
  price spiked, an accession referendum was won. **This single change is most of
  what makes a Paradox game feel as though it is watching you.**
- **Chains.** A choice can arm a follow-up at a delay — the machine V4.1
  deliberately refuses, built once, here.
- **A queue, not a slot.** [[events-model]] §5 argues that one slot *is* what "the
  matter on your desk" means, and at seven events that argument holds. At a
  hundred and fifty it does not. A short queue ordered by severity, plus the
  per-event cooldown designed and cut at V1.8 — cut on the grounds that one slot
  already bounded it, an argument that dies with the slot.
- **The AI gets events.** A minimal chooser scoring choices against the nation's
  own position, so the other twenty-eight nations have politics that happen to
  them. Sixth recorded instance of "no AI chooser", and the one that closes it.
- **Art.** Themed illustrations on CK3's `theme` shape — **40–60 images reused by
  category**, not one per event, on V2.3's generation recipe.

*Content:* **7 → 120–150 authored events**, 350+ choices, across nine categories:
scandal, fiscal crisis, social unrest, personal (needs V4.3), European, foreign,
industrial, commodity and supply (needs V4.6), and disaster ([[v3|V3.5]]'s sited
disasters are the event schema with a place).

*The balance constraint that survives the growth:* [[events-model]]'s E1b finding
still binds — holding a choice's size-weighted mean `bloc_offset` near zero pushes
the cost onto the **smallest** bloc, so `business` (size 0.10) becomes the natural
loser far more often than its share unless the content is spread deliberately. At
seven events that was a note. At a hundred and fifty it is an authoring constraint
with a probe attached.

*Refuses:* `overall_approval_pct` as a precondition, still. An event gated on low
approval and priced in approval is positive feedback with nothing damping it, and
[[events-model]] and [[pledge-model]] already refuse that field for two different
reasons — which is worth more than either.

## V4.6 — The commodity economy

**Track S + data + U. The largest block on this roadmap: 15–22 sessions of the
tier's 70–95, and the one that replaces a model rather than extending it.**

*BUILT-AT NOTE (2026-09-20, branch `v4.6-commodity-economy`):* both step-0
probes ran before a recipe was authored and are committed at dd20c51 — the
tick budget PASSED (29.3/5.2 ms against the 300 ms band), and the GDP probe
FAILED ON LEVELS (ρ = 0.902, worst 0.822 against a 0.20 band), which per
this section's own rule named the abstraction level: the layer models GDP
*changes*, the level stays the register's measured value, and a per-nation
productivity lever was refused as circular. The build: 60 goods / 34 recipes,
`commodities.gd` as a leaf, the seven-key register on SAVE_VERSION 37, the
tick wired after Economy before Opinion, panel + two map layers. As-built
record: [docs/commodities-model.md](docs/commodities-model.md). The
re-run held its bit-identical prediction (both probe regimes, cold-worktree
noise isolated per V1.7c). The block closed in ONE session of the estimated
15–22 because the narrowed scope is a layer beside the growth model, not
the replacement this section originally wrote — the replacement decision
(GDP-delta wiring, V4.7's labour market) is recorded open in the model doc.

**Track S + data + U.** *(The original estimate below is preserved for the
record; see the built-at note.)*

*Today:* three sectors on a growth rate; `economy.gd:289` — *"GDP IS THE REGION
SUM"*; `_apply_growth_to_sectors()` the single monthly writer, reconciling
sector output to that sum exactly through a cyclical residual over weights
summing to 1.0. Trade is an abstract directed weight table and a growth
multiplier — **nothing physical is exchanged anywhere in the simulation**, and
`energy.gd`'s `output_twh` is the sim's one lonely physical quantity, reaching the
economy through a single growth addend.

*The call, and the reason:* **goods become the economy.** Not a layer that nudges
the sectors — the production model replaces the growth-rate model. The normal
argument against that is the re-baseline of every constant measured since the MVP,
and **the author's verdict at the gate is that the current game is not worth
protecting**, which removes most of that argument's force.

**The region spine survives, and that is what makes this affordable.** GDP is
already the region sum, so [[region-economy-model]]'s architecture is untouched:
what is replaced is the layer *beneath* a region. A region's output stops being a
trend rate and becomes what is produced there, priced.

*What it adds:*

- **A goods register.** **~60–80 commodities in categories**, on Victoria 3's
  category shape (it runs ~50 across staple, luxury, industrial and military) with
  the author's own granularity rule applied: **granularity follows strategic
  consequence.** Metals are individualised — iron, copper, aluminium, nickel,
  lithium, rare earths, uranium each their own good, because who holds the supply
  *is* the geopolitics. Finished consumer goods are lumped — one *appliances*, one
  *clothing*, one *electronics* — because the player never cares about microwaves
  against fridges. Categories on the *matières premières* axis the author named:
  raw extraction, agricultural, energy carriers, refined and intermediate,
  chemicals, capital goods, consumer goods, and services.
- **The industrial tree.** Each good declares its inputs and its output ratio —
  steel from iron and coal, exactly the author's example; batteries from lithium,
  nickel and chemicals; appliances from steel, plastics and electronics. This is
  Victoria 3's production-method shape reduced to one authored recipe per good,
  because production *methods* (a choice of recipe per building) are a second
  system and this block is already the biggest one here.
- **Prices from supply and demand**, bounded — Victoria 3 clamps at ±75 % of a
  base price and that bound is the precedent, because an unbounded price in a
  deterministic sim is how a spiral becomes the whole game.
- **Trade becomes physical.** [[trade-model]]'s weight table stops being the
  exchange and becomes the *routing*: what actually crosses a border is a named
  good in a quantity, priced, and a nation's balance per commodity — produce,
  consume, import, export — is the number the whole tier's foreign policy hangs
  on. [[someday]]'s **supply-chain disruption** and **export controls** are
  reachable the moment a flow has a name.
- **Energy is absorbed, not paralleled.** Electricity becomes a commodity;
  `energy-sources.json`'s plants become producers of it consuming coal, gas or
  uranium. [[energy-model]]'s import-dependency channel stops being a special case
  and becomes what every commodity's balance already says. **This is a
  consolidation: one physical-quantity system where there were two.**
- **The `sector_output` reconciliation is rewritten, not deleted.** Three sectors
  remain as the *reporting* partition — agriculture, industry, services are how a
  finance ministry reports, and every panel and probe reads them — but each
  becomes the sum of its goods rather than a trend times a weight. The
  single-writer rule survives with a new writer.

*Surfaces:* a Commodities panel with the balance per good; the tree, drawn, as its
own screen; a shortage alert; map modes for production and for import dependency.

*What must be measured before anything is built,* on E0's own precedent (the
events probe that ran before a single threshold was authored): **does a
production economy reproduce the observed GDP of twenty-nine real nations to
within a declared band from authored recipes alone?** If it does not, the recipes
are wrong or the abstraction level is, and that is a step-0 finding rather than a
tuning problem discovered in session fifteen.

**And a second step-0 probe, on the comparison's own warning: what does a month
cost.** Victoria 3 ships this same shape — goods, recipes, prices, bilateral
flows — and its own players report it *"still incredibly laggy in the late game
(1900+)"*, *"late game is unbearable"*, with a **Playable Late Game** mod in the
workshop to answer it. That is a studio with an optimisation team arriving at an
unplayable tick, and the cause is scale, not craft: every commodity balances
against every producer every turn. **This project has no tick budget and has never
measured one** — `tools/frame_probe.gd` is the only timing instrument in the
repository and it times *frames*, so [[map-platform-model]]'s whole measured
culture (G1–G5, a 16.67 ms budget, a cap shipped when a gate failed) covers the
renderer and nothing covers the simulation. So: **a synthetic tick at full V4.6 +
V4.7 scale — ~70 goods × 29 nations, ~2,900 firms, bilateral flows — timed before
a recipe is authored, against a declared per-month budget.** A grand-strategy
month that costs a second is a game that cannot be played at speed 5, and finding
that out in session eighteen costs the block. V4.6 and V4.7 together are what
make the scale, so the probe is sized for both and belongs to whichever runs
first.

*Refuses:* production methods as a per-building choice, pop-level consumption
baskets, a labour market that clears per good, and a stock market. Each is named
here so a later session recognises it as declined scope rather than a fresh idea.
Victoria 3 has all four and Victoria 3 is not what this game is.

## V4.7 — Industry

**Track S + data + U.**

*Today:* eighteen firms in three nations, five fields, six verbs, and share-
shifting under subsidy and regulation is a firm's entire behaviour.

*What it adds:*

- **The roster: 18 → ~2,900**, the author's *at least a hundred per nation* across
  the EU-27 plus GBR and CHE.
- **And the count breaks the current mechanic, which is the finding this block
  turns on.** A firm today is an exact `share` of its sector. At a hundred firms
  per nation the average share is one per cent, so subsidising one moves
  essentially nothing and every existing company verb becomes theatre. **The
  answer is two tiers, and the project already uses the pattern**: [[geography-model]]
  treats *rural-and-unnamed* as the residual of an exact partition.
  - **Majors** — roughly ten to fifteen per nation, each a share large enough to
    be worth a verb: subsidised, nationalised, broken up, rescued, lobbied by.
    These are hand-authored, they have a named chief from V4.3, and they are what
    `companies-model` §2's "the count is the design" was written about.
  - **The body** — the remaining eighty-five-plus per nation, **generated** rather
    than hand-written, on the fictionalisation rules and the existing naming
    check. They employ people, sit in real cities, produce real commodities after
    V4.6, appear when a region is inspected, fail, get bought — and they aggregate
    into the residual rather than each carrying a verb.
- **Firms act** — [[someday]]'s standing rows, finally reachable: invest or
  disinvest, relocate between regions (V2.2's regions and [[v3|V3.4]]'s network
  make a relocation *mean* something), hire and fire, fail, be bought by a foreign
  rival.
- **Firms lobby.** [[someday]]'s *corporate lobbying shifting policy*: a firm
  spends against a law it dislikes or for one it wants, through the assembly
  arithmetic V4.2 has just opened to composition.
- **New player verbs:** block a foreign takeover ([[someday]]'s strategic-asset
  protection), bail out or let fail ([[someday]]'s bankruptcies and bailouts),
  break up, court an investor, appoint to a state-owned board ([[someday]]'s state
  enterprises with patronage — which needs V4.3's people to mean anything).

*Refuses:* per-firm balance sheets, and a firm-level labour market. A firm is a
producer with a share and a payroll, not an accounting entity.

*(As built at V4.7, branches `v4.7a-industry-register` (PR #46) and
`v4.7b-industry-autonomy`: the register is **317 majors + 2,420 body = 2,737
firms across all 29 nations**, two tiers as planned, the body generated and
pinned by `tools/firms_generate.py --check`; the sparse register repairs
pre-roster saves at the first tick (`_seed_missing_majors`, the PR #46
review's headline). The step-0 probe's escalator finding scoped the design:
the share channel is absolute, so the pool is MAJORS-ONLY and `BODY_SHARE_MAX`
0.03 — **a deliberate deviation from the row above**: body firms do not yet
fail or get bought, because body in the drainable pool would make a major's
reachable share depend on the generated body count; their acts are a later
block's market. Firms act: `src/sim/industry.gd` — failures with a rescue
window, foreign bids, investment drift, relocation priced on V3.4's network;
bands declared then probe-corrected (0.62 failures/nation per 168-month run
idle); no RNG anywhere (Cycle, private key namespace). The player verbs:
block/allow a takeover, rescue-as-bailout (gated on the window), break up,
court an investor, appoint a SOE board seat. Model: [[companies-model]]
§31–38. **Two deltas from the row:** firms do NOT lobby here — the signed
order moved V4.2 after V4.7, so lobbying ships there, and the executive chiefs
(one per major, from V4.3's bench) are the channel's standing faces until it
does. And the tick cost was found by the re-baseline's wall clock, not by a
gate — the scale probe predates the machine and times Companies only;
1,124 ms/month over the roster, memoised to 16.45.)*

### The reception pass, added 2026-09-08

The 09-07 read above is a read of **mechanics** — what these games ship and how
it is shaped. A second pass a day later read what players say about living with
them, and it moved exactly one thing in this document, which is why it is kept
separate rather than folded in above.

- **Late-game performance, and it is the reason V4.6 now carries a tick probe.**
  [The game is still incredibly laggy in the late game (1900+)](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com)
  and [Late game is unbearable](https://steamcommunity.com), with a
  [Playable Late Game](https://steamcommunity.com) workshop mod existing to answer
  it. Victoria 3 runs the model V4.6 is copying the shape of, and its tick does
  not hold at scale.
- **Geopolitical Simulator's reception is a UI verdict, not a simulation one** —
  *"confusing mess with bad explanations, frustrating UI"* (GPS 2026 Edition,
  Steam app 4021780, Mixed across 244 reviews), and Metacritic on Power &
  Revolution: its realism and uniqueness *"CAN'T be discriminated because of bugs
  and a terrible UI"*. **This is the strongest evidence in the comparison for
  V4.8 and against a granularity race.** The game the author named as the scale
  reference is not judged badly for simulating too little; it is judged badly for
  being unreadable, which is what 150+ sectors bought. V4.6's asymmetric rule and
  V4.8's whole existence are the same argument, and this is the outside
  confirmation of it.
- **[Victoria 3 Building](https://vic3.paradoxwikis.com/Building) and
  ["a game of pops"](https://medium.com)** — the building-and-pop spine V4.6 and
  V4.7 deliberately fork from. Named here so the fork is visible as a choice.
- **[Is the economy in Victoria 3 realistic](https://forum.paradoxplaza.com)** —
  the ±75 % clamp described by its own forum as price controls, *"this is mainly a
  game not an economy simulator"*. V4.6 already took the bound; this is the
  designers' own framing of why a bound is not a compromise.

**What the pass did not find:** nothing in either game's reception speaks to
V4.0. Both are judged on content, economy and interface; neither is judged on
